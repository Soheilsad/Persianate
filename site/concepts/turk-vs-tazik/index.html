    Türk vs Tāzīk¶

    The men of the sword and the men of the pen.

    Every Persianate Sultanate carries one extra societal value: Turk vs Tazik, shown in game as a slider between Turkic Military Tradition on the left and Tazik Administration on the right. It is not a culture slider — your pops stay whatever they were. It measures which of the two service elites your state defaults to: the Amirs or the Dīwān.

    What it represents¶

    Persian chancery writers of the Ilkhanid and post-Ilkhanid centuries divided the ruling class into Turk and Tāzīk: the Turco-Mongol military households who held commands, pastures and iqṭāʿ, and the Persian-writing secretaries, accountants and judges who kept the registers. The division was one of function and household more than of language — Iraqi Arab secretaries of Baghdad and Ḥilla belong to the Tāzīk side as surely as the Juvaynīs of Khurāsān, and a Persianised Mongol amir remains a man of the sword. Both sides were Muslim, both wrote and patronised Persian, and no court ran without both. What a ruler decided, month by month, was whose advice counted and whose grievances were heard first.

    The two poles¶

    The slider runs from −100 (fully Türk) to +100 (fully Tāzīk). As with every societal value, the listed effects are the values at the extreme and scale down toward the centre; at 0 neither side applies.

    Turkic Military Tradition (−100) Tazik Administration (+100)
    Amirs estate power +50% −50%
    Dīwān estate power −50% +50%
    Tribes estate power +25% —
    Dīwān satisfaction equilibrium — +8%
    Amirs levy size +25% −40%
    Tribes levy size +25% −40%
    Manpower +15% −15%
    Heavy and light cavalry power +15% each —
    Tax efficiency −10% +10%
    Cabinet efficiency — +10%
    Maximum urban control −5% +12.5%
    Maximum rural control −15% +5%
    Raw material output +10% —
    Cultural influence — +15%
    Cultural tradition — −15%
    Court spending efficiency — −10%
    Tribesmen desired population +15% rural, +5% towns −50% rural, −75% towns
    Nobles desired population +3% rural, +1% towns +1% rural, +3% towns
    Subject loyalty +15 +15
    Monthly drift +0.10 toward decentralization +0.10 toward centralization

    Both ends raise subject loyalty: either elite, once clearly in charge, is a party your subjects can deal with. The muddled middle gets nothing.

    The Amirs also read the slider directly. Their opinion of the crown gains up to +20 at the Türk extreme and loses up to 20 at the Tāzīk extreme (one point for every five on the slider). The Dīwān have no mirror-image opinion term; their reward for a Tāzīk realm is the satisfaction and power in the table.

    Where you start¶

    Starting setup Slider Who
    Türk-dominant sultanates −25 Jalayirids (JAL), Chobanids (CHB), Eretna (ERE) and most of the Turco-Mongol amirates
    Tāzīk-dominant sultanates +25 Muzaffarids (MZF), Injuids (INJ), Karts (KRT), Shirvan (SRV), the Caspian and Persian Gulf dynasties, Rasulid Yemen (YEM)

    The Türk-dominant setup also starts with the Tribal Host recruitment law, Amir Autonomy and Iqta Assignments; the Tāzīk-dominant setup starts with Amir Recruitment, Curtail the Umara and Diwan Administration. Those laws are a large part of why each side keeps drifting the way it began (see below).

    How it moves¶

    Steady drift¶

    Reforms, laws, privileges and a few long modifiers each add a monthly push. A push of +0.05 is 0.6 points a year; they all stack.

    Source Typical push per month
    Amirs privileges 0.05–0.20 toward Türk each. The heaviest are Hereditary Tuyul (0.20), Tribal Hosts (Yurt) and Universal Tarkhan (0.15), Umarāʾ at the Dīwān (0.10).
    Dīwān privileges 0.05 toward Tāzīk each; 0.10 for Tāzīk Command of Arms, Mavājib Cash Pay, Ṣāḥib-Dīwān Plenipotentiary and Chancery Commissions for Amir Sons.
    Laws Amir Recruitment, Tribal Host, Amir Autonomy: 0.10 Türk. Rights of the Umara: 0.05 Türk. Diwan Administration, Curtail the Umara: 0.05 Tāzīk.
    Government reforms From 0.025 to 0.15 either way — the full list is on the government reforms page. Amīr Sultanate (0.15 Türk) and Chinggisid Regency (0.10 Türk) are the strongest early pushes; Iranian Kingship, Ghulām Corps and Khāṣṣa Crown Demesne (0.10 Tāzīk each) the strongest later ones.
    Cabinet The base game's "change societal values" cabinet action works on this slider in both directions, at 1 point a month multiplied by cabinet efficiency. It is by far the fastest deliberate lever.

    Note that the plain Iqtāʿ Grant privilege carries no drift of its own. It is the iqṭāʿ buildings it permits that move the slider (next section).

    What reads the slider¶

    Threshold What it gates
    Below 0 Rally Turkic Tribes is available. Petty Persianate realms in Erānzamīn use the Türk rank titles (Tūmān, Amirate) instead of the Tāzīk ones (Ḥukūmat, Vilāyat). At the Erānzamīn victory, a slider below 0 gives the Türk settlement (Mandate of the Great Khan, the Invite Turkic Settlers cabinet action and the road to Iran o Toran).
    0 or above At the Erānzamīn victory, gives the Tāzīk settlement (Restoration of Eran and the Integrate into the Dīwān cabinet action).
    Below −15 Grant the Yurt is available.
    Above +25 Consolidate the Diwan is available (it also needs the Dīwān of the Realms in your capital). The in-game tooltip currently says 15; the real threshold is 25.
    +25 or above A Qanāt Network can be built without the Land Registers privilege. Also one of the conditions for closing the Matter of Tork by dissolution.
    +50 or above A kingdom can seat the Dīwān of the Realms on the slider alone (an empire, or a realm that has used Restore Persian Bureaucracy, can seat it regardless).

    The three province-integration actions and the two rallying actions belong to the Erānzamīn situation and are only offered to its contenders. In brief:

    Action Needs Cost Result
    Consolidate the Diwan Slider above +25; Dīwān of the Realms in the capital; a Local Dīwān in the conquered province Gold (3 months of income) Integrates the province at once; +10% max control, +10% raw material output and lower unrest there for 10 years; Dīwān pleased, Amirs slightly displeased; +1.5
    Grant the Yurt Slider below −15 5 legitimacy, 5 political influence Integrates the province at once; +30% local manpower, −7.5% max control and lower unrest for 10 years; Amirs pleased, Dīwān slightly displeased; −1.5
    Mixed Settlement — Gold, 5 legitimacy, 5 political influence Integrates the province at once; +10% local manpower and lower unrest for 10 years; no estate reaction, no slider movement
    Rally Turkic Tribes Slider below 0; prestige above 40 20 prestige and gold Türkmen tribesmen and their nobles arrive in one rural location (more for higher ranks); Amirs +10%, Dīwān, burghers and peasants −25% each; −10 stability; −5
    Restore Persian Bureaucracy Prestige above 30; at least duchy rank; not already a Tāzīk (+50) kingdom Gold, 20 legitimacy, 15 prestige, 10 stability Diwan Extension for 25 years (+7.5% tax efficiency, +5% max control, Dīwān +5% satisfaction and +10% power; stacks); Dīwān +15%, Amirs −15%; +10; permanently allows the Dīwān of the Realms

    Playing the axis¶

    • Pick a side early, or pay for the middle. Every bonus in the pole table is zero at the centre. A realm sitting at ±25 gets a quarter of one column. The slider rewards commitment, and so does subject loyalty.
    • Your starting laws are the drift. A Türk-dominant start carries about 0.45 a month toward Türk from its starting laws and privileges alone (more under a Chinggisid Regency). If you mean to turn such a realm Tāzīk, change the recruitment law and the nobility law first; otherwise you are rowing against them.
    • Going Tāzīk costs soldiers. At the Tāzīk extreme the Amirs and the tribes each raise 40% fewer levies and manpower falls 15%. Plan to replace them with paid troops — see the military system.
    • Going Türk costs the countryside. −15% maximum rural control and −10% tax efficiency at the extreme, with the Dīwān at half power. The Türk realm is rich in horsemen and poor in registers.
    • The cabinet seat is the real lever. One point a month from the cabinet outweighs any three privileges. Use it to cross a threshold (−15 for Grant the Yurt, +25 for Consolidate the Diwan, the sign of the slider before the Erānzamīn victory), then free the seat.
    • Watch the Amirs' opinion. A Tāzīk realm takes up to −20 Amir opinion from the slider itself, on top of whatever the privileges you revoked cost you.
