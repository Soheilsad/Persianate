    The Persianate Sultanate¶

    The sultan rules through a dual settlement: the pen keeps the registers, the sword keeps the field.

    The Persianate Sultanate is the government type of almost every state the mod touches: the successor amirates of the Ilkhanate, the Persian dynasties of Fārs, Herat and the Caspian shore, and — in their own regional dress — the Mamluks, Delhi and Rasulid Yemen. It is not a monarchy with different art. It has its own estates, its own cabinet rules, its own levy system, its own societal value and its own reform tree.

    What it represents¶

    By 1337 the lands from Anatolia to Bengal shared a recognisable way of running a state. A Turco-Mongol or Turkic military class held commands and revenue assignments (iqṭāʿ); a Persian-writing secretarial class — Iranian, and in Iraq equally Arab — staffed the dīwān, kept the tax registers and wrote the diplomas; jurists and Sufi shaykhs supplied the courts and the pulpits; and the ruler's right to rule was argued, not inherited. The theory of this state was written by Muslim statesmen and scholars — Niẓām al-Mulk, Ghazālī, Naṣīr al-Dīn Ṭūsī — for whom the just king, the farr that marks him and the circle of justice were ordinary Islamic political thought. The mod calls the working machinery of that state the chancery, and nearly everything on this page hangs from it.

    Who has it¶

    At the 1337 start:

    Group Countries Opening reform
    Türk-dominant successor amirates Jalayirids (JAL), Chobanids (CHB), Eretna (ERE) and the other Turco-Mongol amirates — about twenty in all Persianate Bureaucracy; JAL and CHB also start as a Chinggisid Regency
    Tāzīk-dominant dynasties Muzaffarids (MZF), Injuids (INJ), Karts (KRT), Shirvan (SRV), Hormuz (ORM), the Caspian houses and others Persianate Bureaucracy
    The periphery Mamluks (MAM), Delhi (DLH) and the Muslim Indian minors, Rasulid Yemen (YEM) Mamluk Government, The Delhi Dispensation, Rasulid Chancery
    Sufi orders The landless tariqa orders Tariqa Order (a special case — no chancery)

    The full roster is on the countries and tags page. Not everyone on the plateau is a sultanate: the Sarbadārs are a Commonwealth of Martyrs, the Kurdish principalities are Kurdish emirates, and the Chaghatayids and their neighbours are hordes.

    Later in the game a country becomes a Persianate Sultanate when:

    • a new country appears with a tribal government but a sedentary Iranian-world culture (Persian, Khurasani, Azari, Tajik, the Caspian peoples, Iraqi Arab, Armenian, Georgian and their neighbours) — it is converted on the spot and given Persianate Bureaucracy;
    • a Kurdish emirate takes one of the great seats — Tabriz, Isfahan, Shiraz, Nishapur, Baghdad or Herat — and accepts the offer to take over its chancery;
    • a Sufi order rises in arms, a Commonwealth of Martyrs gives way to a sultan, or a Zaydi imam makes his house hereditary (see those pages);
    • an Anatolian march is founded during the Rise of the Ottomans, or a breakaway sultanate splits from Delhi;
    • The Ṣāḥib-qirān's realm settles down as the Timurid Empire.

    Any new country whose capital lies in the Erānzamīn region also joins the Erānzamīn organisation automatically.

    The chancery¶

    Five reforms carry the chancery — Persianate Bureaucracy, Mamluk Government, The Delhi Dispensation, Rasulid Chancery and Timurid Empire — and a realm holds exactly one. The details differ (see government reforms); the Iranian form is the reference:

    Area Persianate Bureaucracy
    Administration +10% cabinet efficiency, +10% tax efficiency, +10% maximum estate tax, +0.10 monthly toward centralization
    Control +15% maximum urban control, +5% maximum rural control
    Dīwān +25% power, +5% satisfaction; seated in the cabinet; almost never rebel
    Other estates Amirs, nobles, burghers and clergy kept out of the cabinet; nobles −90% power
    Command Only the Amirs lead armies
    Levies Peasants −90%, Dīwān −90%, Amirs +30%, tribes +10%
    Other +2 cultural capacity; tributary subjects allowed

    Persianate states are built around their cities: urban control is high and resilient, rural control is thin. And the settled population does not fight. Your soldiers come from the Amirs' households, the tribes and whatever you pay for — see the military system.

    Two service estates¶

    The base game's nobility is replaced by the Dīwān and the Amirs. Under 1.4 an estate's weight comes from its pops, and the mod adds two scaling terms on top:

    Modifier Effect
    Base values Dīwān +70% power, Amirs +30% power; both start from a 40% satisfaction equilibrium
    Sedentary Power Base Dīwān power rises with the share of peasants, burghers and labourers in your population (+250% at a fully sedentary realm)
    Tribal Power Base Amirs power rises steeply with the share of tribesmen (+250% for every tenth of the population that is tribal)
    Persian court language Dīwān +5% satisfaction
    At war Amirs +2.5% satisfaction

    A realm with many tribesmen has strong Amirs whatever its laws say. The Türk vs Tāzīk slider then tilts the balance by up to ±50% either way.

    The two prohibitions¶

    Two lines are drawn by the chancery itself, and both are still in force:

    Prohibition How to lift it What lifting it costs
    The Dīwān may not lead armies. The Dīwān privilege Tāzīk Command of Arms Dīwān +10% satisfaction, +75% power; +10% cabinet efficiency; +0.05 centralization, +0.10 Tāzīk a month. Amirs −10% satisfaction, −15% power.
    The Amirs may not sit in the cabinet. The Amirs privilege Umarāʾ at the Dīwān, or the Amīr Sultanate reform Privilege: Amirs +10% satisfaction, +75% power; −10% cabinet efficiency; −10% political influence gain; +0.05 decentralization, +0.10 Türk a month. Dīwān −10% satisfaction, −15% power.

    The objection on each side is professional, and each side has its texts: the Siyāsatnāma complains at length about military men ruining the registers, and the amirs' retort that a tāzīk has no business at the head of a host was proverbial. While the Matter of Tork is running and unanswered, neither privilege can be granted unless the Amirs, the Dīwān and the tribes are all at 50% satisfaction or better.

    The vizierate¶

    The Dīwān expect one of their own to head the cabinet. Exactly one of the first three applies; the fourth stacks.

    Situation Modifier Effect
    No Dīwān member in the cabinet The Chancery Locked Out Dīwān −20% satisfaction, −25% cabinet efficiency, −15% tax efficiency
    Dīwān present, an outsider heads the cabinet No Vizier of the Pen Dīwān −5% satisfaction, −10% cabinet efficiency, −5% tax efficiency
    A Dīwān member heads the cabinet A Vizier of the Pen Dīwān +5% satisfaction, +5% cabinet efficiency, +5% tax efficiency, +15% crown power
    Two or more non-Dīwān members in the cabinet Crowded Vizierate Dīwān −5% satisfaction, −5% cabinet efficiency

    In practice: fill the cabinet with Dīwān characters, make one of them head, and keep at most one seat for a relative or favourite. The base-game habit of packing the cabinet with royal kin is punished here.

    Legitimacy is an answer, not an inheritance¶

    The Sultanate runs on legitimacy, and a Persianate Sultanate that has not said in whose name it rules suffers Unsettled Legitimacy: −0.6 legitimacy a month, Amirs and Dīwān −20% satisfaction, clergy and burghers −15%. The answers — Chinggisid Regency, Ilkhanate Governorate, Farr-i Īzadī, Ghāzī Sultanate and what they grow into — are the first tier of the reform tree, and the choice is put to you in the opening event of the Erānzamīn situation. The periphery chanceries, the Timurid Empire, the Rûm reforms and a ruler of Chinggis Khan's own house are exempt.

    Succession, court and justice¶

    • Succession defaults to Favored Son Succession: the son the late ruler preferred and the amirs will accept. The other base-game Muslim and steppe succession laws remain selectable.
    • The parliament is the Majlis-e Mazalim — the ruler sitting in judgment on his own officials rather than bargaining with estates. See the Mazālim court.
    • Courtiers belong to the Dīwān by default, so the pool you draw ministers from is the pool the vizierate wants.

    Finishing a conquest¶

    Conquered land in Erānzamīn is not simply left to integrate. Contenders in the Erānzamīn situation choose how each province is absorbed — Consolidate the Diwan, Grant the Yurt or Mixed Settlement — and each choice pleases one estate and moves the Türk–Tāzīk slider. The requirements and effects are tabulated on the Türk vs Tāzīk page.

    The winner of the situation receives one permanent cabinet action, decided by the sign of the slider at the moment of victory:

    Cabinet action Awarded to Needs Effect
    Integrate into the Dīwān A Tāzīk victor (slider 0 or above) A province in Iran, Khurāsān, the Caucasus, Iraq or the Jazīra with a Local Dīwān and land still only conquered Integration in that province runs at +600% until it is done
    Invite Turkic Settlers A Türk victor (slider below 0) A province in the same lands with land still only conquered A wave of tribesmen migrates in over ten years from the most populous Turkic province outside your borders; integration runs at +300%

    Both hold a cabinet seat while they run and end by themselves when the province is integrated.

    Formable realms¶

    Realm Who Requirements
    Persia A country of the Iranian culture group Own Tabriz, Isfahan and Shiraz and three quarters of the Persia region. Not a Chinggisid Regency. The question of right settled: the Farr vindicated or endured, the Ghāzī wager won or held, or the murid bond sworn. The place of the sword settled: the Matter of Tork resolved without the hosts founding a rival — or never raised, in a realm with fewer than 750,000 tribesmen. Becomes an empire.
    Iran o Toran A Chinggisid Regency, Gurkani Regime, Amīr Sultanate, Timurid Synthesis or Timurid Empire Win the Erānzamīn situation as a Türk realm. Hold three quarters of the Persia and Caucasus regions together with Cappadocia, Arab Iraq, the Jazīra and western Khurāsān. Takes up the Ilkhanate's own tag (ILK) and becomes an empire.
    Rûm A Muslim country of the Turkic culture group Hold 85% of Anatolia, come out of the Rise of the Ottomans as the winner without being the Ottomans, and hold a completed claim: the vindicated Seljuk inheritance or the Caliph's investiture. See Shattered Rûm.

    Practical advice¶

    • Answer the legitimacy question in the first month. Unsettled Legitimacy is the single worst modifier a new sultanate can carry, and every answer except a self-made Amīr Sultanate removes it at once.
    • Staff the cabinet from the Dīwān. The swing between The Chancery Locked Out and A Vizier of the Pen is 30% cabinet efficiency and 20% tax efficiency.
    • Do not expect peasant levies. Budget for the Amirs' and tribes' goodwill, or for paid soldiers.
    • Lift a prohibition only when you mean to change sides. Each of the two privileges hands one estate +75% power and pushes the slider 0.10 a month — they are commitments, not conveniences.
