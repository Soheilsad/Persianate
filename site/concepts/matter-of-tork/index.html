    The Matter of Tork — The Place of the Sword¶

    What is the Türk's place now?

    New in 1.4 — not yet verified in play

    The Matter was rebuilt after the June wiki: paths now stack, the disaster closes only when you press The Answer Is Given, and the frontier march is earned through the Great Raid. The Great Raid migration, Release the March and the closing proclamation have not been verified end to end in a full campaign.

    The post-Ilkhanid armies were Turco-Mongol tribal hosts, and their idea of rank ran through nearness to the house of Chinggis. A ruler who consolidates Iran without that blood — whether he rests his crown on the farr, on the ghazā, or on a shaykh's blessing — has settled his own legitimacy and, in doing so, has made the hosts' whole ordering of the world irrelevant. They do not vanish. They stand in the realm, armed, holding the pastures and most of the cavalry. Every dynasty from the Seljuks to the Safavids had to say what became of them: absorbed into the Persian-speaking service gentry, planted on a frontier, bound to the throne as disciples, driven out, or broken.

    The Matter of Tork is that question as a disaster. It does not audit a claim, as the Farr Under Examination and the Burden of the Sword do. It applies a light standing friction and waits for an answer, and it is the only door to the mechanics that settle the Türk question.

    When it fires¶

    All of the following must hold for a country:

    • it is a contender in the Erānzamīn situation, independent, with its capital in Erānzamīn;
    • it owns, directly or through subjects, more than 240 locations, and has held that size for 24 consecutive months (falling below resets the count);
    • at least 750,000 Turco-Mongol tribesmen (Turkic, Tatar or Mongolian cultures) live in the realm. The census is taken once a year. A realm whose tribesmen are Kurds, Lurs and Baluch never faces the Matter;
    • the ruler is not of the Borjigin dynasty, and the realm is not a Chinggisid Regency, an Amīr Sultanate or a Türkmen Confederation. A khan, or a regent ruling in a khan's name, gives the hosts no grievance; in the Türk-coded regimes the hosts are the constituency;
    • the Amirs or the Tribes estate has a target satisfaction below 65%;
    • the Matter has not already been faced. It happens once per country.

    Once the gate is open the disaster is rolled monthly. In practice this is a mid-campaign event, arriving as the consolidation of Iran completes.

    The standing friction¶

    While the Matter runs:

    Effect Value
    Amirs estate target satisfaction −10%
    Tribes estate target satisfaction −10%
    Monthly Amirs rebel growth +0.01
    Monthly Tribes rebel growth +0.015
    Monthly legitimacy −0.10

    Beginning the Dissolve or releasing a march applies An Answer Given, which cancels three quarters of this (+7.5% to both estates, −0.0075 and −0.01 rebel growth, +0.075 monthly legitimacy) until the disaster ends.

    The clock¶

    The panel shows three bars: Months without an answer (out of 240), Iranization — the long work, and Expulsion — the hosts moved on (out of 120).

    • At 240 months without the proclamation, the hosts answer for you (see Catastrophe). Only pressing The Answer Is Given, an eradication, or a defection war stops this clock. A finished path does not.
    • The clock is set back 12 months each time the hosts see the state respond: when the amīrs are seated at the dīwān, at each of the other three Dissolve steps, and for every Registered Pasture built.

    How it is answered¶

    The Matter has three layers.

    1. An approach, chosen at the Council on the Hosts. An approach is a direction of preparation: it applies one ambient modifier and switches on its own panel actions.
    2. One or more paths, the lasting answers. Paths stack: you may dissolve the settled hosts, release a march and swear the rest as disciples in the same reign, and each completed path keeps its own rewards. Eradication alone excludes every other path.
    3. The proclamation, The Answer Is Given, which closes the Matter with whatever you have completed.

    The Council on the Hosts¶

    The disaster opens with the council, which offers four approaches or an adjournment. Change Approach reconvenes it, with a two-year cooldown. Switching swaps only the ambient modifier; privileges granted, corps tiers bought, buildings and axis movement all stay, and the clock does not reset.

    One trap: the council can no longer be reconvened once any path has been begun (the Dissolve claimed, a march released, the bond sworn, Expulsion ordered). Approach actions only work while their approach is active, so finish the approach-bound preparation for every path you intend to take before you begin the first.

    Prices below are in scaled gold, which grows with the size of your economy.

    "We must reduce our reliance on these barbarians" — Reducing the Reliance¶

    Ambient: +2.5% discipline, −2.5% Amirs satisfaction. Prepares Expulsion and Eradication, though the corps is useful under any answer. Choosing it once makes three privileges permanently grantable: Dīwān al-ʿArḍ (the chancery runs the corps: Dīwān power +20%, Amirs satisfaction −10%) or Amir-Held Commands (painless, but Amirs power +15%) — one or the other — and Fostering Households for the peasants. Either administration privilege lets you build Ghulam Barracks (towns and cities, up to three levels, staffed by slaves, lets regiments be recruited there).

    The first purchase stages The First Muster, which leaves the corps Drilled to the Drum (+2% discipline, +5% morale) or with Teething Troubles (−1% discipline, −5% army maintenance efficiency) for five years. The bad result is much likelier under Amir-Held Commands.

At 24 months you are warned (The Amirs Smell the Plot); at 48 the hosts defect early (The Plot Realized) with the same civil war as the 240-month catastrophe. The counter resets the month both estates recover.

    "Iran is the Beacon of Civilization" — The Beacon of Civilization¶

    Ambient: +15% pop assimilation speed, +0.05 monthly toward Tāzīk. Prepares the Dissolve. Choosing it once makes three privileges permanently grantable: Iqtaʿ Commutation and Chancery Commissions for Amir Sons (Amirs) and Salaried Pastoral Levies (Tribes). Each pleases its estate in the long run, costs satisfaction on the day it is granted, and pushes the axis toward Tāzīk.

    Action Price Effect
    Sponsor Qanat Settlement (5-year cooldown) 6 A pastoral location gets Qanat Settlement for 20 years: +25% local assimilation speed, −0.05 unrest, a little prosperity
    Endow the Madrasa of the Registers (10-year cooldown) 6 Ten years of +10% assimilation speed, +5% Dīwān satisfaction and +0.05 monthly toward Tāzīk, at −5% cabinet efficiency
    Commission the Shāhnāma Recension (once) 12 +10 prestige, +5 legitimacy, 5 points toward Tāzīk

    Three events follow at 18, 40 and 70 months under this approach: an amīr's son passes the chancery examination, the qanat reaches a winter pasture, and the margins resist. Each offers a choice between axis movement and Tribes satisfaction.

    "We must forge a new Identity" — Courting the Vilāyat¶

    Ambient: +0.20 monthly toward Mysticism, −5% Clergy satisfaction, −0.05 monthly religious influence. Prepares the murīd bond. Choosing it once makes the Clergy privilege Lodge Endowments permanently grantable (+0.10 monthly toward Mysticism, Clergy power +15%, −5% tax efficiency).

    Action Price Effect
    Endow a Khanqah (3-year cooldown) 6 A Sufi lodge is built free of charge in a location you choose; 5 points toward Mysticism; Clergy −2.5%
    Sponsor the Dhikr Festivals (4-year cooldown) 6 3 points toward Mysticism; Tribes +2.5%, Clergy −2.5%
    Invite the Shaykhs of the Order (once) free A shaykh joins the court; 7.5 points toward Mysticism; Clergy −5%

    The courtship runs on its own counter of months spent under this approach:

    • 12 months — A Shaykh Preaches in the Camps. Tribes +5%. The hosts take to the devotion before the cities do, which is why the bond will hold them.
    • 36 months — The Jurists' Ultimatum. Cave, and the approach is abandoned and the courtship counter starts again from nothing (Clergy +10%); defy, and take The Jurists' Fury for ten years (Clergy −5%, clergy rebel growth).
    • 60 months — Red Headgear. Ten years of +5% morale. This event is what unlocks the bond.

    The Claim of the Hidden Imam is offered once, while this approach is active, with the shaykh at court, Mysticism at −25 or beyond, and the realm not yet Shia. Accepting converts the country, the ruler, the heir and the dynasty to Shia Islam, converts a quarter of the non-Shia pops in every location with a Sufi lodge, and costs 10 stability, 20 legitimacy and 20% Clergy satisfaction. The bond does not require it: a Sunni court at Mysticism −50 may swear the hosts just the same.

    "Why tame what we can unleash upon our enemies?" — The Hosts Unleashed¶

    Ambient: Tribes levy size +15%, +5% morale, +5% Tribes satisfaction. Prepares the frontier march.

    Action Price Effect
    Proclaim the Great Raid (15-year cooldown) free Name a region as the theater. Ten years of The Great Raid (+5% morale, Tribes levy size +10%, a little prestige), Tribes +5%, and the Great Raid casus belli against anyone who owns land there
    Grant the Yurt (1-year cooldown) 12 Needs a holding in the theater. The largest Turco-Mongol tribal pops of the homeland send about 70% of their people to your most populous holding over ten months, and Turco-Mongol nobles ride with them. The destination gets a permanent Yurt Charter (+0.25 unrest)
    Release the March free See Point Them Outward below

    The theater may be almost anywhere in the Old World except Iran itself (Persia and Khorasan, which includes Transoxiana), Anatolia, Arabia, Iraq and Syria, Egypt, the Maghreb, Somalia and the Sahel. The Caucasus, Hindustan and the steppe are open.

    Proclaiming the raid marks every location in the region as a Theater of the Great Raid: tribesmen there do not settle, assimilate or convert while the banner stands, and local unrest rises by 0.25, for natives and newcomers alike. From the month you own a location in the theater, the hosts start to move by themselves: each month the largest Turco-Mongol pop of Erānzamīn not yet committed is paired with your most populous holding and sends about 40% of itself there over ten years, with at most two such streams per holding. The Great Raid war is a superiority war in which taking and subjugating land costs you three quarters of the usual price.

    The five paths¶

    Path Panel button Opens when Takes

    Dissolve Them Into Us¶

    The historical default: Türk becomes Tāzīk over a generation. Claiming it applies An Answer Given and The Long Work while the program runs: −25% cabinet efficiency, +25% assimilation speed, −10% levy size, −5% Amirs satisfaction, +0.10 monthly toward Tāzīk. The program is four institutional acts:

    1. Grant the Amirs Umarāʾ at the Dīwān — Niẓām al-Mulk's nightmare as the price of his solution. The grant pushes the Türk–Tāzīk axis 20 points toward Türk.
    2. Grant the Dīwān Mavājib Cash Pay.
    3. Build a Registered Pasture in every rural location with more than 20,000 tribesmen. It is built on an existing Tribal Pasture, and each one costs 5% Tribes satisfaction.
    4. Grant Salaried Pastoral Levies (Tribes) or Tāzīk Command of Arms (Dīwān).

    While the Matter runs, every one of these steps can only be taken with the Amirs, the Dīwān and the Tribes all at 50% satisfaction or better. Steps 2 to 4 each move the axis 5 points toward Tāzīk.

    Completion needs all four steps, 120 months since the claim, the axis at +25 or more toward Tāzīk, and the three estates at 50% again at that moment. It grants:

    • Türk Into Tazik (permanent): +10% tax efficiency, +5% maximum control, +10% stability investment, −5% levy size, +0.05 monthly toward Tāzīk;
    • Hosts of the State (permanent): Tribes levy size −75%, Amirs levy size −25%, Tribes estate power −25%, +10% manpower. The free tribal levy ends with the tribal compact;
    • what replaces it: the Cantonment of the Hosts building (two levels, in rural settlements and towns with more than 5,000 tribesmen; raises local manpower and rural control) and recruitable Divani Cavalry, light horse that deals 10% more and takes 10% less morale damage;
    • the Īlāt Compact government reform: Tribes satisfaction +7.5%, +5% tax efficiency, +5% tribesmen growth, +3% rural control, Tribes levy size −15%;
    • in roughly four pastoral locations in ten, the tribesmen settle as peasants of your culture.

    Point Them Outward — Release the March¶

    The Seljuk play: the hosts planted on a frontier and pointed away from you. The march is earned by conquest in the Great Raid theater. To release it you must be at peace and hold:

    • at least a fifth of the theater's locations, and no fewer than 25;
    • 500,000 tribesmen living in your holdings there;
    • a seat: a city of yours in the theater whose area has at most one location in foreign hands.

    Everything you own in the theater leaves with the seat as a new tributary — not a march, so that it can wage its own wars. It is a Persianate sultanate under the Amīr Sultanate reform, ruled by a beg of the largest Turco-Mongol people in it, of your religion if you are Muslim and Sunni otherwise, with an aggressive disposition.

    Releasing it seals the compact. A realm on the ghāzī road (Ghāzī Sultanate or Sword of The Sharia) gets The Marcher Compact: +10% manpower, +5% morale, −5% antagonism received, +0.05 monthly legitimacy. Any other realm gets Hired Marches: +5% manpower, −5% tax efficiency. The ghāzī can give the hosts a mission on the frontier; a king of the farr can only pay them to stay there.

    Bind Them to the Guide — the murīd bond¶

    The bond of disciple and guide replaces the bond of the khan: the cavalry is kept whole and re-sworn to the ruler as murshid. It needs five years of courtship (the Red Headgear event), Mysticism at −50 and four Sufi lodges.

    The Murid Bond (permanent): +10% Amirs and Tribes satisfaction, +10% morale, +15% levy size, +1 tolerance of heretics, +0.10 monthly toward Mysticism, −5% Clergy satisfaction.

    The price depends on what your legitimacy already rests on. Farr is itself a claim about the person of the king, so adding the guide's authority to it is a fusion of like with like: −10 legitimacy and −5% Clergy satisfaction. The ghāzī's claim rests on law and deed, and its jurists exist to deny that any living man carries such authority; he pays −40 legitimacy, −20% Clergy satisfaction and twenty years of The Cry of Zandaqa (−10% Clergy satisfaction, clergy rebel growth, −0.10 monthly legitimacy). He keeps his ghāzī reform, but in every case the bond closes the Muhtasib-King law for good and opens Ẓill Allāh — The Shadow of God whatever the realm's rite.

    The bond also satisfies the legitimacy requirement for forming Persia. A year later The Oath of the Oymaqs asks whether to press the camps that held back.

    Remove Them From the Realm — Expulsion¶

    Ten years of The Hosts Moved On: −15% levy size, −5% tax efficiency, −0.05 monthly legitimacy and heavy Tribes rebel growth. No An Answer Given here; the hosts know what is coming. At 3, 7 and 10 years a tranche of tribesmen is removed from every pastoral location (7,500 from each tribal pop, doubled at the second tranche), with Pastures in Ferment behind the first two.

    At completion one of your pastoral locations is ceded to The Exiled Hosts, a new independent country that rides with +15% morale and +25% manpower for 25 years. You receive:

    • A Realm Made One (permanent): +10% maximum control, +3% stability investment, +1 tolerance of the true faith;
    • The Missing Arm (25 years): −20% manpower, −5% morale;
    • the refounding of the Amirs estate (below).

    End Them as a Class — Eradication¶

    The confirmation event lets you recoil; the button returns after a year. Confirm, and both military estates drop to nothing, The Ending of a Class applies (−25% levy size, −0.20 monthly legitimacy, −50% target satisfaction for Amirs and Tribes), and the hosts defect at once as The Türkmen Confederation in a civil war, fighting under Total Mobilization of the Hosts (Tribes levy size +150%, +20% morale) for ten years.

    • Win, and the purge completes: more tribesmen are removed, and two modifiers apply together. The Post-Tribal State is permanent: +5% discipline, +10% maximum control, +15% tax efficiency, Amirs estate power −30%, +0.10 monthly toward centralization. The Fifty-Year Gap cancels the first three for fifty years and adds −30% manpower, −25% levy size and −10% morale. When the gap expires, the payoff stands alone.
    • Lose or settle, and you take The Bleeding Flank and −20 legitimacy.

    Either way the Matter is closed in blood, without the proclamation.

    The Answer Is Given¶

    Pressing The Answer Is Given (not during a civil war) closes the Matter. Each completed path adds to the settlement:

    Completed path Added at the proclamation
    Dissolve +10 legitimacy, +5 stability
    March +10 legitimacy, +5 prestige
    Murīd bond +10 legitimacy, +5% Tribes satisfaction
    Expulsion +5 stability

    Pressing it with nothing completed is legal and ruinous — The Answer Is Nothing: −50 legitimacy, −25 stability, −15% Amirs and −20% Tribes satisfaction, and The Bleeding Flank.

    One caution for players aiming at Persia: as currently scripted, the formable's requirement that "the Place of the Sword is settled" recognises a Matter closed in blood (a won eradication or a won defection war) or one never posed, but does not yet count a Matter closed by the proclamation. See known issues.

    The Dehqān Revival¶

    Once the hosts are dissolved, expelled or destroyed, the Qizilbāsh Aristocracy reform is closed to the realm for good, and Umarāʾ at the Dīwān, Iqtaʿ Commutation and Amir-Held Commands can no longer be granted.

    Expulsion goes further and refounds the Amirs estate: Tribal Hosts (Yurt), Tribal Autonomy Rights, Umarāʾ at the Dīwān and Iqtaʿ Commutation are revoked, and Amir-Held Commands is replaced by Dīwān al-ʿArḍ. After Expulsion or Eradication — but not after the Dissolve, which fills the gentry's place with the commuted amīrs — a new charter becomes available for the Amirs estate:

    Privilege Effect
    The Dehqan Revival Amirs levy size +15%, heavy cavalry power +10%, Amirs power +30% and satisfaction +5%, −5% tax efficiency, +0.05 monthly toward Tāzīk; privileges cost 50% more to revoke
    Service Tenure Registers +5% rural control, +2.5% tax efficiency, Amirs power −10%, Dīwān power +10%, Amirs satisfaction −2.5%
    The Shāhnāma Endowment +0.05 monthly prestige and legitimacy, +10% cultural tradition, Amirs satisfaction +5%

    This is the road Iran did not take: the Persian landed gentry, the dehqāns who kept the Shāhnāma alive, rearmed as registered cavalry on service tenure instead of buying slave-soldiers or leaning on a new confederation.

    Catastrophe¶

    Three roads lead to the same civil war against The Türkmen Confederation: 240 months without the proclamation (They Stopped Waiting), the plot under Reducing the Reliance, and Eradication. In each, the tribesmen and Turco-Mongol nobles of your pastoral locations change allegiance, those locations go over to the rebels, and the confederation fights under Total Mobilization.

    For the first two:

    • War lost or left standing — The Flank Bleeds. The Bleeding Flank (permanent): −15% manpower, −5% morale, −0.05 monthly prestige.
    • War won — An Answer Written in Blood. Broken Hosts (permanent): −10% manpower, +2% maximum control, +5% stability investment. Weaker than any chosen path.

    A realm carrying The Bleeding Flank cannot form Persia.

    The Matter of Tazik¶

    The mirror question — a Türk-leaning court facing the revolt of the cities, the sayyids and the lodges — is not active in 1.4. Its events, its breakaway ("The Urban Realm") and its outcomes are still in the files, but the disaster itself is switched off pending a redesign, so nothing fires. In practice this means Amīr Sultanates, Türkmen Confederations, Chinggisid regents and Borjigin rulers currently face no consolidation disaster of this kind at all.

    Practical advice¶

    • Decide early which paths you want and prepare them in order. The council closes when the first path begins. A realm that wants both a march and the bond should finish the five-year courtship first, switch to The Hosts Unleashed, and only then release the march.
    • Watch the 50% line. The Dissolve stalls whenever the Amirs, Dīwān or Tribes dip below half satisfaction, and the standing friction is already pulling two of them down. Grant the Umarāʾ privilege first; it buys a year and a large Amirs bonus.
    • The axis requirement is modest but real. Seating the amīrs costs 20 points toward Türk; the three later steps return 15, and the Beacon tools and the Long Work supply the drift.
    • Never press the proclamation empty-handed, and do not let the clock run out with a path half-finished: buy time with Registered Pastures.
    • Under Reducing the Reliance, keep the military estates above 30%. Four years below it is a civil war against a half-built corps.
    • Eradication is for realms that already have the corps. The third tier's +15% manpower is the only thing in the Matter that offsets the Fifty-Year Gap.
