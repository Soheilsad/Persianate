    Hordes: the yam, the darugha and the ulus¶

    Where the grass runs, the khan's word runs.

    A Chinggisid khanate was neither a kingdom with provinces nor a crown with fiefs. It was the decimal army — tümen, minghan, jaghun — holding allotted pasture, and authority moved through it by means a settled chancery did not have: the yam relay posts, the khan's migrating camp (the ordu), and the muster. Where the khan needed a settled place held he posted a resident officer, the darugha (the basqaq of the Rus chronicles): Qutlugh-Temür at Urgench and Tuluktumur at Solkhat were both hosts to Ibn Baṭṭūṭa in 1333–34. Where he needed a whole country held he gave it to a prince of the line as an ulus, a share of the patrimony. The two answers failed differently. Appointed begs could never be khan, so they learned to keep one — Mamai, Edigü, Qazaghan. Princes with shares could be khan themselves, and their quarrels were settled at successions. The rule above both was the törü: only the seed of Chinggis reigns, and among that seed the quriltai decides.

    The base game gives a steppe horde no tools of control, a flat penalty at every khan's death and a civil war that follows from it almost automatically. The mod replaces that with the choice a khan actually faced.

    Who this applies to¶

    Rule Applies to
    The Yam and the Ordu, Darugha's Seat, Grant a Share of the Ulus, the event The Question of the Far Country Any Steppe Horde whose ruler is of the Borjigin dynasty, or which holds the Beglerbeg's Regency reform (see The Amirs' Regency and The Beglerbeg's Regency)
    No flat horde-unity loss at a ruler's death; the tümen levy Every Steppe Horde
    The succession poll Every independent Steppe Horde, except the amirs of Ulus Chaghatay, a regent's state there, and a Ṣāḥib-qirān's realm
    The Beglerbeg's Regency disaster Any Steppe Horde outside Ulus Chaghatay whose throne a Chinggisid has held, when its succession seats a man not of the line (see The Beglerbeg's Regency)
    The Törü of the Ulus succession law Seated at game start on Jochi (GLH, the Golden Horde) and Chagatai (CHG); a horde that holds it cannot change it
    The changed Horde Civil War Every Steppe Horde; the rule differs by whether a Borjigin has ever held the throne
    Horse-Lords of the Steppes and Kurultai advances Any country of a steppe or Mongolian culture, whatever its government
    The steppe privileges Hereditary Tümens and Bound to the Tümen Every Steppe Horde that starts from the base game's Eurasian horde setup; any Steppe Horde may hold them
    The tribes in the horde's parliament Every horde with the Legacy of Genghis reform

    A horde ruled by a house that is not Borjigin — the Barlas, Jalayirs and the other amir houses of Transoxiana, for instance — has none of the khan's reach: no yam, no darughas, no shares to grant.

    The Yam and the Ordu¶

    A horde under a khan of the line carries the standing modifier The Yam and the Ordu:

    Effect Value
    Proximity Efficiency +33%
    Number of Local Governors +8

    The first makes proximity carry further from the capital across the whole realm. (The in-game description speaks of flatland only; the effect as implemented is not limited by terrain.) The second is the budget of resident appointees, which is what the darugha seats are built against.

    At game start every such horde also receives the one-time explanatory event The Reach of the Yam.

    Darugha's Seat¶

    The Darugha's Seat is the horde's counterpart of the base game's local governor, without that building's requirement of a road to the capital and allowed in towns as well as cities.
    Where A town, city or megalopolis you own that is not your capital, not a historical capital, and has no other proximity-source building
    Levels 1 per location
    Staff Nobles
    Effect in the location It is a proximity source of 60 (the base governor's is stronger); the country around it is measured from the seat instead of from the capital
    Effect on the realm Each seat adds 0.30 to the nobles' estate power
    Upkeep Tools, masonry, weaponry, paper, books, furniture and fine cloth; no gold
    Built by The khan only; estates and automation do not build it

    How many seats. Seats share one budget with governors, lieutenancies, itinerant court seats and king's manors: your number of local governors (rank, plus the 8 from the yam), plus one seat for every full 10% of power the nobles estate holds. The stronger the begs, the more of the realm is run through their appointees.

    The man. Building a seat fires A Darugha Is Appointed and creates a real character in your nobles estate: a 40-year-old beg with administrative and diplomatic skill between 40 and 70 and military skill between 60 and 90. He is marked as a darugha of the khan, which counts for him in the törü (below). He outlives his post.

    Losing the seats. If the ruler is no longer Borjigin and there is no Beglerbeg's Regency, every Darugha's Seat in the realm is destroyed. The appointed begs remain.

    The Question of the Far Country¶

    Once a year, and no more than once in five years, a qualifying horde is asked what to do about an area it does not hold in hand: a core town or city outside the capital's area, in an area where it owns at least 60% of the locations and where no darugha sits. The best-developed such town is the seat in question.

    Option Requires Result
    Post a darugha 10 political influence −10 political influence; a Darugha's Seat is built there at once at half cost; +5% nobles satisfaction
    Give it to a son of the line A grown Borjigin prince who is not the ruler The area, and a neighbouring area if one qualifies, become his ulus (see below); +5 horde unity, a small gain of political influence, +3% nobles satisfaction
    Buy a man with no name and no people More than 100 gold A payment in gold scaled to your income; a seat is built at once for free; −5% nobles satisfaction
    Let it lie dark — Nothing

    The area is not offered again once it has been settled by a seat or a grant.

    Granting an ulus¶

    Grant a Share of the Ulus is a character interaction, available at any time the realm is at peace.

    Requirements

    • The recipient is a grown man of the line of Chinggis and not the ruler. The Barlas, whom the base game files under the Borjigin dynasty, do not count (see Ulus Chaghatay).
    • Two areas that border each other. Neither may be the capital's area; you must own at least half the locations in each; neither may contain a Darugha's Seat (pull the seat down first if you have changed your mind).

    What happens

    • Everything you own in both areas passes to a new country, with the prince as its ruler and its capital at the best-developed location of the first area. It is a Steppe Horde of Mongolian culture (Chaghatay, if the granting realm is Chaghatay) and your religion, with the Legacy of Genghis reform, 250 gold, two regiments of Horse Archers, and the modifier Newly Granted Share for twenty years (+0.30 maximum control and +0.005 monthly control, fading over the period).
    • It is your subject of the type Ulus.
    • You gain +5 horde unity, +3% nobles satisfaction and a small amount of political influence. The grant costs no gold: its price is the land, and a claimant with an army.
    • Inside Ulus Chaghatay during the Amirs' Regency, a share granted to a prince sets the regency's clock back by 10.

    The AI now grants shares. It does so when it holds much core land at under a quarter control, when horde unity is low, or when the nobles hold more than 40% of estate power; each share already granted makes the next less likely, and it does not grant while at war.

    The Ulus subject¶
    Pays the senior khan 25% of manpower, 10% of sailors, 5% of gold income
    Loyalty +30 loyalty to overlord (three times a fiefdom)
    The ulus itself +10% levy size, −25% cabinet efficiency
    The senior khan, per ulus +0.05 monthly horde unity, +5% levy size, +0.02 nobles estate power
    War Can be called into the khan's offensive and defensive wars but does not join automatically; the khan is not called automatically into its wars; it may declare wars of its own
    Succession Keeps its own ruler, house and succession law. If the line dies out the share returns to the khan
    Building The khan cannot build roads, buildings or RGOs inside it
    Annexation Possible after 10 years at 160 opinion, at three-quarters speed
    Ending it The khan may revoke the share (Revoke a Share); the holder cannot leave

    An ulus cannot be created or offered through diplomacy; it exists only where a khan carved one out.

    The seam. A loyal ulus may not attack the khan. Once its loyalty falls below the game's minimum-loyalty threshold, it can — and that is the succession war the system is built to produce.

    The share returns. If the holder of an ulus is himself raised as khan of the senior realm, his ulus is annexed back into it at the succession poll and the realm gains 5 horde unity.

    The loyalty shock at the granting khan's death that the in-game text promises ("sworn to the khan who makes it, and to no one after him") is designed but not yet scripted; today the cost of an ulus at a succession is the weight it gives its holder in the törü and in the poll.

    For the unrelated sworn governors of the Ilkhanate's successors, see Custody of the Ulus and Ilkhanate governorate.

    The Törü of the Ulus¶

    The base game gives hordes succession by the oldest male. The Törü of the Ulus replaces it for Jochi and Chagatai with a scored, visible field: sons, brothers, nephews and cousins of the ruler, every noble in the realm, and the rulers of your own uluses. Women are not candidates; children are, without penalty.

    Term Score
    Of the line of the Borjigin +150
    Son of the late khan +50
    Grandson of the late khan +25
    Named by the late khan before his death +75
    Holds a share of the patrimony (rules a subject of yours) +100
    Holds a district for the khan (a darugha) +25
    The weight his estate carries in the realm — only for men not of the line nobles' estate power × 100, so up to +100
    Reputation in the field his military skill (0–100)
    Seniority of years his age
    Unsuited to rule −1000

    Blood is worth a great deal and nothing is deducted for lacking it. A capable darugha in a realm where the nobles hold much of the power can outscore a prince; that is intended.

    What happens when a khan dies¶

    The base game's −50 horde unity at every ruler's death is cancelled for all Steppe Hordes. Instead, 30 to 80 days after a new ruler is seated, the realm is polled and one of the following happens. They are tested in this order:

    Outcome When Horde unity
    The regent's own succession A Beglerbeg's Regency is running, or the house holds the office See that page
    There Is No One Left to Raise No grown man of the Borjigin line anywhere in the realm — the new ruler included — or ruling one of its uluses, and no Beglerbeg's Regency −50, and the Horde Civil War can begin
    The Amirs Raise a Khan The seat of Ulus Chaghatay only: one of the two bars is at its line and a man of the line exists to enthrone The Amirs' Regency begins
    A Boy on the Felt The seat of Ulus Chaghatay only: the new khan is a child or has a regent −5, and both bars stand a third lower at the next succession
    The Beglerbeg Outside Ulus Chaghatay: the new ruler is not of the line, on a throne a Chinggisid has held The Beglerbeg's Regency begins
    The Amirs Have Been Counting The new ruler is not of the line, where the row above does not apply; or the realm has 4 or more Darugha's Seats and the nobles hold over 35% of estate power; or there is a regent and 3 or more seats −20
    Too Many Sons of the Line The contest reaches 4, counting 1 for every grown Borjigin man in the realm other than the ruler and 4 for every ulus ruled by one −15
    The Quriltai Is of One Mind Otherwise +5

    So the bill at the funeral is for the road taken in life: many shares make a contested field, many seats make the begs' play, and a realm that used neither pays nothing.

    At present the four generic events (There Is No One Left to Raise, The Amirs Have Been Counting, Too Many Sons of the Line, The Quriltai Is of One Mind) carry only their horde-unity consequence. The choices their texts point to (purge or appanage; the beglerbeg's terms) are not yet written, and two of the descriptions still show a developer's placeholder note.

    The Horde Civil War¶

    The mod keeps the base Horde Civil War disaster and changes two things.

    When it can start.

    Horde Condition
    A throne a Borjigin has ever held Only when the blood has failed: no living man of the line in the realm who is an adult, the ruler or the heir, and no Beglerbeg's Regency. Horde unity no longer matters
    Any other horde The base rule: horde unity at 40 or less; or at 75 or less with a regent; or at 60 or less with an heir

    Who can be a pretender. In the base game any adult noble of the realm may claim the throne. Here a pretender must be a patrilineal Borjigin or of a steppe or Mongolian culture. Religion is not tested.

    The begs' privileges, the kuriltai and the parliament¶

    A beg's hold was on tribesmen, not on peasants: a hereditary command, and men who could not leave their unit. The base game starts a horde's nobles with two privileges written for a lord and his manor (a monopoly of mill and oven; serfs tied to the land). The mod's copy of the base horde setup swaps them for two steppe privileges of the nobles' estate, which any Steppe Horde can hold.

    Privilege Effect
    Hereditary Tümens +50% nobles' estate power, −10% political influence gain, −5% maximum control, a monthly drift toward aristocracy, and the nobles may keep a private army
    Bound to the Tümen +40% nobles' estate power, −10% tribesmen food consumption, +5% levy size

    Both raise the nobles' target satisfaction, and both carry a line lowering "tribesmen enfranchisement", a modifier the mod defines as a test and which may have no effect in the engine.

    Two smaller rules changed with them:

    • The Kurultai building may stand wherever its owner's primary culture is of the steppe group, not only where the location's majority is. A horde seated in a Tajik city (the Yasa'uri at Samarkand and Bukhara) can build and upgrade its Kurultai, and so recruit and grow its manpower.
    • The tribes sit in a horde's parliament. The Legacy of Genghis reform now lets the tribes estate take part; before, a horde's assembly seated nobles and peasants only. The reform is also open to hordes of Chaghatay culture, not only Mongolian ones.

    The tümen levy, advances and pasture¶

    • Levy. The horde levy (Steppe Horde Militia) is raised from tribesmen only and at 10% of them. The base game uses the generic peasant-infantry rate and draws on the whole population.
    • Advances. Horse-Lords of the Steppes (−33% light cavalry maintenance and reinforcement cost, levies always allowed, Horse Archers) and Kurultai (−50% levy maintenance, the Kurultai building) are open to any country of a steppe or Mongolian culture — including Persianized Mongol — and no longer only to Steppe Hordes. A Turco-Mongol house that takes a sultan's titles keeps its horsemen; see The Matter of Tork for what it loses instead.
    • Steppe Tribal Pasture. A rural building for the Steppes and Khorasan regions (cold arid or continental climate, grassland or sparse vegetation, more than 1,000 tribesmen). Up to 20 levels of 1,000 tribesmen each; a level yields horses (0.45) or livestock (0.75), lowers the location's population capacity by 2,000 and gives +0.3 local tribes estate power and +2% local levy size.
    • Tribal Pasture. The larger range of the plateau and its neighbours (Persia, Khorasan, the Fertile Crescent, Anatolia, the Caucasus and the Steppes; arid, cold arid or continental climate; grassland, sparse or seasonally irrigated land). It needs more than 5,000 tribesmen and local control below 33%. Up to 5 levels of 5,000 tribesmen each; a level yields horses (1.8) or livestock (3), lowers population capacity by 7,500 and gives +1.5 local tribes estate power and +10% local levy size.
    • At game start every Steppe Horde with its capital in the Steppes or Khorasan region receives a free Steppe Tribal Pasture in each of its rural locations there that has between 1,000 and 10,000 tribesmen, and each of its locations producing salt, iron, copper, gold or silver gains +1 maximum RGO workers and 1,000 labourers.

    Playing it¶

    • Count your seats before the khan dies. Four seats with the nobles above 35% of estate power turns the next succession into the begs' play. Each seat itself raises the nobles' power, and each 10% of that power opens another seat.
    • Grant shares with the törü open. A prince with an ulus gains +100 in the succession and counts four times in the contest. One share is safe; a second share to a second prince is a contested succession.
    • Keep a grown man of the line alive. For Jochi and Chagatai the civil war now has a single cause. Uluses held by Borjigin princes count as living blood for the poll.
    • A darugha cannot share ground with an ulus. The two answers are exclusive in the same area, in both directions.
    • Read the line of succession. The törü shows its candidates and their scores at all times. A beg near the top is a warning that the next accession may go to the begs.
