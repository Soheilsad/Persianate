    The Borrowed Mandate — Custody of the Ulus¶

    One ulus, one khan, one steward — and it will be us.

    The Mongol political order rested on one rule: only a descendant of Chinggis Khan could reign. When the Hülegüid line ran out, the great amirs did not break the rule; they kept it and hollowed it. Ḥasan-i Kūchak the Chobanid ruled through the princess Sati Beg and then through Sulaymān; Ḥasan-i Buzurg the Jalayirid through Muḥammad and Jahān Temür. Coin was struck and the khuṭba read in the khan's name while the amir held the seal. It worked for about twenty years, and it failed in two ways that were both on display between 1336 and 1357: rivalry — the Jalayirid–Chobanid wars were fought over whose khan was the khan — and expiry, as khans died and each replacement was a little less convincing than the last. In 1356 Shaykh Uvays quietly stopped minting a khan's name at all. A generation later Tīmūr made the arrangement permanent by ruling as gūrkān, son-in-law of the line.

    The Borrowed Mandate is the disaster that audits the regent, the third of the legitimacy trials beside the Proof of Fortune and the Burden of the Sword.

    Proof of Fortune Burden of the Sword Borrowed Mandate
    The claim the king bears the farr sword of the faith steward of the khan
    Under audit the ruler's fortune the realm's deeds the fiction itself
    How it fails the houses stop believing the umma stops believing rival khans, and dead ones

    The regency¶

    The Chinggisid Regency cannot be chosen from the reform menu. The only way in is the opening event of the Succession of Erānzamīn — Find us a Borjigin to enthrone — or to start as the Chobanids (CHB) or Jalayirids (JAL), who hold it from the first day with Sati Beg and Muḥammad Khan as their kept khans. Only a member of the Erānzamīn organisation can hold it.

    It is the cheapest legitimacy in the game, and it comes with a ceiling.

    Effect of the reform Value
    Monthly legitimacy +0.5
    Tribes levy size +15%
    Tribesmen population growth +20%
    All estates' target satisfaction +7.5%
    Tolerated and accepted culture upkeep −66%
    Monthly prestige −0.2
    Political influence gain −20%
    Maximum control −3%
    Cabinet efficiency −10%
    Monthly drift toward decentralisation +0.10
    Monthly drift toward Türk +0.10

    Every act is issued in the puppet's name and has to be laundered through him first; the chancery never quite reaches full stride. While it lasts, a regent also cannot be asked the Matter of Tork and cannot form Persia — he rules in another man's name.

    When the audit starts¶

    The disaster begins as soon as a realm holds the Chinggisid Regency, and it happens once per realm: however it ends, it never returns. It opens with the event The Borrowed Mandate, which lays out the two truths below.

    While it runs, the disaster itself costs:

    Effect Value
    Monthly legitimacy −0.10
    Dīwān rebel growth +0.005 per month

    That is the price of keeping the mask on. The heavier costs come from the two axes.

    The mandate is indivisible — the Duel of Khans¶

    Each month a census counts the rival custodies standing: every other member of the Erānzamīn organisation that either

    • holds the Chinggisid Regency itself, or
    • is ruled by a Borjigin outright.

    Subjects count as well as independent realms. In 1337 that means, at the least, the other great regent (Chobanid for the Jalayirids and the reverse) and Gurgan (GRG) under Ṭoghā Temür — the actual Borjigin sovereign of Khurasan, against whom every regency's fiction is junior — plus any other Borjigin-ruled member and every house that answered the opening question with a puppet of its own.

    While any rival stands you carry The Duel of Khans, sized by their number:

    Per rival custody (up to eight) Value
    Monthly legitimacy −0.10
    Antagonism received +2.5%

    Three rivals plus the disaster's own drag take −0.40 of the regency's +0.5. Every rival that falls — annexed by you, annexed by someone else, driven out of the regency, or replaced by a non-Borjigin ruler — gives a tenth back the next month.

    A rival that you annex by force while the disaster runs is also a kill, and kills unlock the apparatus below faster than waiting does.

    When the count reaches zero the Sole Custody clock starts. It needs 24 unbroken months; a single new rival — an AI that takes up a puppet, a Borjigin inheriting somewhere — resets it to nothing.

    The mandate is mortal — the charade¶

    The kept khan is a character at your court and he will die. Each time, The Khan is Dead asks whether to end the fiction (−100 legitimacy, −25 stability, Amirs −30%, Tribes −30%, Dīwān +5%) or to enthrone another. Each enthronement deepens The Charade Continues by one step: −0.15 monthly legitimacy, −5% Amirs and −10% Dīwān target satisfaction per step. After three enthronements no fourth is offered, and the next death leaves only the full-price exit. The details are on the situation page.

    The disaster panel counts this as the charade out of 4. The count starts at 1 with your first khan and rises by one with each replacement.

    The panel¶

    The disaster panel shows three tracks:

    Track Reads You want
    Rival custodies standing the monthly census 0
    Sole Custody — months held months at zero rivals, out of 24 24
    The charade — successions survived out of 4 as low as you can keep it

    The apparatus¶

    Six actions belong to the disaster. Most of the maintenance tools are gated on the contest going your way: you earn the fiction by winning the duel, or by outlasting the field as it kills itself. Gold costs scale with the size of your economy; the figure given is the multiplier.

    Action Requires Costs Cooldown Effect
    Press the Custody at least one rival; at peace — 1 year pick any rival custody from the roll and receive the Succession of Erānzamīn casus belli against it for 10 years. Unlike Press Claim on the situation panel, the target need not be a neighbour — the picker is also the plainest list of who the other khans are
    Demand Fealty at peace 5 political influence, paid win or lose 2 years call an independent Ilkhanate Governorate to swear; see below
    Proclaim the True Custody at least one rival standing, and either one kill or no more than three rivals left — 5 years +5 prestige; The True Custody Proclaimed for 5 years: +0.05 monthly legitimacy and +0.025 monthly prestige, multiplied by your kills (up to four)
    Secure the Khan's Household once only gold ×12 — The Khan's Household Secured for the rest of the disaster: +0.05 monthly legitimacy, +5% stability investment
    The Khan's Name on Every Coin two kills, or no more than two rivals left gold ×6 5 years The Khan's Name in Silver for 5 years: +0.10 monthly legitimacy, +15% minting income
    Summon the Kuriltai of Confirmation Sole Custody held 24 months; once only — — opens the fork below

    All of these modifiers are tools of the disaster and are cleared when it ends.

    Demand Fealty and the sworn governorates¶

    The demand goes to a governorate that is a member, is nobody's subject, and has not been asked by anyone in the last six months. The governor decides:

    The governor… Result
    accepts becomes your sworn governorate; you send robes and gifts — gold equal to your tax base, which he receives; you lose 10 legitimacy (a sole ruler would not have to ask) and gain 10 prestige
    refuses nothing changes hands but the 5 political influence already spent; you are told Fealty Refused

    An AI governor is likeliest to accept when it is weak or at war, and likeliest to refuse when it has an army of its own.

    Governorates also come to a regent unasked — through their own Declare Fealty and through a periodic event that presses them to choose among the strongest regencies (see the Ilkhanate Governorate). However they arrive, while you rule as a Chinggisid Regency each sworn governorate is worth +0.05 monthly legitimacy and +0.10 monthly political influence (Sworn Governorates). The oath is thin — the governor still collects his own taxes and keeps his own horsemen — but each one is a witness that the custody is real. Two of them cancel a rival khan.

    A sworn governorate does not drag you into its wars. When one is attacked by anyone but you, The Governorate Calls: ride to it and join the war as a defender, or leave it to its own walls at a cost of 5 legitimacy and its resentment.

    Resolutions¶

    Sole Custody — the Kuriltai of Confirmation¶

    Winning the custody war buys nothing durable; the fiction still expires. What it buys is the right to decide what the regency becomes.

    Choice What happens
    The Quiet Crown — the Shaykh Uvays move the Chinggisid Regency is dropped on your own terms: −25 legitimacy instead of −100; Dīwān +10%, Amirs −10%. You are now free to take a crown in your own name from the reform menu, the Matter of Tork can be asked of you, and Persia is no longer barred. Until you adopt a new answer you carry Unsettled Legitimacy (−0.6 monthly legitimacy), so have the next reform ready
    The Restored Yarliq — wed the golden line the regency is replaced by The Gurkani Regime and you gain the permanent The Restored Yarliq: +0.25 monthly legitimacy, +0.05 monthly prestige, Amirs and Tribes target satisfaction +5%, a monthly drift toward Türk; Amirs +10%, Tribes +10% satisfaction. The khan is your house now and the puppet-death cycle ends. This is the Mongol endpoint, and deliberately not Persia

    Endured — The Long Regency¶

    If the disaster has run 300 months and the charade stands at 2 or more — the fiction has survived a succession and twenty-five years — you are offered a weaker, dated fork:

    Choice What happens
    Institutionalise it −10 legitimacy; the regency becomes The Gurkani Regime
    Drop it a late Quiet Crown at a survivor's price: −50 legitimacy
    Ride on the disaster closes its books, but you remain a regent and khans keep dying

    The Fiction Expires¶

    If the regency is lost with none of the above to explain it — the mask taken off at a khan's death, by choice or because no further enthronement was credible — the disaster ends in public failure. On top of the −100 legitimacy of the exit itself, the realm is forced into the Amīr Sultanate, the honest version of what the regency always was, and carries The Mask Came Off permanently: −0.05 monthly prestige, −1 diplomatic reputation. As things stand the Amīr Sultanate does not by itself lift Unsettled Legitimacy, so the fallen regent nets roughly −0.45 legitimacy a month until he moves on to a better answer.

    What the endpoints are¶

    Reform In brief
    The Gurkani Regime the capped, stable version of the borrowed mandate: +0.2 monthly legitimacy, Tribes levy size +20%, tribesmen growth +10%, estates +5%; −0.1 monthly prestige, −10% political influence gain, −2% maximum control, −10% cabinet efficiency. No more successions to survive and no rivals to outbid. Land held by a Gurkani realm is immune to the succession's sword rule, and the regime is one of the gates to Iran o Turan
    Amīr Sultanate rule by the army's assent: +0.15 monthly legitimacy, Amirs power +25%, Amirs levy size +20%, Amirs seated in the cabinet, cavalry and manpower bonuses. No farr claimed, no khan needed — and, for a realm that arrives here by failure, no relief from Unsettled Legitimacy. See Government reforms

    Practical advice¶

    • Count your rivals before you count your legitimacy. +0.5 looks generous until the disaster (−0.10) and three rival khans (−0.30) are subtracted. A regent with four or more rivals is losing legitimacy from the first month.
    • Annex, do not merely beat. Only a rival that ceases to exist — or ceases to be a regent — leaves the census. Vassalising a rival regent does not remove him. Annexing him yourself is a kill, and kills are what unlock the denunciation and the coinage early.
    • Gurgan is the long pole. Ṭoghā Temür is a rival by blood, not by reform, and he is on the far side of Iran from Tabriz and Baghdad. Sole Custody is not possible while a Borjigin rules any member.
    • Collect governorates. At 5 political influence a try, sworn governorates are the cheapest legitimacy available to a regent, and each also brings political influence. Defend them when they call, or accept the 5-legitimacy fine.
    • Buy the Household early. It is the one tool with no gate, and it pays for the whole length of the disaster.
    • Decide before the third khan. After three enthronements the charade costs −0.45 a month and the next death is a forced −100. Reach the Kuriltai, or reach the 25-year mark, before that.
    • The Quiet Crown is a ramp, not a home. It is the only orderly road from the regency to the farr, the ghazā, a Tāzīk victory or Persia. Take it with legitimacy to spare and a reform in mind.
