# Demography and cultures

> *The registers do not stop.*

The mod replaces the base game's population of Iran, Iraq, the Caucasus, Khurasan and Transoxiana, redraws who lives where on the eastern marches, adds one new culture, gives the region a web of cultural attitudes, and sets every realm's accepted and tolerated cultures from the people it actually rules. None of it is a button you press; all of it is what you find when you open the population map on day one.

## What it represents

In 1340 Ḥamd-Allāh Mustawfī of Qazvin, a career accountant of the Ilkhanid finance office, finished the *Nuzhat al-Qulūb*: a geography of the realm written by a man who had seen its tax registers. It goes district by district — the revenue assessed, the towns and their walls, the crops, the water, the legal school the people follow. It is the nearest thing to a census the fourteenth-century Middle East produced, and it describes a country of large cities and dense irrigated districts: Tabriz as the greatest emporium between Cairo and Delhi, Yazd and Kerman as industrial oases, Soltaniyeh as a living capital, Rey as a ruin.

The base game's Iran is built from a model that projects modern settlement backwards. It therefore finds people where they live today and misses them where they lived then: Rey is populous because Tehran now stands beside it; Tabriz, Shiraz and Kerman are market towns; there are almost no townsmen at all. The mod's population is calibrated to Mustawfī instead.

That is roughly 26 million people between the Euphrates and the Pamirs where the base game has a little over 11 million. The calibration must not be read as a buff: it is the denominator every other system in the mod is balanced against.

Three neighbouring passes follow the same method on a smaller scale: Anatolia (the Ottoman march thinned to a frontier, Konya and Sivas strengthened — see [Shattered Rum](shattered-rum.md)), the cities of the Golden Horde on the Volga (Sarai al-Jadid, Bolghar, Astrakhan and their neighbours, enlarged), and Yemen (see [the Zaydi Imamate](zaydi-imamate.md)).

### Who they are

The second change is social. The base game's Iran is nine parts peasant and tribesman with no one to keep a register. In the mod:

| Share of the Persia region | Base game | Mod |
|-|-|-|
| Peasants | 77.5% | 70.5% |
| Tribesmen | 14.1% | 16.3% |
| Burghers | 1.0% | 7.6% |
| Clergy | 0.1% | 2.2% |
| Nobles | under 0.1% | 1.2% |
| Slaves | 7.3% | 2.2% |

This matters more in 1.4 than it did before, because **estate power now comes from pop types**. A realm with real townsmen, a real clerisy and a real military nobility has real estates; [the Dīwān](diwan-estate.md) and [the Amirs](amirs-estate.md) draw their weight from these people.

By confession the plateau in 1337 is about two-thirds Sunni, with Shia communities at roughly one in six — concentrated on the Caspian coast (Gilan, Tabaristan, Talysh), in Khuzestan and Lesser Lur, and in pockets of Khurasan around Nishapur, Esfarayen and Sabzevar — and Zoroastrians, Christians and Jews making up the rest. Iran is not yet a Shia country, and nothing in the setup pretends it is.

### Towns and cities

The base game ranks 41 places between the Euphrates and the Pamirs as towns or cities, so most of Iran starts as open countryside. The mod ranks **85 more** there — 77 towns and 8 cities — and raises Tabriz to a **megalopolis**. Kerman, Sabzevar, Kashan, Qazvin, Maragheh, Hillah, Erbil and Shamakhi become cities, and Ganja is raised from town to city; the road halts, shrine towns and district seats between them (Semnan, Damghan, Bastam, Mashhad, Karbala, Kufa, Kazerun, Lar and many more) become towns.

Each starts with a set of buildings suited to what it was:

| Kind of place | What you find in it |
|-|-|
| Iranian town or city | A full bazaar economy: cloth and fine cloth, paper and a scriptorium, pottery, glass, jewellery, dyes, tools and weapons, a marketplace. Even the smallest *qaṣaba* has a paper guild and a scriptorium. |
| Khurasani town or city | The same, with more paper and pottery and a caravanserai — the Khurasan road. |
| Caravan town | Extra marketplace, granary and caravanserais; walls. |
| Pilgrimage town or city | Extra scriptoria and paper: shrine towns are book towns. |
| Kāshī town or city | Kāshī Guilds and kilns — Kashan and Saveh, the tile and lustreware centres. |
| Fortified small town | A castle over a small market. |
| Shiraz | The book city: the largest scriptorium and paper sector in Iran, fine cloth and dyes, almost no metalwork. |
| Kerman | Weapons, tools and fine cloth, two caravanserais. |
| Mosul | Metalwork and jewellery, with a castle. |

What the mod's own buildings in these places do is on [Buildings](buildings.md).

## Corrections on the marches

Four changes correct who lives in particular places, each for a stated historical reason.

### Türkmen in the Jazira

The base game has no Türkmen at all between Mosul and Diyarbekir. The mod seeds about **162,000** of them — 155,000 tribesmen and their chiefs, Sunni — across the Jazira: the largest groups around Diyarbekir, Mosul, Sinjar and Mardin, smaller ones from Urfa and Harran to Cizre. These are the winter pastures of the clans that within two generations become the Qara Qoyunlu and Aq Qoyunlu. They are a pastoral population inside Jalayirid, Sutayid, Artuqid and Kurdish lands, not a country of their own.

### No Hazara in 1337

The name *Hazāra* — Persian for the Mongol military "thousand" — is first attested in the late fifteenth century. In 1337 the central Afghan highlands held Mongol military households living beside the older Iranic population; the people later called Hazara are the result of another century and a half of that. So every Hazara pop in the base game (151,365 people in 19 locations) is divided in three, keeping its size, class and religion: one third **Mongolian**, one third **Persianized Mongol**, one third the settled Iranic culture of the place (Khorasani or Tajik). Nobody is made Shia; these people were not Shia yet.

### Khorasani Sistan and the lower Helmand

"Afghan" in this period means Pashtun, and the base game spreads Pashtuns across the whole south-east. The mod keeps them where the sources put them — Kandahar, Kabul, Ghazni and the Sulaymān range — and makes the settled, Persian-speaking country **Khorasani**: Sistan proper (Zaranj, Zabol, Farah — the Nīmrūz of the *Tārīkh-i Sīstān*), the lower Helmand (Bost and Garmsir, the old Ghaznavid winter capital and its canal lands) and Herat's own district (Adraskan). In all, 441,410 people in six provinces. The Mihrabanid maliks of Sistan (MIH) rule as Khorasanis.

### Mongol Neguderi, Persianized Besüd

The Negüderi (NGD) were a raiding soldiery on the Indian march of the Chagatai *ulus*, not a court. They stay **Mongolian**; only a third of their nobles — the men who dealt with the towns of Ghazni and Kandahar — are Persianized. Their neighbours the Besüd (BSD) rule as **Persianized Mongols**. Both belong to [Ulus Chaghatay](ulus-chaghatay.md), not to the Ilkhanid succession.

## The Persianized Mongol

By 1337 the great military houses of the western Ilkhanate were three generations deep in Iran: Muslim since Ghazan's conversion in 1295, Persian in their chanceries, married into the country's notable families, born in Soltaniyeh, Kayseri and Diyarbekir rather than on the Orkhon. Ḥasan-i Buzurg's grandson would be Sulṭān Aḥmad Jalāyir, poet, calligrapher and patron of Ḥāfiẓ. Giving them the same culture as the Mongols of Mongolia was simply wrong.

The mod therefore adds a culture, **Persianized Mongol**:

- its language is Persian;
- it belongs to **both** the Mongolian and the Iranian culture groups, and not to the steppe group;
- it thinks well of Mongolian, Oirat, Farsi, Adhari, Khorasani and Tajik culture; Mongolians and Oirats regard it as kindred.

The practical effect is that a Persianized Mongol court finds it cheap to accept its Persian-speaking subjects — as it should — while still being counted as Mongol by its own soldiery.

| Rule as Persianized Mongol | Stay Mongolian |
|-|-|
| Chobanids (CHB), Jalayirids (JAL), Hüleguids (HLG), Besüd (BSD) | Gurgan (GRG) under Ṭoghā Temür, the Sutayids (SUT), the Negüderi (NGD), the Jurmanids (JUR) and Ughanids (UGH) of Fārs, and every amir of the Chagatai *ulus* |

The line runs through conversion. Persianization came through Islam, and the houses that had not come in — the two Tengri-following garrison hordes of eastern Fārs, two valleys from Shiraz — are still culturally on the steppe. Ṭoghā Temür's camp faces Khurasan and the Türkmen plain, and stays Mongol too.

Ten characters carry the new culture at the start: of the Chobanid house and court, Ḥasan-i Kūchak, his father Tīmūrtāsh and grandfather Chūpān, Malik Ashraf, Dimashq Khwāja and Sūrgān, with the two Chinggisids the Chobanids keep, Sātī Beg and Sulaymān Khān; on the Jalayirid side Ḥasan-i Buzurg and the child Muḥammad Khān.

As a population the Persianized Mongols are very small — about 60,000 people. Roughly 9,000 are nobles — the Muslim Mongol aristocracy of the western cities and the Persian-using fringe of the eastern march; most of the rest are the former "Hazara" third in the Afghan highlands. In both great regencies the ruling culture is well under one percent of the realm. That is the point: see **A Thin Stratum** below.

## Protected communities

Jews, Christians and Zoroastrians under Muslim rule were *dhimmī* — protected communities with their own courts, clergy and tax status, living in the towns as merchants, physicians, craftsmen and cultivators. The base game enters most of them as slave pops, in places at absurd scale. The mod corrects this where it has reworked the population:

| Community | Base game | Mod |
|-|-|-|
| Jews of Iran (Kalīmī) | about 9,000, most of them slaves | about 61,000 burghers, peasants, clergy and notables; none are slaves |
| Jews of Yemen (Teimani) | about 287,000, all slaves | about 35,000 burghers, peasants and clergy; none are slaves |

The starting culture lists (below) also guarantee these communities recognition: the Jews of Hamadan, Fārs, Kerman, Qom, Yemen and Egypt are **tolerated** by their rulers however small their share of the realm, and six Kurdish emirates of the Armenian highlands tolerate the Armenian majorities they rule.

This pass is not finished. The Syriac and Armenian Christians of Iraq, the Jazira and Azerbaijan still carry much of the base game's slave typing, and are due the same correction.

## Culture relations

The base game gives the region almost no cultural attitudes. The mod adds a web of them, on one principle: the quarrels of this world were confessional and dynastic, not ethnic, and the real fault line ran between the settled country and the horde.

| Who | Thinks of whom | Attitude |
|-|-|-|
| Farsi, Adhari, Khorasani, Tajik | one another | Kindred |
| Farsi, Adhari, Khorasani | Iraqi, Kurdish, Lur, Gilak, Mazanderani, Talysh, Achomi, Semnani, Dehwar | Positive |
| Farsi, Adhari | Mongolian, Oirat | Negative |
| Farsi, Adhari | Persianized Mongol | Positive |
| Khorasani, Tajik | Mongolian, Oirat | **Enemy** |
| Khorasani | Persianized Mongol | Negative |
| Tajik | Persianized Mongol | Neutral |
| Iraqi | the four Persian-speaking cultures, Kurdish, Persianized Mongol | Positive |
| Iraqi, Kurdish, Lur, the Caspian and Baloch peoples, Afghans | Mongolian | Negative |
| Kurdish, Lur, Talysh, Gilak, Mazanderani, Baloch, Afghan, Achomi, Semnani, Dehwar | the four Persian-speaking cultures | Positive |
| Gilak and Mazanderani | each other | Kindred |
| Azeri | the four Persian-speaking cultures, Persianized Mongol | Positive (not returned) |
| Armenian, Georgian | Mongolian | Negative |

Three things to notice.

**Khurasan remembers.** Merv and Nishapur were annihilated in 1220–21, and a century later Khurasan and Transoxiana are still under Mongol hordes. Their hatred is the only "enemy" attitude in the web, and it is one-sided — the victim remembers, the conqueror never noticed. The Sarbadar rising is this anger in the form of a state.

**Iraq is Persianate ground.** Iraqi culture belongs to both the Arabic and the Iranian culture groups. ʿIrāq-i ʿArab and ʿIrāq-i ʿAjam are one name split in two; Baghdad inherited its chancery houses from Ctesiphon thirty kilometres away, and the Iraqi scribal class shares the *dīwān* tradition with urban Iran. In play, an Iranian court accepts Iraqis cheaply and an Iraqi one accepts Iranians cheaply, and Iraqi townsmen staff the Dīwān alongside Persian ones.

**Outsiders see one people.** Any culture that holds an opinion of one of the four Persian-speaking cultures holds the same opinion of all four. "Tāzīk" is the outsider's word for the settled, Persian-speaking world as a whole.

Attitudes matter because the cost of accepting or tolerating a culture depends on what *that culture* thinks of yours: kindred peoples are far cheaper to accept, hostile ones far dearer. A Mongolian-culture horde ruling Khurasanis pays heavily for every people it recognises; a Farsi dynasty ruling Adharis pays almost nothing.

## Starting accepted and tolerated cultures

The starting lists are new in 1.4 and have not yet been verified in play; what follows is how they are designed to come out.

In the base game each country's accepted and tolerated cultures are a hand-written list, often inherited from a different map. In the mod, on the first day of the campaign, each of the 83 realms the mod touches — every member of [the Erānzamīn organisation](eranzamin-organisation.md), the Kurdish emirates, Yemen, the Chagatai khan and his carved domains, Egypt and Delhi — has its lists set by one rule:

- only cultures holding **2% or more** of the realm's people are considered (the protected communities above are the exception);
- each is priced at what it would really cost that realm to accept or tolerate it;
- the realm takes the combination that **recognises the most people** — an accepted person counting fully, a tolerated one at 40% — while using no more than **85% of its culture capacity**. The remainder is yours to spend.

Some results, as planned:

| Realm | Ruling culture | Accepts | Tolerates |
|-|-|-|-|
| Chobanids | Persianized Mongol | Adhari, Mongolian, Azeri | — |
| Jalayirids | Persianized Mongol | Iraqi, Kurdish | Lur |
| Gurgan | Mongolian | — | Adhari, Semnani |
| Injuids | Farsi | Lur | Kalīmī |
| Muzaffarids | Farsi | Kalīmī | — |
| Kartids | Khorasani | Mongolian, Tajik | Persianized Mongol |
| Sutayids | Mongolian | — | Armenian, Oirat |
| Eretnids | Turkish | Turkoman | Armenian |
| Mihrabanids | Khorasani | Tajik, Baloch | — |
| Ormus | Farsi | Omani, Achomi | Bahrani |
| Hazaraspids | Lur | Farsi | — |
| Sarbadars | Khorasani | — | — |
| Yemen | Yemeni | Hadhrami | Teimani |
| Egypt | Lower Egyptian | Coptic, Levantine, Upper Egyptian, Syriac, Alawite | Mizrahi |

The table repays reading. Ṭoghā Temür, a Mongol khan ruling a million and a half mostly Iranian subjects who resent the horde, can afford to tolerate two peoples and accept none. The Chobanid regent, three generations Persianized, accepts the Adharis who are half his realm. A **Chinggisid Regency** also cuts the upkeep of accepted and tolerated cultures by about two-thirds, which is what lets the two regents carry lists that long; lose the regency and the bill comes due.

Capacity itself comes from rank, advances and government. The **Persianate Bureaucracy** reform gives +2 culture capacity; an **Iranic Tribal Polity** has +1.

The Jalayirid list leaves the Syriac Christians of the Jazira (about 6% of the realm) unrecognised: there is room for them only by dropping the Kurds. That is a choice the player can make on day one.

## Cultural weight

In the base game every location's cultural influence and tradition is credited to the culture of whoever owns it. A Mongol-ruled empire therefore becomes, on paper, an overwhelmingly Mongol civilisation, and over time its Iranian and Iraqi subjects assimilate to their rulers. The opposite happened: the Ilkhanid elite became Persian or Turkic within decades. Three standing modifiers correct this.

| Modifier | Who has it | Effect |
|-|-|-|
| **A Thin Stratum** | any realm whose ruling culture is in the Mongolian group — Persianized Mongol included | −85% cultural influence, −95% cultural tradition |
| **The Persianate Continuum** | any realm ruled by Farsi, Adhari, Khorasani or Tajik culture | +0.5 cultural influence, +0.5 cultural tradition |
| **The Arabic Continuum** | any realm ruled by Iraqi culture | +0.5 cultural influence, +0.5 cultural tradition |

(A matching **Sinic Continuum** applies to realms of the Chinese culture group.)

A fourth layer works location by location, wherever the majority is Farsi, Adhari, Khorasani or Tajik:

| Location | Owner of the Iranian culture group | Any other owner |
|-|-|-|
| Rural | **Persianate Countryside**: +0.10 influence, +0.10 tradition | **Persianate Countryside, Foreign-Held**: −0.10, −0.05 |
| Town | **Persianate Town**: +0.25, +0.25 | **Persianate Town, Foreign-Held**: −0.25, −0.10 |
| City | **Heart of the Persianate**: +0.50, +0.50 | **The Conqueror's Bargain**: −0.50, −0.25 |
| Megalopolis | **Jewel of the Persianate**: +0.75, +0.75 | **Jewel of the Persianate, Foreign-Held**: −0.75, −0.40 |

"Of the Iranian group" is deliberately broad: a Kurdish, Lur or Persianized Mongol dynasty holding Isfahan counts as the Persianate world ruling itself. A conqueror from outside it is drained by every Persian city he holds — the more of the civilisation he sits on, the faster his court is absorbed by it. The location-level layer is the least tested part of this page; if the modifiers do not appear on your locations, only the three realm-level ones are in force.

## Court language

Every Persianate Sultanate of Iran starts with **Persian** as its court language — the Chobanid and Jalayirid courts, the provincial dynasties and the Baloch chiefs alike — and so do Rasulid Yemen, Delhi and the Kurdish emirates. Ṭoghā Temür's horde keeps a Turkic court. Among [the *ṭarīqa* orders](tariqa-orders.md), the Qadiriyya and Rifaiyya use the Arabic of Iraq, the Yasaviyya Kipchak Turkic, and the rest Persian. In [Shattered Rum](shattered-rum.md), restoring a chancery in Anatolia switches the court to Persian as part of the act.

Court language feeds the same acceptance arithmetic as attitudes do: peoples who share the court's language are cheaper to recognise.

## Practical advice

- **Open the culture tab before you unpause.** Your lists were set for you with about 15% of capacity to spare. Check who was left out and whether you would rather have them.
- **Regents should count what the regency is paying for.** The Chobanid and Jalayirid lists are affordable because of the Chinggisid Regency's discount. Plan for the day you drop the khan.
- **If you play a Mongolian-culture horde in Iran, you are on a clock.** A Thin Stratum means your culture will not spread, Khurasanis and Tajiks hate you, and every people costs you more to recognise. The settled houses do not have this problem.
- **Cities are worth more than land.** Population, townsmen and the cultural weight of the Persianate core are all concentrated in the ranked towns and cities. Tabriz alone holds a quarter of a million people.
- **Do not expect Pashtun levies in Sistan or Hazara anywhere.** If you are used to the base game's south-east, look again.
