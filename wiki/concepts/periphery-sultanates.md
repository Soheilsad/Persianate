# Periphery sultanates

> *Every amputation of Delhi is performed with Delhi's own instruments.*

## What it represents

The Persianate order did not stop at the borders of the Ilkhanate. In 1337 its two greatest working examples stand outside Iran altogether. In Cairo a military household of Turkic and Circassian *mamlūks* rules through an Arabic chancery (the *dīwān al-inshāʾ*, the army office, the cadastral survey of 1315 that redistributed every *iqṭāʿ* in Egypt) and holds its warrant from an Abbasid caliph who lives in the city. In Delhi the Tughluq sultans keep a Persian-speaking court and the most far-reaching revenue registers of the age, over a countryside that is overwhelmingly Hindu and governed in practice through its own *rājas* and *taʿalluqdārs*. Both are states of the same family as the Iranian sultanates: a cavalry elite paid by revocable assignment, a scribal class that keeps the books, a khuṭba and a coinage. Neither has Iran's problem in 1337. Their legitimacy question is already answered, by the caliph in one case and by a century and a half of the Delhi dispensation in the other.

The mod therefore brings Egypt, Delhi and the Indian sultanates onto the [Persianate Sultanate](persianate-sultanate.md) government, each with its own starting reform in place of the Iranian **Persianate Bureaucracy**. The Anatolian marches are the third periphery and have their own page: [Shattered Rum](shattered-rum.md).

## What they share

Each periphery reform counts as a working chancery. That is what creates the two Persianate estates: Muslim nobles become [Amirs](amirs-estate.md), and literate urban notables of Iranian, Arab or Indian Muslim culture become the [Dīwān](diwan-estate.md). Each reform also:

- exempts its holder from **Unsettled Legitimacy**, the penalty Iranian realms carry until they answer the khuṭba question;
- closes off the Ghāzī Sultanate, the Amīr Sultanate and the Seljuk claim. These realms have their own answer already;
- reserves the cabinet for the Dīwān and military command for the Amirs, as in Iran;
- cannot be combined with Persianate Bureaucracy.

Egypt and Delhi both sit with a [Majlis-e Mazalim](mazalim-court.md) as their assembly from the first day, and both start with chancery offices already built.

| Country | Starting reform | Rank | Starting chancery buildings |
|-|-|-|-|
| Egypt (MAM) | Mamluk Government | Kingdom | Dīwān of the Realms at Cairo; Regional Dīwān at Damascus; Local Dīwāns at Aleppo and Alexandria; nine Iqṭāʿ Grants |
| Delhi (DLH) | The Delhi Dispensation, with Taluqdar Nobility | Kingdom | Dīwān of the Realms at Delhi; Regional Dīwāns at Daulatabad and Multan; eight Iqṭāʿ Grants |

## Egypt: Mamluk Government

The base game's Mamluk reform, kept with its own mechanics (promoting mamlūks, Mamluk barracks, the Mamluk rank, army and promotion laws, Mamluk succession) and rebuilt as a chancery state. The principle of the military change is that the *fallāḥīn* fund the army; they do not fight in it.

| Effect | Value |
|-|-|
| Can promote Mamluks / build Mamluk barracks | yes |
| Monthly army tradition | +0.1 |
| Land morale | +10% |
| Peasants levy size | −100% |
| Burghers levy size | −50% |
| Amirs levy size | +75% |
| Nobles levy size | +50% |
| Dīwān levy size | −90% |
| Tribes levy size | +10% |
| Tax efficiency | +5% |
| Maximum estate tax | +10% |
| Cabinet efficiency | +5% |
| Court spending efficiency | −10% |
| Dīwān estate power / satisfaction | +15% / +5% |
| Maximum control | +15% urban, +10% rural |
| Peasant enfranchisement | full |
| Government size / cultures capacity | +1 / +2 |

The rural control bonus is the Nile: the most governable countryside in the mod. Egypt has no peasant host at all, so its army is the Amirs' cavalry, the mamlūk regiments and whatever it pays for.

Egypt also starts as overlord of the Dulkadirids on the edge of [Shattered Rum](shattered-rum.md), and the Abbasid caliph in Cairo is the authority a victorious Anatolian ghāzī must petition for the title of Sultan of Rum.

## Delhi: The Delhi Dispensation

The base game already gives the Indian sultanates a shared reform; the mod calls it **The Delhi Dispensation** and replaces its content with the Tughluq chassis.

| Effect | Value |
|-|-|
| Can build Mamluk barracks (the *bandagān*) | yes |
| Tax efficiency | +10% |
| Maximum estate tax | +15% |
| Cabinet efficiency | +10% |
| Monthly drift towards centralization | +0.15 |
| Dīwān estate power / satisfaction | +25% / +5% |
| Nobles estate power | −60% |
| Amirs levy size | +40% |
| Peasants levy size | −50% |
| Dīwān levy size | −90% |
| Tribes levy size | +10% |
| Maximum urban control | +15% |
| Government size / cultures capacity | +1 / +2 |

Three things set it apart from the Iranian model:

- **Harder extraction.** Higher tax efficiency and a higher tax ceiling than any Iranian reform gives at the start.
- **A garrison-city state.** The control bonus is urban only. Delhi's writ runs through fortified towns; the countryside is thinner.
- **A real Nobles estate.** The Amirs are a closed Muslim service order, so Hindu lords stay in the Nobles estate as hereditary right-holders. Their power is cut less deeply than in Iran (−60% against −90%), and the peasant levy is halved, not abolished, because the Indian foot soldier is a real tradition.

The **Open Muster Rolls** privilege of the Amirs is the historical way to open that closed order: while it stands, non-Muslim noble pops qualify as Amirs. See [Estate privileges](estate-privileges.md).

The reform belongs to Muslim countries with a capital in South Asia that do not hold Persianate Bureaucracy. It is locked: it is held from the start or granted by the events below, and cannot be dropped by choice.

### The five lesser sultanates

Ma'bar, Rajouri, Samma, Sindh and Honnavara hold this reform in the base game but on mismatched governments. At game start the mod moves all five onto the Persianate Sultanate government with *favourite-son* succession and a Persian court language.

### The Governor's Khutba

The base game's *Fall of Delhi* releases breakaway states, and without correction many of them appear as Hindu polities on a default government. Two days after it forms, any of those breakaways (the Bahmanis, Malwa, Gujarat, Jaunpur and the unnamed provincial sultanates) is put on the Delhi chassis:

- Persianate Sultanate government, The Delhi Dispensation, *favourite-son* succession, Persian court language;
- primary culture becomes Dehlavi, with the local majority kept as an accepted culture;
- Sunni state religion if it was not already Muslim;
- a transplanted service class: Dehlavi Sunni noble pops of 1,000 in the capital, 500 in every other city and 250 in every town.

Countries that rise in India without being one of those scripted breakaways are left alone. A Hindu rebellion stays Hindu.

### The Faith of the Realm

If a country holding The Delhi Dispensation ever leaves Islam, it must choose at once:

| Option | Effect |
|-|-|
| *"The dispensation is ended. Let the realm be what its people are."* | Loses The Delhi Dispensation (and with it the chancery, the Dīwān and the Amirs); −100 stability, −100 legitimacy |
| *"The amīrs remember Khusraw Khan. Kill the apostate; find a believer."* | The ruler and all his children are executed; a Dehlavi Sunni amīr takes the throne; the old religion is restored |

Changes within Islam do not trigger it.

## The Rasulid Chancery

Yemen (YEM) starts with a third reform of the same family, **Rasulid Chancery**: the Egyptian block with a kept peasant levy (−50%, not −100%), a much stronger Tribes estate (+75% power, +25% levy size), a higher urban control ceiling (+20%) and a rural penalty (−5%).

## Advice

- **Egypt** is rich, well administered and thinly armed. With no peasant levy, protect the Amirs' satisfaction and keep the treasury able to hire.
- **Delhi** starts with the best fisc in the mod and the largest unreconciled countryside. Regional Dīwāns at Daulatabad and Multan are your reach; build more before the distant provinces test you.
- **In India, the reform travels with the state.** Every scripted breakaway from Delhi receives The Delhi Dispensation, so Persianate India does not depend on Delhi surviving.
