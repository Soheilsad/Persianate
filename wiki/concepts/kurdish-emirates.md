# Kurdish emirates

> *They could not be taken, and they would not expand.*

## What it represents

When Sharaf Khān of Bidlīs wrote the *Sharafnāma* in 1597 he arranged the Kurdish ruling houses the way a Persian chancery would: by rank of sovereignty, from dynasties that had once struck coin and held the khuṭba down to the hereditary lords of a single valley, each with its genealogy and its seat. The houses he describes (the Rōzhikī of Bidlīs, the ʿAzīzān of Bohtān at Jazīra, the lords of Hakkârî, Çemişgezek and the Zirqī country) were already old in 1337 and most would outlast the Ilkhans, the Timurids and the Aq Qoyunlu alike. A *mîrnishîn* was a hereditary principality with a court that wrote Persian and kept the etiquette of Tabriz, but its structure was confederate: the *mîr* ruled through tribal *aghas* and valley lords who kept their own followings, over a peasantry that in places such as Bidlīs and Mush was largely Armenian. It had no surveyed tax register and no salaried bureaucracy. What it had was a fortress, a lineage, and mountains that made conquest more expensive than a nominal oath of vassalage, which each successive empire therefore accepted.

The base game models these polities as generic tribes. The mod gives them their own government, the **Iranic Tribal Polity**, and prunes the map down to the houses that actually existed.

## Who starts with it

Eleven countries in 1337, all with the **Kurdish Emirate** reform, all members of Erānzamīn in the Sutayid camp:

| Bitlis (BIT) | Bitlis | Paramount emirate of the Van basin; vassal of the Sutayids; overlord of Hizan, Mus and Çapakçur |
| Hakkari (HKK) | Hakkari | Paramount emirate of the high Zagros; vassal of the Sutayids; holds Van, Amadiya and Zakho |
| Hizan (HZN) | Hizan | Vassal of Bitlis |

| Çapakçur (BIN) | Bingöl | Vassal of Bitlis |
| Dasni (DSN) | Duhok | Vassal of Bohtan |
| Zirqan (ZRQ) | Mayyafariqin | Vassal of the Sutayids |

| Çemişgezek (CEM) | Çemişgezek | Vassal of the Sutayids |
| Hazro (HZR) | Hazro | Vassal of the Sutayids |

Changes to the map around them:

- **Hakkari** has absorbed two small base-game neighbours (the Şemdinli and Van highland clusters), and **Dasni** has absorbed Akre.
- The base game's many small Kurdish tags in Iraqi Kurdistan, the central Zagros and the Azerbaijan fringe (Ardalan among them) **do not exist at the start**. Their lands begin inside the Jalayirid and Chobanid realms.
- The Ayyubids of Ḥiṣn Kayfā (HSN) are **not** an Iranic Tribal Polity. They keep their base-game monarchy.

Any country of Kurdish culture that comes into being later as a base-game tribe (a rebel state, a released country) is converted to an Iranic Tribal Polity with the Kurdish Emirate reform on the day it forms.

## The government

**Iranic Tribal Polity** uses the base game's **Tribal Cohesion** as its government power, described in game as *the mîr's standing among the aghas and valley lords who actually hold the guns*. Succession is by eldest male of the house at the start; the other tribal succession laws are available.

| Government effect | Value |
|-|-|
| Expected diplomatic spending | −50% |
| Cultures capacity | +1 |

The extra culture capacity is there so that a mîr can accept the people he actually rules. Without it, the Kurdish emirs of Bitlis, Mus and Hizan could not recognise their own Armenian majorities.

The starting setup gives each emirate a Persian court language, a council rather than a *maẓālim* court, and societal values that are strongly decentralised, defensive, aristocratic and land-bound.

### The Kurdish Emirate reform

The defining reform. It is locked: it cannot be removed while the country remains an Iranic Tribal Polity.

| Effect | Value |
|-|-|
| Monthly legitimacy | +0.10 |
| Land morale | +10% |
| Fort defense | +25% |
| Tribes estate levy size | +25% |
| Tribes estate power | +150% |
| Tribes estate sits in the council | yes |
| Monthly drift towards decentralization | +0.10 |
| Monthly drift towards Türk | +0.05 |
| Government reform slots | +1 |

### Defense of the Mountains

While an Iranic Tribal Polity is at war and is **not the attacker**, it gains **+50% levy size**. When the emirate is the aggressor the aghas stay home and the bonus is absent. This one modifier is most of the design: devastating at home, ordinary abroad.

## How it differs from a Persianate Sultanate

| | Persianate Sultanate | Iranic Tribal Polity |
|-|-|-|
| Ruling class | [Dīwān](diwan-estate.md) and [Amirs](amirs-estate.md), split by the chancery | Nobles and a very strong Tribes estate; no chancery, so no Dīwān or Amirs estate |
| Assembly | [Maẓālim court](mazalim-court.md) | Council, with the Tribes seated |
| Government power | Legitimacy | Tribal Cohesion |
| Legitimacy question | Must answer the khuṭba question or suffer Unsettled Legitimacy | Not asked; the Erānzamīn legitimacy reforms are not available |
| Türk vs Tāzīk | Contested (see [Türk vs Tāzīk](turk-vs-tazik.md)) | Drifts steadily towards the saddle |
| Character | Extraction and reach | Durability |

An emirate is still a member of Erānzamīn, and as a vassal it is drawn into its overlord's wars in the [Erānzamīn situation](eranzamin-situation.md) whether it wants them or not.

## Levies and units

Under this government the base game's generic tribal levies are switched off and replaced by the mod's Iranian tribal levies, drawn from tribesmen pops of Iranian-group cultures (Kurdish among them). The same levies serve Persianate Sultanates that rule such tribes.

| Levy | When | Share of tribesmen | Unit raised |
|-|-|-|-|
| **Zagros Tribal Levy** | Ages 1–2 | 10% | Zagros Skirmishers |
| **Tofang Tribal Levy** | Age 3 onwards | 10% | Tofang Levies |
| **Retinue of the Mir** | Always | 2% | Frontier Amir Cavalry |

The steppe rule that a tribesman is a horseman does not hold in the Zagros. The mass of the levy is light infantry; the mounted element is a small elite.

| Unit | Type | Notable traits |
|-|-|-|
| **Zagros Skirmishers** | Light infantry (levy only) | +10% morale damage dealt, −15% morale damage taken, +10% strength damage taken, +2 initiative; +30% in hills and mountains, +15% on plateau |
| **Tofang Levies** | Light infantry (levy only) | +10% strength and morale damage dealt, −10% morale damage taken, +2 initiative; same terrain bonuses |

Fight in the hills. On open ground the skirmishers are fragile.

## Becoming something larger

The road out of the mountains runs through a great city. When an Iranic Tribal Polity comes to own **Tabriz, Isfahan, Shiraz, Nishapur, Baghdad or Herat**, it receives **The Chancery Within Reach**:

| Option | Effect |
|-|-|
| *"Take up the chancery — let the dīwān rise."* | Becomes a [Persianate Sultanate](persianate-sultanate.md); loses Kurdish Emirate; gains the **Persianate Bureaucracy** reform; −10 legitimacy, −10 stability |
| *"We remain the mîr of the mountains."* | +5 legitimacy |

Taking up the chancery is a one-way door. The emirate's bonuses (the defensive levy, the fort defense, the built-in legitimacy) go with the reform, and the new sultanate inherits everything a Persianate Sultanate must deal with instead: the Dīwān and Amirs estates, the Türk–Tāzīk balance, and the khuṭba question (see [Government reforms](government-reforms.md)). Refusing does not close the door for good; the offer comes again with the next great city taken.

There is no other conversion. An emirate that grows without one of the six cities stays an emirate.

## Advice

- **Play the long game.** In 1337 every emirate is somebody's vassal. That is the historical position and a comfortable one: the Sutayids and the Jalayirids above them fight the succession wars while you keep +0.10 legitimacy a month.
- **Let them come to you.** +50% levies, +25% fort defense and hill-fighting infantry make a defensive war in your own valleys the best fight you will get. Wars of aggression lose the levy bonus.
- **Bitlis and Bohtan start with vassals of their own.** They are the natural cores for anyone trying to gather the emirates.
- **Baghdad and Tabriz are the nearest great cities**, and both are prizes the great contenders for Erānzamīn fight over. Reaching one usually means winning independence first and then outlasting a succession war.
- **Decide before you take the city.** Once you accept the chancery you are playing a different, much more demanding government, starting at −10 stability.
