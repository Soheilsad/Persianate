# The Zaydi Imamate and Yemen

> *He is Imam who is qualified, who calls the people to himself, and who rises.*

!!! warning "New in 1.4 — not yet verified in play"
    The imamate organisation, its tenets, the zakāt payment, the Imam's actions and the rebuilt Yemen are new and have not been played through in a full campaign. Numbers below are read from the files; how the AI uses bayʿa, how much zakāt actually flows, and the road to the Imam's House are the least-tested parts.

## What it represents

Zaydī Shiʿism holds that the imamate did not end in occultation and is not inherited. Any descendant of ʿAlī and Fāṭima — Ḥasanid or Ḥusaynid — who is learned enough to rule by his own reasoning in the law, just, and brave, and who publicly calls the community to himself (*daʿwa*) and rises against unjust rule (*khurūj*), is Imam once the scholars and the tribes give him their oath (*bayʿa*). The oath binds both ways: the sworn owe him the sword and their *zakāt*, he owes them justice. Since al-Hādī ilā al-Ḥaqq founded the imamate at Ṣa'da in 897 the office has passed among sayyid houses rather than down one line, rival imams are common, and the imam's army is the highland tribes — Ḥāshid, Bakīl, Khawlān — under their own shaykhs. In 1337 the Imam is the great jurist al-Muʾayyad bi-llāh Yaḥyā b. Ḥamza. Below him, on the coast and in the southern highlands, the Rasulid sultans run one of the wealthiest states of the age: heirs of the Ayyubid order in Yemen, masters of the Aden customs house where the Red Sea and Indian Ocean trades meet, builders of the madrasas of Zabīd and Ta'izz, and authors of treatises on agriculture and astronomy. Each held what the other could not: the sultans the plain and the ports, the imams the mountains.

## Yemen in 1337

The base game ships one Rasulid country owning everything from Jāzān to Ḥaḍramawt, and no imamate. The mod redraws it.

| Country | Government | Capital | Holds |
|-|-|-|-|
| **Yemen** (YEM) — the Rasulids | [Persianate Sultanate](persianate-sultanate.md) with the **Rasulid Chancery** reform; Shāfiʿī; kingdom rank | Ta'izz (moved from Zabīd) | The Tihāma, the southern highlands, Aden, Ḏamār and Ma'rib, the Ḥaḍramawt coast and wadi |
| **Zaydi Imamate** (ZYD) — new | Theocracy with **The Zaydi Imamate** reform; Zaydī; duchy rank | Ṣa'da | Ṣa'da, Ḥūth, Šahāra, Ḵamir, Ṯulā, Kawkabān, Ṣan'ā' and the Jawf fringe |
| **Yām** (YAM) | Tribe; Zaydī (the base game makes them Ismaili, which is a sixteenth-century fact) | Najrān | Najrān and Ẓahrān |

- The **Rasulid sultan** is al-Mujāhid ʿAlī. Yemen keeps cores on Ṣan'ā', Ṯulā and Kawkabān — the Rasulids held them within living memory.
- The **Imam** is al-Muʾayyad bi-llāh Yaḥyā of the Āl Ḥamza, 67 years old and a formidable administrator (90 / 55 / 35). The imamate holds cores on Ḏamār, Ḥajja, Radā', the Ḥarāz edge and the Ma'rib country: the reach of the imams whenever they pushed out of the north.
- **Yām** is the Imam's vassal and a sworn member of his imamate.
- The two states are **not at war** at the start, and no other Yemeni country was changed: Ḥaḍramawt, Dhofar and the sharifs of Jāzān are as the base game has them.

### The land and its people

The total population of Yemen proper is left roughly where the base game put it (about 2.75 million); what changed is who those people are. A player will notice:

- **Cities exist.** Ta'izz, Zabīd, Ṣan'ā' and Aden are cities; Ṣa'da, Ḏamār, Ibb, Radā', Najrān, Ḥays, Jāzān, al-Shiḥr, Tarīm and Shibām are towns. In the base game Yemen had two towns and no city.
- **Estates exist.** The base game gave Yemen no nobles, no burghers and almost no clergy. The cities now carry burghers, scholars and a small military aristocracy.
- **The confessional line is in the right place.** The Tihāma, the south and Ḥaḍramawt are overwhelmingly Sunni; the northern highlands are about four-fifths Zaydī; Ṣan'ā' is roughly two-thirds Zaydī; the Ḏamār belt is split evenly.
- **Jews are not slaves.** The base game typed Yemen's Jews as slave pops at a fifth or more of every highland district. They are now burghers, clergy and peasants at about 3% in the main towns and less elsewhere. The mainland Nestorian pops are gone; Socotra's Christians stay.
- **Tribesmen are where the armies come from.** A share of the Tihāma and Abyan peasantry is tribal (the Rasulid auxiliaries, below), while the settled Ḥāshid and Bakīl country is mostly peasants — terrace farmers who are tribesmen in politics, not in livelihood.
- **The land is no longer uniform desert.** The southern highlands (Ta'izz, Ibb) are subtropical farmland, the Tihāma wadis and the Ḥaḍramawt oases are flood-irrigated, the central plateau is cold and grain-growing. Goods follow: indigo at Zabīd, gems at Ṣan'ā', iron at Ṣa'da, sugar and cotton on the Tihāma, salt at Aden. Aden is also now one of the best natural harbours on the Indian Ocean rim.

See [Demography and cultures](demography-and-cultures.md) for the mod-wide population work.

## The imamate as an organisation

The **Zaydi Imamate** appears twice in the interface: as a country, and as an international organisation named after its seat — at the start, the *Imamate of Ṣa'da*. The organisation is the community of a living Imam. Its leader is the *ruler* of the leading country, who carries the title **Imam**.

### Membership is bayʿa

| | |
|-|-|
| **Who may swear** | Any Shia country that is not itself the Imam of an imamate. Sunnis cannot. |
| **How** | The religious action **Swear Bayʿa to an Imam**: 30 religious influence and 10 prestige, at peace. There is no free "join" button. The Imam may also invite. |
| **One oath at a time** | Swearing to one Imam withdraws you from any other. |
| **Twelvers and Ismailis** | May swear, but doing so makes them Zaydī: their commitments to a hidden Imam lapse and they take the **Zaydi Imamate** aspect. |
| **Leaving** | Only at peace. Costs 10 prestige and the Zaydi Imamate aspect, and you cannot swear to the same Imam again for five years. |
| **Automatic loss** | A member that stops being Shia leaves. A member that attacks the Imam is expelled. |

What the oath does:

- **The sword.** Members are called automatically into every offensive war the Imam declares, and every member defends every other. All members give each other military access when at war.
- **Zakāt.** Members pay the Imam (next section).
- **Every member** gets +1 tolerance of the true faith and +0.02 monthly religious influence, plus whatever the tenets grant.
- **The Imam** additionally gets +0.10 monthly devotion, +0.05 monthly prestige and +0.05 monthly religious influence.

AI countries are reluctant. Their willingness starts at −40 and moves with their opinion of the Imam (half of it), +40 if they are his subject, +30 if they already hold the Zaydi aspect, +15 for a shared culture, −20 if they hold a hidden Imam, and a sliding penalty for military strength: a state as strong as the Imam is at −30, one twice his strength at −60. In practice the Imam's own vassals and small Zaydī neighbours swear; equals do not.

A player cannot found a new imamate in the current build — the organisation exists only where the setup places it. The rival claimant of 1337, al-Wāthiq bi-llāh al-Muṭahhar, is present as a candidate inside the imamate rather than as a second organisation.

### Zakāt to the Imam

Each month the sworn members together owe the Imam the canonical fortieth: **2.5% of their combined tax and trade income**, shared among them in proportion to their income. The Imam himself does not pay.

The payment has a maintenance slider on the member's side, and the slider is the politics. A member who pays less than half is withholding zakāt and loses up to 0.05 prestige a month at zero; one who pays above half gains up to 0.02 a month at full. With only Yām sworn at the start the sum is small — zakāt becomes real money only if larger Shia states swear.

### How an Imam is chosen

The imamate's succession law is **Rising of the Qualified Sayyid**. It is fixed while the country holds the Zaydi Imamate reform. Candidates are the men of the clergy estate in the country (the Imam's brothers and sons included); women and foreign rulers are excluded. Each is scored:

| Qualification | Score |
|-|-|
| Of the blood of ʿAlī and Fāṭima | +100 |
| Administrative ability | + the full value |
| Military ability | + the full value |
| Age | + half the candidate's age |

Blood weighs most but does not decide it: among sayyids the learned and the brave win, and being the Imam's son counts for nothing in itself. In the current build "sayyid" means the two houses scripted for 1337, the **Āl Ḥamza** (the Imam's) and the **Āl al-Muṭahhar** (his rival's). Each has three or four men at the start. By the numbers the front-runner when Imam Yaḥyā dies is not his son but al-Muṭahhar al-Wāthiq, the rival — which is roughly what the doctrine would predict.

The imamate is a theocracy: its currency is **devotion**, not legitimacy.

## Tenets

The imamate's doctrine is held by the organisation and binds every member. The Imam changes a tenet from the organisation's laws; each change takes a year and is paid in religious influence and devotion. The 1337 imamate holds the mainstream Hādawī position on all three questions (marked *start*).

| Price tier | Cost |
|-|-|
| **The Imam's ruling** | 40 religious influence, 15 devotion |
| **The Imam's ruling, against the grain** | 80 religious influence, 30 devotion |
| **The Imam's ruling, against the doctrine** | 120 religious influence, 75 devotion, 20 prestige |

### Making of the Imam

| Tenet | Price | Effect |
|-|-|-|
| **Recognition of the Qualified** (*start*) | ruling | Imam: +2.5% clergy satisfaction. Members: +0.5 tolerance of the true faith |
| **Explicit Designation** | against the grain | Imam: +0.05 monthly legitimacy, −2.5% clergy satisfaction |
| **The Sword Alone** | against the grain | Everyone: +5% army morale, +25% tribes estate power |

### Who May Be Imam

| Tenet | Price | Effect |
|-|-|-|
| **Open to the Sayyids** (*start*) | ruling | Imam: +2.5% clergy satisfaction |
| **The Imam's House** | against the doctrine | Imam: +0.10 monthly legitimacy, −5% clergy satisfaction. Unlocks **Proclaim the Imam's House** |

### The Companions

| Tenet | Price | Effect |
|-|-|-|
| **Preference without Cursing** (*start*) | ruling | Everyone: +1 tolerance of heretics |
| **Cursing of the Companions** | against the grain | Everyone: +10% pop conversion speed, −2 tolerance of heretics |
| **Acceptance of the Companions** | against the doctrine | Everyone: +2 tolerance of heretics, drift toward Jurisprudence |

Two cautions. The first two tenets do **not** change how the heir is scored: the election runs as described above whatever the doctrine says, until the Imam's House is actually proclaimed. And the legitimacy bonuses do little for a theocracy that runs on devotion; they matter once the imamate has become a sultanate.

## The Imam's actions

Both are religious actions.

| Action | Who | Price | Conditions | Effect |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| **Declare the Imam's Jihād** | The Imam of an imamate | 20 devotion, 10 prestige | At peace; once every 12 months | Choose a neighbour who is of another religion **or** is a rival living Imam, with whom you have no truce or alliance. You receive **The Imam's Jihād** casus belli on them for ten years |
| **Swear Bayʿa to an Imam** | Any Shia country outside an imamate | 30 religious influence, 10 prestige | At peace | Join the chosen imamate (see above) |

**The Imam's Jihād** is a religious war of conquest: its war goal is to take a province, and because the sworn are called automatically, it brings the whole imamate with it. Against the Rasulids — Sunni, adjacent, and sitting on the imamate's cores — it is available from the first day.

## The road to a sultanate

The imamate never built a bureaucracy; when it took the lowlands, it captured one. In play this is a two-stage road, entirely optional.

**Stage one — The Dīwān of the Lowlands.** The event fires when the imamate takes a location that holds a **Regional Dīwān** or a **Dīwān of the Realms**, or any of Ta'izz, Zabīd, Aden or Ṣan'ā' (the last only if it has been lost and retaken).

| Choice | Effect |
|-|-|
| *Let the scribes serve the Imam. Take up the dīwān.* | −20 devotion, −5 stability, −10% clergy satisfaction, −5% tribes satisfaction. The Imam now **holds the dīwān**. If the imamate already holds the tenet The Imam's House, stage two happens at once |
| *The Imam rules by the Book and the sword. Disperse them.* | +10 devotion, +5% clergy satisfaction. The offer does not return for 20 years |

**Stage two — Proclaim the Imam's House.** A religious action costing 50 devotion, 20 prestige and 5 stability. It needs a held dīwān, peace, a living son of the Imam, and the tenet **The Imam's House** (itself the most expensive ruling there is). Then:

- the country becomes a [Persianate Sultanate](persianate-sultanate.md) under the **Rasulid Chancery** and a second reform, **The Imam's House** (+0.05 monthly religious influence, +1 tolerance of the true faith, +5% army morale, +25% tribes estate power, +25% tribes levy size);
- succession changes to **Favored Son Succession**;
- −10 legitimacy and −10 stability on the day;
- **The Jurists' Grievance** for 25 years: −10% clergy satisfaction and −0.05 monthly legitimacy.

The organisation survives: the sultan is still Imam, the sworn still march and pay. The highland levy is kept, and the Tihāma levies are added to it. This is the Qāsimid state of the seventeenth century, reached early.

## Armies

Yemen's two states field armies that are good on opposite ground. Neither unit set can be recruited; all four are raised as levies.

| Unit | Type | Raised by | Character |
|-|-|-|-|
| **Qabīlī Highlanders** | Light infantry | The imamate (and the Imam's House): from peasants and tribesmen of mountain, hill and plateau locations | Takes 25% less morale damage and deals 10% more; deals 15% more strength damage but takes 10% more; +2 initiative. **+35% in mountains, +30% in hills, +15% on plateau** |
| **Tribal Horse of the Jawf** | Light cavalry | The imamate: from tribesmen, nobles and clergy where horses reach the market | +2 initiative; +10% in mountains, hills, plateau and sparse country, +5% in desert. Few in number |
| **Tihama Arab Horse** | Light cavalry | The Rasulid Chancery (and the Imam's House): from lowland tribesmen where horses reach the market | +2 initiative; takes 10% more morale damage. +10% on flatland and desert; **−25% in mountains**, −10% in hills and on plateau |
| **Tihama Arab Foot** | Light infantry | The Rasulid Chancery (and the Imam's House): from lowland tribesmen | Takes 10% more morale damage. +10% on flatland; **−20% in mountains**, −10% on plateau, −5% in hills |

The reforms sit on top of this:

| | **The Zaydi Imamate** | **Rasulid Chancery** |
|-|-|-|
| Levies | +15% levy size; tribes +25%, peasants +33% | Amirs +75%, nobles +50%, tribes +25%; peasants −50%, burghers −25% |
| Fighting | +20% army morale, +15% defensiveness | +10% army morale; may build mamluk barracks |
| State | +0.05 monthly devotion, +0.10 monthly religious influence, +1 tolerance of the true faith, +2.5% clergy satisfaction, one extra reform slot | A chancery state: +5% tax efficiency, +5% cabinet efficiency, the [Dīwān](diwan-estate.md) in the cabinet and the [Amirs](amirs-estate.md) in command, +20% maximum urban control, −5% maximum rural control |

The mod's amir heavy cavalry also takes **−25% in mountains** everywhere. The Rasulid mamlūk core is small and excellent on the plain; it does not belong on a ridge.

## Playing the Rasulids

You are a rich Persianate chancery state at the edge of the Persianate world. The Rasulid Chancery is a complete constitutional answer in itself: Yemen does not suffer the legitimacy crisis that pushes Iranian sultanates to choose a path in [the government reforms](government-reforms.md), and it stands outside the [Erānzamīn situation](eranzamin-situation.md). Your money is the coast — Aden's harbour, Zabīd, the Tihāma wadis — and your urban control is high while your rural control is capped low, which is the highlands problem stated as a number.

The imamate is not a country you can absorb cheaply. Its Qabīlī levy is far larger than its size suggests and close to unbeatable in its own mountains, your Tihāma auxiliaries and your cavalry are penalised there, and the Imam holds a ten-year casus belli on you whenever he cares to pay for it. Fight him on the plain or at the walls of the lowland cities; take Ṣan'ā' (your core) if you can, and do not expect to keep Ṣa'da. You cannot join or use the imamate organisation: it is closed to Sunnis.

## Playing the Imam

You are small, poor, devout and very hard to dislodge. Devotion pays for everything — tenets, the jihād, the Imam's House — so guard it. Your first Imam is old; look at the succession candidates early, because the next Imam is whoever scores best, and you may not like him.

- **Use the ground.** Meet the Rasulids in mountains and hills, never on the Tihāma. A jihād for Ḏamār or Ḥajja (your cores) is the natural first war.
- **Decide what the lowlands are for.** Taking Ta'izz, Zabīd or Aden triggers the Dīwān of the Lowlands. Dispersing the scribes keeps the imamate pure and the clergy content. Taking them up starts the road to a hereditary sultanate with a chancery, a tax base and both levy systems — at the price of devotion, stability and a generation of the jurists' resentment.
- **The Imam's House is a commitment.** The tenet alone costs 120 religious influence, 75 devotion and 20 prestige; the proclamation another 50 devotion. Plan it over a reign.
- **Bayʿa grows slowly.** Vassals and weaker Zaydī neighbours will swear; nobody stronger than you will. Each sworn member is both an ally bound to your wars and a payer of zakāt.

## How it connects

- The **Zaydi Imamate** religious aspect (+5% army morale, drift toward Jurisprudence) is given to every sworn member, and the imamate starts with **Qiyām bi'l-Sayf**, the rising by the sword, as well. These belong to the mod's wider treatment of Shia Islam; the Twelver and Ismaili commitments are described with [the Commonwealth of Martyrs](commonwealth-of-martyrs.md) and [the ṭarīqa orders](tariqa-orders.md).
- Only the living imamate exists as an organisation. There is no organisation for the Twelver hidden Imam in the current build.
- The chancery the imamate can capture is the same institution described under [the Dīwān estate](diwan-estate.md) and [buildings](buildings.md).
- Levies and the light/heavy cavalry split: [the military system](military-system.md).
