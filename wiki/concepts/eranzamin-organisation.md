# The Erānzamīn organisation

> *Interregnum — no Īlkhān since 1335.*

!!! warning "New in 1.4 — not yet verified in play"
    The organisation's panel (the header, the Overview cards, the Notables tab and the role groups on the Members tab) was rebuilt for 1.4 and has not been checked through a full campaign. The rules below are what the mod sets up; the exact look of the panel, and whether every card fills in correctly every month, is still in testing.

After Abū Saʿīd no Ilkhan commanded general recognition, but nobody concluded from this that the realm had ceased to exist. The successor houses went on striking coin on the Ilkhanid standard, staffing the same *dīwān*, and fighting one another for the same four or five cities, on three distinct bases of claim. The **khan-maker** was an amir who held a Chinggisid in custody and ruled in his name, as Ḥasan-i Kūchak the Chobanid ruled through Sati Beg and then Sulaymān, and Ḥasan-i Buzurg the Jalayirid through Muḥammad and Jahān Temür: the *yarlıq* still issued in the khan's name, and the amir held the seal. The **Chinggisid claimant** was a prince of the blood pressing his own right, as Ṭoghā Temür pressed his from Khurasan. The **sovereign shah** had stopped borrowing a Chinggisid name and ruled in his own. Everyone else was a governor, a client, or waiting.

The organisation is the game's roll of that contest. It is a flat federation — it has no leader, no parliament and no votes — and it does two jobs: it marks who is inside the quarrel, and its panel keeps the ledger of who is winning it. The quarrel itself, with its rules and its victory, is [the Succession of Erānzamīn](eranzamin-situation.md).

## Membership

### Who is in it

The organisation exists from the first day with **73 members**: essentially every realm of the former Ilkhanate, from the Eretnids of Anatolia to the Kartids of Herat, from Georgia to Ormus. In broad groups:

| Group | Members at the start |
|-|-|
| The great amirs' houses | Chobanids (CHB), Jalayirids (JAL) with their Eretnid and Sutayid vassals and the Sutayids' own under-vassals |
| Khurasan and the east | Gurgan (GRG) under Ṭoghā Temür, the Hüleguids (HLG), the Kartids (KRT), the Sarbadars (SRB) and their smaller neighbours |
| The plateau and the Persian Gulf coast | Injuids (INJ), Muzaffarids (MZF), Ormus and Makran with their vassals, and the lesser lords between them |
| The Christian north-west | Georgia and Armenia with their vassals |

Of the 73, **44 are independent** and **29 are already the vassals of another member** — the Sutayid house under the Jalayirids, Georgia's and Armenia's dependants, Makran's and Ormus's, and a handful of others.

Membership is wider than contention. Christian members are in the organisation, carry its passive and fight in its wars, but they are not contenders for a Muslim throne. Conversely, the *ṭarīqa* orders — the Safavid order at Ardabil included — are not members at all; see [Tariqa orders](tariqa-orders.md). A full list of tags is on [Countries and tags](../reference/countries-and-tags.md).

### Joining and leaving

| Rule | Detail |
|-|-|
| Who may join later | an independent country of at least duchy rank whose **capital** is in the region. Owning a border province does not qualify — this keeps Cairo and other neighbours out |
| Who may be invited | the same: a country seated in the region |
| Who is barred | the amirs of [Ulus Chaghatay](ulus-chaghatay.md), who answer a living khan of their own |
| Leaving | **not possible.** A member cannot abandon the roll, and is never expelled. A member that kneels to another power stays a member and is filed as a sworn client |
| The end | the organisation dissolves the moment the succession situation ends, by victory or by running out of time |

Members may freely declare war on one another; the organisation gives no military access and calls nobody into anyone's defence.

### What membership does

Every member carries the same passive for as long as the organisation exists.

| Effect | Value |
|-|-|
| War score efficiency | +33% |
| Antagonism received | −50% |
| Diplomatic annexation efficiency | +25% |
| Land morale | +10% |
| Pops' threshold for joining rebels | +0.10 (harder to raise) |

Membership is also the key to two other things: only members can hold the **Succession of Erānzamīn** casus belli against each other, and only members can adopt the **Chinggisid Regency** or the **Ilkhanate Governorate**.

## The four roles

Each member is filed, automatically and month by month, by the basis of its claim. The roles carry **no modifiers of their own** — they are a filing system, shown as groups on the Members tab and as colours on the diplomatic map. The mechanics live in the reforms and disasters behind them.

| Role | Who is filed here | Map colour |
|-|-|-|
| **Khan-Maker** | an independent member that rules through a Chinggisid: it holds the **Chinggisid Regency** or **The Gurkani Regime**, or simply has a living kept khan at its court | dark red |
| **Chinggisid Claimant** | an independent member whose ruler is himself of the Borjigin line | gold |
| **Sovereign Shah** | an independent member that rules in its own name: **Farr-i Īzadī**, **Iranian Kingship**, **Ghāzī Sultanate**, **Sword of The Sharia**, **Amīr Sultanate**, **Timurid Synthesis**, **The Crowned Murshid** or **Sultanate of Rum** | blue |
| **Sworn Client** | a member that is the subject of **another member** — a sworn governorate, an *ulus* share, or a plain vassal | tan |
| *(no role)* | everyone else: independent governors waiting to see who wins, Christian members, and powers that have not yet named the basis of their claim | member tint |

Two rules settle the edge cases:

- **Precedence.** The three independent roles exclude one another in the order khan-maker, then Chinggisid, then shah. Holding a khan outranks being one: Ḥasan-i Buzurg's Jahān Temür was a Chinggisid, but the claim was the Jalayirid's. A regent who also happens to be of the blood is still a khan-maker.
- **Independence.** Only an independent member can hold one of the three head roles. A vassal is filed under its overlord, whatever reform it holds. A member that becomes the subject of an *outsider* is nobody's client within Erānzamīn and stays a plain member.

### Moving between roles

Nobody appoints anyone. You change role by changing what you are.

| From | To | How |
|-|-|-|
| no role | Khan-Maker | answer the opening question with the regency (see [the situation](eranzamin-situation.md)) |
| no role | Sovereign Shah | take the *farr* or the *ghazā* at the opening, or adopt one of the sovereign reforms later |
| Khan-Maker | Sovereign Shah | leave the regency — by the Quiet Crown, or by the fiction expiring into the Amīr Sultanate — and take a crown in your own name (see [The Borrowed Mandate](borrowed-mandate.md)) |
| Khan-Maker | Khan-Maker, for good | institutionalise the fiction as The Gurkani Regime |
| any independent role | Sworn Client | become the subject of another member: a governorate that declares or accepts fealty, a realm vassalised in war |
| Sworn Client | independent again | break free — a sworn governorate drops the khan from the khuṭba (see [the Ilkhanate Governorate](ilkhanate-governorate.md)), an overlord releases or loses the subject |
| anything | Chinggisid Claimant | only by a Borjigin coming to the throne of a realm that holds no khan of its own |

In 1337 the picture is: the Chobanids and Jalayirids are khan-makers from the first day; Gurgan under Ṭoghā Temür is the standing Chinggisid claimant; 29 members are sworn clients; and nearly everyone else is unfiled until the opening event is answered.

## The panel

Open it from the organisations list like any other. It has no buttons of its own beyond the standard ones: **it is a ledger, not a control room.** The verbs of the contest are on the situation panel (Press Claim, Rally, Restore, the integration actions, Declare Fealty) and, for a regent, on the Borrowed Mandate's disaster panel. The ledger is drawn up on the first day and redrawn at the turn of every month.

### The header

| Reads | Meaning |
|-|-|
| **Interregnum — no Īlkhān since 1335** | the standing title; there is no leader to show |
| **Leading:** and a name | the leading contender (see below) |
| **Notables** | how many bloc heads there are — khan-makers, Chinggisid claimants and sovereign shahs who answer to no overlord |
| **Sovereign Members** | how many members are not the subject of another power |
| **Consolidation of the Leader** — *n*/24 | the leader's months at the territorial gate |

### Overview tab

- **The Leading Contender.** The independent member closest to the throne, with a **Consolidation** bar out of 24. "Leading" is measured first by unbroken months spent above the territorial gate — more than 240 locations, its own and its subjects', with its capital in the region — and only then by realm size. Twenty-four unbroken months opens [the Matter of Tork](matter-of-tork.md) or the Matter of Tazik for that realm. Fall below the gate for a single month and the count returns to zero.
- **The Five Seats.** Tabriz, the Ilkhanid capital of record; Baghdad, the old caliphal seat; Soltaniyeh, Öljeitü's city; Isfahan; and Shiraz, the city of the poets. Each row shows the flag of whoever holds the city and, if the holder is someone's subject, the overlord's flag after the word *for*. The throne needs all five **in the claimant's own hands**: a seat held by a vassal is shown under its overlord's flag but does not count for him until it is his own.
- **Khans in Custody.** Every kept khan currently alive, by name, under the flag of the house that holds him. If nobody holds one it reads *No Chinggisid is held in custody.* A subject can hold a khan too; the row shows the holder's own flag.

### Notables tab

One card for each **bloc head**, largest realm first. Each card shows:

| On the card | Meaning |
|-|-|
| Name and ruler | the head and the man himself |
| Basis | *Holds a khan*, *Of the blood of Chinggis*, or *Rules in his own name* |
| **Share of the realm** | the locations held by this head and his subjects (and their subjects), as a share of everything held by all the organisation's independent members and their subjects |
| **Bloc:** *n* | how many countries follow him |

Below the cards:

- **Bloc Rolls** lists every follower beside the name of the head it follows. A bloc is the head's subjects of every kind — *ulus* shares, sworn governorates and vassals, **whether or not they are members** — and their own subjects, followed by allied members who are independent and head no bloc of their own. An ally of two heads is filed under the larger. Allies are listed in the bloc but **do not count toward the head's share of the realm**: they are independent and counted in their own right.
- **Unaligned** lists members in no head's bloc: governors waiting to see who wins, and powers that have not yet named the basis of their claim.

### Members tab

The standard member list, grouped under **Khan-Makers**, **Chinggisid Claimants**, **Sovereign Shahs** and **Sworn Clients**, with unfiled members in the plain group.

### On the situation panel

The succession situation's own panel borrows the same ledger for its header: it sets the **Leading Contender** against **Your Realm**, or reads **You Lead the Succession** when no other independent member outranks you in months of consolidation and bloc size, or **No Clear Leader** before the ledger has one.

## Sworn clients and sworn governorates

"Sworn client" is the organisation's word for any member under another member. Three quite different bonds are filed there:

| Bond | What it is | Where it is explained |
|-|-|-|
| Sworn governorate | an **Ilkhanate Governorate** that has read a steward's khan in its khuṭba: autonomous, hard to annex, free to make its own wars | [The Ilkhanate Governorate](ilkhanate-governorate.md) |
| *Ulus* share | a prince's share of a khan's patrimony, owing men rather than money | [Hordes](hordes.md) |
| Vassal | the ordinary bond — everything seeded in 1337 is of this kind | — |

For the ledger they are alike: the client's land and its voice count for its overlord's bloc, and a seat it holds is shown *for* him. For the throne they are not enough — locations held by subjects count toward the 400, but the Five Seats must be owned directly.

An overlord at peace who wants to rearrange a bloc's map can use **Cede an Area** on any subject: every location he holds in one area — one that borders the subject or lies in its home area, and never his own capital's — passes to the subject in a single act. No cores are granted with it.

## Practical advice

- **Check the Five Seats before you plan a war.** The Overview tells you at a glance which house holds each seat and whether a vassal is sitting on one you will need in your own hands.
- **Watch the Consolidation bar, not the map.** The leader's 24-month clock is the best warning that someone is about to open their consolidation Matter and turn from warlord into claimant.
- **A big bloc is not a big share.** Allies inflate the bloc count but not the share of the realm. Subjects do both.
- **The Khan-Makers and Chinggisid Claimants are the regent's enemy list.** For a realm under the Chinggisid Regency, every other regent and every Borjigin-ruled member is a rival custody dragging its legitimacy down (see [The Borrowed Mandate](borrowed-mandate.md)).
- **You cannot resign.** A member that tires of the contest can defer — become a governorate, swear to someone stronger — but it stays on the roll until the question is settled.
