    Starting the game¶

    Whose name is read on Friday?

    The campaign opens on 1 April 1337, sixteen months after the death of the last Ilkhan. This page covers what the map looks like on that day, who holds what, what happens in the first month, and how to think about the opening years.

    What it represents¶

    Abū Saʿīd died in November 1335 without a son, and within two years every plausible prince of the house of Hülegü had been raised and destroyed by one amir or another. What was left was an empire with its army, treasury, chancery and provinces intact and nobody on the throne. The great amirs ruled in the name of Chinggisid princes they kept in their own households; the provincial dynasties of Fārs, Yazd, Herat and the Caspian coast governed as they always had and waited; in Khurasan the villagers of Bāshtīn killed a tax collector and found they had started a commonwealth.

    One honest anachronism¶

    The calendar says April 1337. The map is the settlement of 1337–38.

    The base game's April snapshot catches the successor states in mid-move. The mod places them where the dust settled: Ḥasan-i Kūchak already holds Tabriz, Ḥasan-i Buzurg has already fallen back on Baghdad, the Sarbadars already hold Sabzevar, and each of the two great amirs already keeps a Chinggisid in custody — including Sātī Beg and Sulaymān Khān, who were raised a year or two after the start date. The alternative was scripting the same railroad of events into the first eighteen months of every campaign and pretending the player's decisions had anything to do with them.

    The base game's scripted opening war — the Hüleguids against the Jalayirids, with half of Iran called in on one side — is removed for the same reason. Nobody in Erānzamīn is at war on day one. The first war is yours, or your neighbour's.

    The great houses¶

    Realm Seat Ruler Standing on day one
    Chobanids (CHB) Tabriz Ḥasan-i Kūchak 59 locations, about 2.6 million people, kingdom rank. Rules under a Chinggisid Regency. Holds the only Dīwān of the Realms in Iran, the megalopolis of Tabriz, and the Ilkhanid mint at Ganja. No subjects.
    Jalayirids (JAL) Baghdad Ḥasan-i Buzurg 84 locations, about 2.2 million people, kingdom rank. Rules under a Chinggisid Regency with the child Muḥammad Khān. Overlord of the Eretnids and the Sutayids, and through the Sutayids of eight emirates of the Jazira. Holds four of the six shrines of the Imāms and a Regional Dīwān in Baghdad.
    Gurgan (GRG) Gorgan Ṭoghā Temür 50 locations, about 1.6 million people, kingdom rank. A steppe horde, and its khan is a Chinggisid in his own right. Holds Soltaniyeh, the Ilkhans' own capital. Overlord of the Jauni Kurban of Nishapur and Mashhad.
    Injuids (INJ) Shiraz Ghiyāth al-Dīn Kay-Khusraw 51 locations, about 1.5 million people. Holds Shiraz and Isfahan — two of the Five Seats. Overlord of Khunj.
    Kartids (KRT) Herat Muʿizz al-Dīn Ḥusayn 33 locations, about 700,000 people. The one city of Khurasan that came back from the Mongol conquest, and the furthest from everyone else's wars.
    Muzaffarids (MZF) Yazd Mubāriz al-Dīn Muḥammad 27 locations, about 530,000 people. Small, compact, and sitting on Yazd, one of the most populous districts of the plateau.
    Sarbadars (SRB) Sabzevar ʿAbd al-Razzāq 6 locations, about 156,000 people. Not a sultanate at all: a Commonwealth of Martyrs, Shia, surrounded by people who want it dead.
    Hüleguids (HLG) Hoveyzeh Mūsā Khān A single marsh town in Khuzestan. The last prince of the old house, beaten by Ḥasan-i Buzurg in 1336 — nearly powerless, and still a Chinggisid.

    Around them sit some sixty smaller realms: Kerman, the Atabegs of Greater and Lesser Lur, Shabankara, Ormus and its Persian Gulf dependencies, the lords of Gilan and Mazandaran, the Shirvanshah, the maliks of Sistan, the Baloch chiefs of Makran, Georgia and Armenia (which keep their base-game vassals). The full list, with tags, is on Countries and tags.

    Beyond the old Ilkhanid frontier the mod also rebuilds the Chagatai khanate as an organisation of a khan and fifteen amirs, splits Yemen between the Rasulid sultanate and the Zaydi Imamate, moves Egypt and Delhi onto the Persianate Sultanate government, and seats seven ṭarīqa orders — the Safavid order at Ardabil among them — as landless shrine-polities.

    Who rules whom¶

    There are no scripted alliances among the successor states at the start, and only a handful of overlords:

    Overlord Subjects
    Jalayirids Eretnids (Sivas), Sutayids (Diyarbekir)
    Eretnids four small Anatolian tributaries, as in the base game
    Sutayids Bohtan, Bitlis, Hakkari, Çemişgezek, Egil, Hazro, Zirqan, the Dilmaçids
    Bitlis Hizan, Mus, Çapakçur
    Bohtan Dasni
    Gurgan Jauni Kurban
    Injuids Khunj
    Ormus Fal, Jāsk (and the Jarwanids across the water)
    Makran Bampūr, Buleda, Kolwa
    Lahijan Ranekouh

    The base game's long lists of Jalayirid and Gurgan vassals are gone. Qom, Kashan, Hamadan, Tabaristan, the Kartids and the towns of Khurasan all start independent, and every independent Muslim realm seated in the region is a contender. The Jalayirid bloc is the only large one on the map; everything else has to be built.

    The Tabriz problem¶

    There is exactly one Dīwān of the Realms in Iran at the start — the grand chancery, the top of the three-tier dīwān ladder — and it stands in Tabriz, in Chobanid hands. Baghdad has a Regional Dīwān, one tier down. Nobody else in Erānzamīn has a chancery building at all. (Two more Dīwāns of the Realms exist far away, in Cairo and Delhi; they belong to other stories.)

    Whoever holds Tabriz holds the central office of the Ilkhanate's bureaucracy, the largest city between Cairo and Delhi, and one of the Five Seats. The Jalayirids and Chobanids fought over it for two decades. The mod gives you the institutional reason their war mattered: see Buildings for what the chancery tiers do.

    The Five Seats¶

    Five cities decide the succession: Tabriz, Baghdad, Soltaniyeh, Isfahan and Shiraz. Nobody can win the Succession of Erānzamīn without owning all five directly. On day one they are split four ways:

    Seat Holder
    Tabriz Chobanids
    Baghdad Jalayirids
    Soltaniyeh Gurgan
    Isfahan Injuids
    Shiraz Injuids

    The Erānzamīn organisation panel shows the holders at any moment.

    What happens on day one¶

    • The Succession of Erānzamīn and The Sarbadar Phenomenon both begin. Every contender is shown Hulagu's Seat Stands Empty and must say on what ground he rules: through a Chinggisid he keeps, as the Caliph's sword, by the farr of Iranian kingship, as a mere governor, or — for Ṭoghā Temür and Mūsā Khān, Chinggisids themselves — by his own blood. The options differ by who you are; they are laid out on the situation page. Read it before you click.
    • Both estates of a Persianate Sultanate exist from the first day. The Dīwān and the Amirs each start with two named men: the scribes are Persian whatever the dynasty's culture, the amirs share the ruler's.
    • The starting privileges are already granted. A Tāzīk-leaning court (the Injuids, Muzaffarids, Kartids, Shirvan, the Caspian lords and most city-states) opens at +25 on the Türk–Tāzīk axis with Land Registers, Tax Farming, Provincial Auditors, Iqtāʿ Grant and Right to Retinue. A Türk-leaning one (the Chobanids, Jalayirids, Eretnids, Sutayids, Kerman, the Lur atabegs, the Baloch chiefs) opens at −25 with Iqtāʿ Grant, Frontier Governorship, Right to Retinue and Tribal Hosts (Yurt), and only Land Registers on the Dīwān's side. Both have the Muṣādara Right and the Boneh System. See Estate privileges.
    • The countryside is built out. Every member of the Erānzamīn organisation receives, free, its qanāt networks, irrigation works, farming villages and desert clay pits where the land supports them. Some qanāts start damaged.
    • Each realm's accepted and tolerated cultures are set from who actually lives in it. See Demography and cultures.

    What you are called¶

    A Persianate Sultanate seated in the region does not use the base game's titles:

    Rank Realm Ruler
    Under a Chinggisid Regency Faction Regent
    County, Türk-leaning Tūmān Tūman-bāshī
    County, Tāzīk-leaning Ḥukūmat Ḥākim
    Duchy, Türk-leaning Amirate Amīr
    Duchy, Tāzīk-leaning Vilāyat Vālī
    Kingdom Sultanate Sulṭān
    Empire Pādshāhī Pādshāh

    The Chobanid and Jalayirid rulers are therefore "Regents" of "Factions" on day one. Nobody is a Sulṭān until he stops ruling in another man's name. The Sarbadar head is a Khwāja of a Commonwealth; the Zaydi ruler an Imām.

    Opening priorities¶

    There is no single right opener, but a few things are true almost everywhere.

    1. Answer the opening event deliberately. It fixes the ground of your legitimacy for the first decades, and each answer opens a different system: a kept khan leads to the borrowed mandate, the frontier answer to the burden of the sword, the Iranian answer to the proof of fortune. The two regents have no choice to make — their khans are already in the house.
    2. Know which pole you start on. The starting privileges already lean your court one way. Drifting to the middle costs you the trust of both estates; committing costs you one of them. See the Türk–Tāzīk axis.
    3. Count seats, not provinces. The Injuids start with two of five and no army to speak of; Gurgan holds the Ilkhans' own capital with a horde behind it; the two regents hold one each and the only chanceries. Whoever you play, you can see from the first day whose land you must eventually own.
    4. Mind the one bloc that exists. The Jalayirid regent can call the Eretnids, the Sutayids and eight Kurdish emirates. Nobody else starts with more than a vassal or three. If you are small and west of the Zagros, that is your weather.
    5. You are surrounded by rivals, not neighbours. Members of Erānzamīn start with a standing −50 opinion of one another, and contenders may take each other's land far more cheaply than ordinary states can. Sitting still does not keep you safe.
    6. A small realm has other roads. Declaring fealty to a regent, or holding out as a governor, are real positions in this contest, not failure states — see the Ilkhanate governorate.
