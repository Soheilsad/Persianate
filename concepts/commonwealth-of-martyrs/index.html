    The Commonwealth of Martyrs¶

    Sar be-dār — "our heads on the gallows, rather than submit."

    New in 1.4 — not yet verified in play

    Estate power now comes from pop types, and the Shaykhī dervishes' weight was rebuilt for 1.4 with a per-location multiplier. Their share is designed to read about 30% at Sabzavār on day one; that figure, and everything keyed on it (preacher dispatch, the notables' takeover), has not been confirmed in a 1.4 game.

    What it represents¶

    In 1337 the men of Bāshtīn, a village in the Bayhaq district of Khurāsān, killed an Ilkhanid fiscal agent, took Sabzavār, and held western Khurāsān for forty-four years. The Sarbadār state was an uneasy partnership between local landed and urban leaders and the Shaykhiyya, the dervish organisation founded by Shaykh Khalīfa and built into a disciplined network of craftsmen and villagers by Shaykh Ḥasan Jūrī. Its Twelver piety was real and was expressed in the ordinary idioms of the age: devotion to the Prophet's house, expectation of the Hidden Imām, the Sufi bond of master and disciple. Under ʿAlī-yi Muʾayyad it took institutional form: Twelver coinage, and a letter to the jurist Muḥammad b. Makkī of Jabal ʿĀmil asking for a book of law, which he answered with al-Lumʿa al-Dimashqiyya. Historians disagree about how consistently Shīʿī or how egalitarian the state was; twelve rulers in under half a century, most of them removed by violence, suggests how unsettled the partnership stayed. The mod's Commonwealth of Martyrs models that partnership as a government type of its own, and extends it past 1381 as a counterfactual.

    Who starts as one¶

    Only the Sarbadars (SRB). They begin with six locations around their capital Sabzavār, the Shaykhiyya as their religious establishment, a level-2 Dervish Khānaqāh and a Zūrkhāna in Sabzavār, and a lodge in each of two other locations. Their neighbours are Togha Temür's ordu in Gurgān, the Jauni Qurbān at Ṭūs and Nishapur, and the Kartids at Herat.

    At the opening of the Succession of Erānzamīn, a Commonwealth has exactly one answer to "Hulagu's Seat Stands Empty": defiance. It gives +10 devotion, +10% Shaykhī and +5% Sufyānī satisfaction, and for the Sarbadars it fires Heads Pledged to the Gallows:

    • 500 gold (the liquidated estates of the expropriated amīrs);
    • five Dervish Corps regiments mustered at Sabzavār;
    • Revolutionary Zeal for 25 years.

    Other Commonwealths can appear later: a Shia religious rising that has to name its own government may choose the Sabzavār answer, and a village-based ṭarīqa order that rises against its patron lands as a Commonwealth (see Ṭarīqa orders).

    How it differs from a sultanate¶

    Persianate Sultanate Commonwealth of Martyrs
    Government power Legitimacy Devotion
    Political dyad Dīwān vs Amirs Sufyānī Notables vs Shaykhī Dervishes
    Succession Dynastic Dyarchic Election between the two wings
    The khuṭba question Must be answered with an Age 1 reform Not asked: coin is struck in the Hidden Imām's name
    Parliament The maẓālim court The Sarbadar Council
    Army core Amirs' levies, ghulāms The salaried Dervish Corps and town militia
    Rulers Regnal numbers No regnal numbering; the Imām is the only sovereign

    The Commonwealth does not carry the unsettled-legitimacy penalty that sultanates suffer before choosing a legitimacy reform, and it cannot take those reforms.

    The Bāshtīn Proclamation¶

    The founding reform is applied at start and defines the polity:

    Effect Value
    Nobles estate power −50%
    Amirs estate power −75%
    Dīwān estate power −30%
    Shaykhī Dervishes levy contribution +75%
    Sufyānī Notables levy contribution +25%
    Burghers levy contribution +25%
    Peasants levy contribution −50%
    Tribes levy contribution −80%
    Dervish Corps build, maintenance and reinforcement cost −50% each
    Manpower +25%
    Land morale +10%
    Monthly devotion +0.05
    Tolerance of the true faith +2
    Pop promotion speed +150%
    Stability cost efficiency −10%
    Monthly legitimacy −0.10
    Monthly drift towards Tāzīk +0.05

    It also seats both wings in the cabinet and the council, fully enfranchises the peasantry, and lowers the control ceiling.

    The two wings¶

    Both estates start with a base target satisfaction of 40%. Each one's opinion of you falls as the other gains power, so the dyarchy cannot be made happy on both sides at once.

    Sufyānī Notables¶

    Sayyids, civic notables and the heads of merchant families: the men of the courts, the endowments and the ledgers. Their power is drawn from nobles pops living in cities and province capitals. Their characters have dynasties. They like stability, high control in the home region and a Tāzīk-leaning realm.

    State Effects
    Satisfied +12% cabinet efficiency, +8% tax efficiency, +10% maximum control, +5% stability cost efficiency
    Dominant +25% cabinet efficiency, +15% tax efficiency, +15% maximum control, −10% policy change cost, −0.05 monthly devotion, drift towards centralization
    Collapsed −30% cabinet efficiency, −20% tax efficiency, −15% stability cost efficiency, +0.08 monthly devotion

    Shaykhī Dervishes¶

    The armed brotherhood of the khānaqāh network. Their power is drawn from soldiers pops, which the Dervish Khānaqāh attracts and employs, so lodges are how the wing grows. Their characters have no dynasties. They gain +25 opinion while you are at war and +15 under a Shia ruler. They pay almost no tax.

    State Effects
    Satisfied +20% land morale, +20% levy combat efficiency, +15% manpower, +0.04 monthly devotion, −50% Dervish Corps build cost
    Dominant +30% land morale, +25% manpower, +40% loot, +0.10 monthly devotion, +2 tolerance of the true faith, cheaper Dervish Corps upkeep, −20% cabinet efficiency, −20% tax efficiency, −10% stability cost efficiency, drift towards decentralization
    Collapsed −20% land morale, −15% manpower, −0.08 monthly devotion

    Devotion¶

    Devotion replaces legitimacy as the Commonwealth's government power. Its sources beyond the reform, estates and privileges:

    • Conquest: +1 devotion for every location taken.
    • The Martyrs' Rolls: with the privilege, a great battle against a realm whose ruler is of Turkic, Mongol or Tatar culture gives +2 devotion if won and +4 if lost. (Minor skirmishes do not count.)
    • The Tenth of Muḥarram: a roughly annual ʿĀshūrāʾ commemoration. The street procession gives +5 devotion and favours the Shaykhīs; the sponsored majlis gives +2 and favours the Sufyānīs and burghers.
    • The Sarbadār Movement: +0.02 monthly devotion for members.
    • Custodian of the Threshold: +0.05 monthly devotion, permanently, for taking Mashhad.

    Revolutionary Zeal¶

    The founding generation is a wasting asset. Revolutionary Zeal lasts 25 years and decays steadily to nothing over that time. At full strength:

    Effect Value
    Manpower +50%
    Army maintenance efficiency +25%
    Regiment reinforcement speed +25%
    Land morale +20%
    Land morale recovery +10%
    Tax efficiency +10%
    Monthly prosperity and development (all locations) −0.001 each

    While the zeal lasts and the Commonwealth is fighting a defensive war, The Vow Besieged adds a second, undecayed helping of every bonus in the table (and a further −0.0005 monthly prosperity and development). Because the surge is flat while the base withers, a late defensive war restores most of what the years have taken. A Commonwealth founded later by a rising also receives the full 25 years.

    Succession: the Dyarchic Election¶

    A ruler serves until death. The council then chooses among up to five candidates drawn from the two wings.

    Factor Weight
    Shaykhī candidate + his military skill
    Sufyānī candidate + his administrative skill
    Shaykhī candidate while the Commonwealth is at war +150
    Sufyānī candidate while the Commonwealth is at peace +100
    Any candidate + age and all three skills

    In practice: die at war and a shaykh follows; die at peace and a malik follows.

    The Sarbadar Council¶

    The Commonwealth's parliament seats the two wings, the clergy, the peasants and the tribes; nobles are barred. Each wing brings its own issues.

    Issue Wing Result if passed
    Codify the Qāḍī Courts Sufyānī Codified Qāḍī Courts for 10 years: +10% maximum control, +5% stability cost efficiency
    Curb the Dervish Excesses (Shaykhī power above 55%) Sufyānī 8 years of −10% Shaykhī power; −15% Shaykhī and +15% Sufyānī satisfaction
    Negotiate the Amīr Settlement Sufyānī 15 years of +15% improve relations, +1 diplomatic reputation, +10% Amirs power; −10% Shaykhī satisfaction
    Endow a Great Madrasa Sufyānī 20 years of +8% research speed, +1 tolerance of the true faith, +10% clergy power
    Subsidize the Bazaar Caravans Sufyānī 10 years of +10% tax efficiency and +10% trade income
    Proclaim Jihād (at peace) Shaykhī 5 years of −25% no-casus-belli war cost, +10% land morale, +10% manpower; +10 devotion
    Purge the Surviving Amīrs Shaykhī 8 years of −25% Amirs and −15% Nobles power, +10% tax efficiency, −10% improve relations
    Mobilize the Dervish Corps Shaykhī 5 years of +20% manpower and +10% land morale
    Proclaim the Imām's Return Imminent Shaykhī 6 years of +0.10 monthly devotion, +20% land morale, −15% stability cost efficiency, −10% cabinet efficiency; +20 devotion, −15% Sufyānī satisfaction
    Excommunicate the Compromisers (Sufyānī power above 50%) Shaykhī −20% Sufyānī and +15% Shaykhī satisfaction, +10 devotion

    The council also hears maẓālim cases against each wing, five apiece. Ruling against the notables costs them 15–25% satisfaction and usually seizes a quarter to two-fifths of their estate's gold for the wronged parties or the treasury. Ruling against the dervishes costs them 15–20% satisfaction and 5–10 devotion: the brethren hold no fortunes to confiscate, so the Commonwealth pays for disciplining its own base in fervour and is repaid in order.

    Buildings, units and the cabinet¶

    Name What it does
    Dervish Khānaqāh Up to 5 levels, any settlement. Employs and attracts soldiers pops, gives local manpower and garrison, raises local Shaykhī power, and allows regiments to be recruited in the location. Consumes cloth and weaponry.
    Mission of the Liberated One level, built in your own land. Converts the location to Shia and lowers unrest; each month Sunni laborers, peasants and tribesmen there have a chance to convert by halves. Disappears if the location leaves Commonwealth rule.
    Dervish Corps Heavy infantry unique to the Commonwealth. Deals +30% and takes −30% morale damage, takes +15% strength damage, +2 initiative, light supply needs. Stronger in farmland, hills and mountains; −10% on grassland and sparse steppe.
    Charter of the Liberated Administrative cabinet action. Target an owned province that is at least half Shia and still has conquered locations: integration runs with a +8 integration speed bonus until every location is integrated.

    The Zūrkhāna and the Shahr Militia are shared with the wider mod; see Buildings and the military system.

    Mashhad and Nishapur¶

    Two conquests fire once each.

    The Threshold of the Eighth Imam (Mashhad). All three choices grant Custodian of the Threshold (+0.05 monthly devotion, +1 tolerance of the true faith, permanent). Give the shrine to the notables or to the brethren for +25 devotion and +15% satisfaction for the chosen wing; or write to the jurist of Jabal ʿĀmil for +15 devotion and +15% clergy satisfaction.

    The Old Metropolis (Nishapur). Seat the notables for +5 stability, +15% Sufyānī satisfaction and The Nishapur Chancery for 25 years (+5% cabinet efficiency, +5% tax efficiency); or convene the tribunal for 100 gold, +10 devotion, +10% Shaykhī satisfaction and −5 stability. Either way, taking Nishapur unlocks the two chancery privileges above.

    The reform path¶

    A single line of later reforms turns the vow into a clerical state. Each requires the one before it and a Shia realm.

    Reform Age Requires Main effects
    The Niyāba ʿĀmma Charter 2 Chancery of the Vow privilege Grants the chancery apparatus that the rest of the mod's administrative content keys on; cancels the −30% Dīwān power; +10% cabinet efficiency, +5% maximum control, +5% tax efficiency
    Usūlī Consolidation 2 The Charter +30% clergy power, +15% cabinet efficiency, +10% maximum control, +10% research speed, −15% Shaykhī power
    Marjaʿiyya 3 Usūlī Consolidation +50% clergy power, +20% cabinet efficiency, +15% maximum control, +0.20 monthly legitimacy, +3 tolerance of the true faith, −25% Sufyānī and −10% Shaykhī power
    Wilāyat-i Faqīh 4 Marjaʿiyya +75% clergy power, +25% cabinet efficiency, +25% maximum control, +0.15 monthly devotion, +5 tolerance of the true faith, −50% Sufyānī and −25% Shaykhī power, −10% manpower, −20% improve relations

    The last step is a twentieth-century doctrine projected backwards; the mod's own text says so.

    The Sarbadar Phenomenon¶

    A situation, active from the first day and for as long as any Commonwealth exists. It is visible to countries in the Erānzamīn region, the Middle East and Central Asia, and to anyone who has a cell.

    Sarbadar Preachers are covert cells that appear in other rulers' cities. They can only take root in a city with Muslim pops, inside those same regions, that is not already Commonwealth-owned.

    Every Commonwealth-owned city and every existing cell is a carrier. Each month, each carrier makes these rolls:

    Roll Monthly chance per carrier
    Plant a cell in a random eligible city with a Zūrkhāna in the carrier's market 6%
    Plant a cell in a random eligible city in that market whose ruler has granted the Asnāf–Futuwwa Compact 4%
    Plant a cell in any random eligible city in that market 2%

    There is also a long jump: each Middle Eastern or Central Asian realm in trade range of a market whose centre holds a cell or belongs to a Commonwealth has a 2% monthly chance of a cell in one of its cities. Cells spread cells, so an ignored infection compounds along the caravan roads.

    What a cell does to its host:

    • −0.005 monthly control and +0.5 unrest in the location; pops there join rebels more readily;
    • steady conversion to Shia, plus a 2% monthly chance for each Sunni burgher or laborer pop that half of it converts.

    A host can Crush the Cell by demolishing it, for three months' income and 10 stability. A cell dissolves by itself if a Commonwealth takes the city or the situation ends.

    For rulers who are not a Commonwealth, the situation panel is also where Patronize An Order lives (see Ṭarīqa orders).

    Dispatch a Preacher¶

    A Commonwealth with Shaykhī power of at least 25% can plant a cell in a chosen foreign city once every 24 months, at no cost in gold. The cost is at home: each dispatch adds Preachers Sent Abroad, −5% Shaykhī power for 25 years, and these stack.

    The Notables Take the Council¶

    If Shaykhī power falls below 10%, there is a 15% monthly chance of this event.

    • Let there be a sultanate. The Commonwealth becomes a Persianate Sultanate with the Persianate Bureaucracy reform, gains +10 stability and +20 legitimacy, and falls out of the Sarbadār Movement. If it was the last Commonwealth, the Phenomenon ends.
    • Summon the brethren home. −30% Sufyānī satisfaction, +15 devotion, −10 stability, and The Last Purge for 10 years (+15% Shaykhī power, +0.05 monthly devotion, −10% stability cost efficiency). The question can be asked again.

    The Sarbadār Movement¶

    An international organisation seeded at the start with the Sarbadars as its only member. Only independent Commonwealths can belong; members cannot resign, and a member that stops being a Commonwealth leaves automatically. Members get +0.02 monthly devotion and +5% land morale, and open their roads to each other in wartime. A Commonwealth founded by a Shia rising that chooses the Sabzavār answer joins at once; members can invite any other. The organisation has no leader and, for now, no actions of its own.

    Practical advice¶

    • Spend the zeal. The first decade is when the Dervish Corps is cheapest and strongest relative to its neighbours. Nishapur and Mashhad are both a short march away and both pay permanently.
    • Stay attacked, not attacking, when you can. The Vow Besieged doubles the zeal only in defensive wars.
    • Build lodges for power, not only for recruits. The Shaykhī wing's weight comes from the soldiers pops that lodges draw in.
    • Count your preachers. Three dispatches are −15% Shaykhī power for a generation. The notables' takeover is the designed consequence of exporting the revolution too freely.
    • Time the succession. If you want a shaykh, be at war when the ruler is old.
    • Convert before you integrate. A Mission of the Liberated first, then the Charter of the Liberated once the province is half Shia.
