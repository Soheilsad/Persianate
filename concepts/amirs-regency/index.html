    The Amirs' Regency¶

    He has the name of khan. We have the khan.

    New — largely untested in play

    The regency was built in November 2026. Its onset, the panel and the regent's actions have been run in spot tests with debug tools; no campaign has yet been played from a khan's death to an ending. Every number on this page is a first setting. See Known issues.

    A man without the blood of Chinggis could not be khan, so the amir who held the army kept one. Qazaghan of the Qara'unas killed Qazan Khan in 1346, raised first an Ögedeid and then the Chaghatayid Bayan Quli at his own camp, and ruled the western ulus in their names for twelve years. He was murdered on a hunt in 1358 by the son-in-law of a man he had slighted. His son assumed he had inherited the ulus with the tümens and was driven out within the year; no single beg replaced him, and each amir made war on his neighbours as he saw fit. In 1360 and 1361 Tughluq Temür, khan of the eastern half, rode into Transoxiana and for two years there was one khan again. In 1370 Temür of the Barlas took Balkh, married into the line, set a khan on the felt and received the oath of the amirs.

    Beatrice Manz's reading, which the mod follows, is that the conqueror was the product of those twenty-four years and not of the calendar. The Amirs' Regency is that sequence as a disaster carried by whichever state holds the khan of Ulus Chaghatay: four stages, a measure of the regent's standing that only victory and gold can fill, and three ways out.

    How it begins¶

    There are two ways in.

    When Who becomes regent The legitimacy gap starts at
    The amirs raise a khan A khan dies, one of the two bars is at its line 30 to 80 days after his successor is seated, and a man of the line exists to enthrone The amir with the highest kingmaker score — or a darugha of the old seat who outscores every amir 50
    Rise Against the Khan An amir wins the war The amir who rose 35

    The regent takes a prince of the line into his keeping: the youngest boy at the court of any member, failing that the least able grown man; if the line has nobody left, a cadet is found. The leadership of the organisation moves to the regent's state, which takes the reform Beglerbeg's Regency, and the disaster begins there. The regent gains 10 prestige (The Amirs Raise a Khan); every other player in the ulus is told (The Amirs' Regency). Pledges to the old khan lapse. The qubchur the amirs send the seat is halved for as long as a regent holds it.

    If the khan's successor is a child and no bar is met, there is no Amirs' Regency: the realm has an ordinary regency (A Boy on the Felt, −5 horde unity) and both bars stand a third lower at the next succession.

    The old seat answers¶

    The khan's old realm keeps its ruler and is asked once (A Khan Not of Our Making):

    Option Result
    A khan is a khan. −10 horde unity. It stays in the ulus as an Amir of the Ulus under the amirs' khan. Its subjects nearer the regent's capital than its own pass to the regent
    A beg's captive is no khan of ours. +10 horde unity. It leaves the ulus as the eastern khanate. Every member whose capital lies nearer its own than the regent's follows it out as its vassal (an AI member at once; a player is asked in Two Khans). It holds Restore the Ulus on the regent, which does not expire while the regency lasts. Regent and khan take −100 opinion of each other ("Two khans of one ulus")

    This is where Moghulistan comes from. From the 1337 roll the houses nearer Almaliq are the Dughlat, Issyk-Kul and Dzungaria.

    What the regent carries¶

    Source Effect on the regent's state
    Beglerbeg's Regency (reform) +0.10 monthly horde unity, −0.10 monthly prestige, −20% political influence gain, −3% maximum control
    The Amirs' Regency (disaster) −0.02 monthly horde unity, −5 subject loyalty
    The Regency Under Strain −0.03 monthly horde unity and −0.03 monthly prestige for each stage reached (so −0.12 each in the fourth)
    The keeper's office +20% political influence gain, which cancels the reform's penalty, and +0.75 / +0.50 / +0.25 political influence a month in the first, second and third stage. Nothing in the fourth

    A regent's state counts as a khan's for the horde systems: it keeps the yam, the darugha seats and the right to grant shares (see Hordes).

    The four stages¶

    The disaster's panel shows The Stage of the Regency and a clock, Toward the next stage, that runs from 0 to 100.

    What moves the clock Amount
    Each month the nobles hold 50% or more of estate power in the regent's realm, or the three greatest amirs together weigh at least as much as the regent +1
    Each month neither is true and the crown holds more power than the nobles in his realm −0.5
    The kept khan dies and another is enthroned +10
    The regent dies +15
    The regent loses a war, or an outsider takes his capital +10
    The regent chooses to rule without a khan +25
    Recall the Darughas −15
    Marry into the Line −20
    A share granted to a prince of the line (Grant a Share of the Ulus) −10
    The khan's purge fails The next stage at once

    Unchecked, a stage lasts 100 months.

    Stage What it opens
    I. The Beglerbeg's Peace One beg rules through a khan. No member may make war on another. The regent may set a kinsman over an amir house and the house must accept
    II. The Amirs Fall Out −10 horde unity for the regent. Members may make war on one another with a casus belli. Every house takes −100 opinion of the keeper ("Keeps the khan who is ours as much as his") unless it is his subject, has pledged to him, or is both lighter than he is and his ally. Every house takes −100 opinion of every other house ("The amirs have fallen out") except its allies, its lord or subject, and the house it is pledged to or that is pledged to it: what is left standing is the blocs. Claim the Khan and Press the Feud open. A house may refuse an imposed kinsman. A kinsman who is the better soldier may seize a tümen
    III. The Shock from Outside −10 horde unity. The regent's three strongest independent neighbours outside the ulus, and the eastern khanate if there is one, hold The Ulus in Disarray on him for ten years (land is cheap for them). Members may leave the ulus. Whoever takes the regent's capital in war takes the khan. No kinsman can be imposed. A chief beaten by an invader may flee and a kinsman submit in his place
    IV. The Ṣāḥib-qirān At the turn the amirs are measured once more: if an amir not of the line now has a higher kingmaker score than the regent, the khan goes to him. Whoever holds the khan gains 15 prestige (The Lord of the Conjunction). The endings are now counted

    The office hardens¶

    Each time a stage turns with the legitimacy gap at 60 or more, the regent gains a step of The Beglerbeg's Office, to three steps (The Office Hardens). Each step gives +5% crown power and +0.03 monthly horde unity and prestige, which undoes one stage's worth of strain, and is meant to raise his heir's standing against the best rival by 10%. All of it goes with the khan: a state that loses him loses the office.

    The legitimacy gap¶

    A beg has no blood to rule by, only deeds. The gap is a measure from 0 to 100 of how well the regent's deeds cover for the blood he lacks. It starts at 50 and counts as filled at 60 or more.

    It drains each month
    Stage I / II / III / IV 0.15 / 0.30 / 0.45 / 0.60
    No khan in keeping × 2
    Any amir's effective weight is greater than the regent's × 1.5
    It is filled by
    A great battle won +4
    A siege won +2
    A war won (a raid counts) +10
    Distribute Largesse +10
    A war lost, or the capital taken by an outsider −15
    Choosing to rule without a khan Halved at once

    Only real engagements count: skirmishes do not fill it. The panel shows the gap, its monthly drain, and the running count of months filled or empty.

    The regent's actions¶

    These three are on the disaster's own panel. Recall the Darughas is offered to whoever leads the ulus, but it is reached from the regency's panel.

    Action Requires Effect
    Recall the Darughas A nobles estate; not already in force For ten years Darughas Recalled: −15% nobles' estate power, −10% nobles' target satisfaction. At once: −15% nobles' satisfaction, −10 stability, the clock −15. The seats themselves stand
    Marry into the Line A living khan in keeping; the ruler not already a son-in-law of the line The ruler takes a wife of the khan's house and stands as güregen. +10 prestige; every member gains +15 opinion of us; the clock −20. It is required of a Ṣāḥib-qirān, and it lets the house raise a khan of its own daughter's blood when a kept khan dies
    Distribute Largesse — The gap +10. Nobles +5% satisfaction. Every member gains +25 opinion of us (fading within about two years). For two years: +10 subject loyalty (Largesse Remembered), +3 weight in the ulus, and no house that took the silver can Claim the Khan

    What the houses can do¶

    These are on the Ulus Chaghatay panel, with the pledges and raids described on the ulus page.

    Action Who and when Effect
    Claim the Khan An amir at peace, from stage II, who has not taken the regent's largesse within the last two years The casus belli Take the Khan on the regent for ten years; the goal is his capital. If the claimant then loses a war to the regent, his own house may put him aside
    Press the Feud An amir at peace, from stage II, who has pledged or been pledged to The casus belli Feud of the Tümens for five years on a house that stands in a different bloc and with which we have no truce. Land is dear in the peace
    Back the Khan's Purge An amir, at any stage, while the kept khan is a grown man with military skill of 50 or more Three chances in ten that the khan kills the regent's house and rules (The Khan Triumphant); an even chance if his military skill is 70 or more. If he fails (The Khan's Hand Is Stayed) the regency moves to its next stage at once, the regent loses 10 horde unity, and three times in four the khan is put to death

    Deaths¶

    The kept khan dies (The Khan Is Dead). The regent chooses:

    Option Requires Result
    Find another of the blood. A man of the line to raise; 20 political influence Another prince is enthroned. The clock +10
    My daughter's son carries the blood of Chinggis. The house has married into the line; 10 political influence A child of the in-law line is enthroned and the ruler stands as güregen
    There will be no khan. — −20 horde unity. The gap is halved at once and drains twice as fast; the clock +25. If the gap is under 60 and no man of the line is left to raise, this ends in the Bulqaq

    The regent dies. His heir inherits the state but not the khan. Thirty to eighty days after the accession the amirs are polled, and the clock moves +15. The heir keeps the khan only if his kingmaker score is at least 80% of the best rival's (The Khan Stays, +5 horde unity). Renown died with his father, which is half of why heirs lose. Otherwise the best rival takes the khan and the leadership (The Khan Comes to Us, +10 prestige), the regency moves to stage II if it was still in stage I, and the dispossessed house loses 10 horde unity and holds Take the Khan on the new regent.

    The regent is murdered (The Beg Is Murdered). Rolled once a year:

    Rivals whose kingmaker score is at least half the regent's Chance
    One or two 2%
    Three or more 4%

    The chance is doubled if the nobles' satisfaction in his realm is under 40%. There is no roll in the twelve months after he wins a great battle.

    The regent's state is destroyed. The amirs are polled again and the strongest takes the khan, or finds another.

    An amir house's succession¶

    When the chief of an amir house dies, whoever leads the ulus is asked (An Amir's Succession):

    Option Result
    The son holds what the father held. The house gains +30 opinion ("Confirmed our succession")
    That tümen is the khan's to give. A kinsman of the same house replaces its ruler. It costs 15 political influence, or 30 for a regent, and the house takes −75 opinion. From stage II there is an even chance the house refuses: it then holds Take the Khan on the regent and he loses 10 prestige. From stage III nobody can be imposed

    The cadet takes the tümen¶

    In 1360 Hajji Beg, chief of the Barlas, fled before Tughluq Temür; a young kinsman, Temür, stayed, submitted and was confirmed over the tribe. A tümen was the house's, not the chief's. The same can happen to any amir house that has a grown man of the ruler's dynasty at court.

    Trigger
    From stage III: the house loses a war to an invader from outside the ulus or to the regent, or its capital falls to one The Chief Looks to the Road
    The house rose against the khan and lost, and its chief survived; or it claimed the khan and lost to the regent The same event, at any stage
    From stage II, at peace: a kinsman has at least 15 more military skill than the chief The Tümen Talks — 3 chances in 100 a year, 12 in 100 for six years after a coup has succeeded anywhere in the ulus

    If the chief rides out, the kinsman becomes ruler with 5 renown. Confirmed by an outsider, the house becomes that invader's tributary if the war between them can be closed; confirmed by the holder of the khan (or by the khan after a failed rising) no subject tie is made, and the new chief is sworn to him for five years. Each gains +40 opinion of the other. A house goes through this at most once in ten years, and a chief who stood his ground is not asked again for two.

    The old chief leaves alive. Within one to three years he returns for his tümen (7 chances in 10: The Old Chief at the Edge of the Pastures, where the kinsman yields or refuses) or dies on the road (3 in 10). The first tümen in a regency to change chiefs by a coup earns its new chief 5 more renown and the house 10 prestige.

    Taking the khan by force¶

    From stage III, when a country at war with the regent takes his capital:

    The captor Result
    The eastern khanate, or any realm whose ruler is of the line The Khan Triumphant by the sword
    A member of the ulus The khan and the leadership pass to him (The Khan Is Ours, +15 prestige, +3 renown)
    Anyone else The khan's household flees: the clock +10, the gap −15

    At any stage, a regent who loses a war to a realm whose ruler is of the line has met the Khan Triumphant by the sword.

    How it ends¶

    Ending Condition Result
    The Khan Triumphant (the purge) Back the Khan's Purge succeeds The kept khan becomes the ruler of the state that held him. The reform ends. The Khan Triumphant for twenty years: +20% crown power, −15% nobles' power, +0.10 monthly horde unity. +25 horde unity. The begs cannot raise another khan for fifteen years
    The Khan Triumphant (the sword) A realm of the line beats the regent in war, or takes his capital from stage III (One Khan Again) The victor's realm leads the ulus, re-entering it if it had left and bringing home the members who followed it east. The same modifier, unity and fifteen years
    Ṣāḥib-qirān Stage IV; a living khan in keeping; the ruler has a wife of the line; the gap at 60 or more for five years running The ulus becomes one man's realm. See The Ṣāḥib-qirān
    Bulqaq The gap empty for three years running; or the clock full in stage IV with the gap under 60; or the regent rules without a khan, no man of the line is left, and the gap is under 60 −25 prestige for the regent and the reform ends. The organisation is dissolved and every member stands alone, at −10 horde unity. The eastern khan, if there is one, holds Restore the Ulus on each of them

    The Breaking of the Chaghatay¶

    The Chaghatay were an army with households. After a Bulqaq what is left in the pastures is the tümens' families and herds, and the neighbours come for them.

    • Claims. Every country bordering a former member holds Break the Tümens on it for 25 years.
    • The prize, once in a game. The first independent country not of Chaghatay culture and not itself a former member that holds, with its subjects, half the pastures the thirteen Chaghatay realms held in 1337 is asked what becomes of them (The Tümens Are Ours to Dispose Of):
    Option Result
    Divide them among our own, hearth by hearth. In every such pasture we own, the Chaghatay tribesmen, soldiers and nobles become people of our own culture at once. The Tümens Distributed for 30 years: +20% manpower, +15% levy size. +50 prestige
    Let them keep their captains and their name, and ride for us. They keep their culture. The Tümens in Our Service, permanently: +10% levy size, +5% land morale, +1 cultures capacity. +25 prestige
    • The dissolution. Every year, in each old pasture held by a country not of Chaghatay culture, there is one chance in ten that its Chaghatay tribesmen, soldiers and nobles pass into the ruler's own people. Townsmen and peasants are untouched.

    The same claims and the same prize open without a Bulqaq if a Ṣāḥib-qirān's army is lost (see his page).

    Playing it¶

    As the regent. You are paid in political influence early and charged in horde unity and prestige later, and the gap drains four times faster in the last stage than in the first. Fight: battles, sieges and won wars are the only free fillers, and a raid counts as a war. Marry into the line early; it sets the clock back, and the last stage requires it. Largesse costs a year's income and buys ten points, two years of goodwill and two years in which no house that took it can claim the khan. Build crown power in your own realm: while it exceeds the nobles' and the three greatest amirs do not outweigh you, the clock runs backwards. Reach each stage's turn with the gap at 60 and the office hardens. Have an heir who is a soldier; he must reach four fifths of the best rival's score with none of your renown.

    As an amir under a regent. Renown is only earned while a regency lasts, so this is when a name is made. From stage II you can claim the khan, but the capital only delivers him from stage III. A regent with no army, or one some house already outweighs, is the one to move against. If you would rather have a khan than this regent and the kept khan is a soldier, back his purge.

    As the old khan. Defying keeps your claim and takes the eastern houses with you; beat the regent in any war and you lead the whole ulus again.

    As a neighbour. Stage III hands the regent's three strongest neighbours a ten-year claim with cheap land. A Bulqaq hands every neighbour one.
