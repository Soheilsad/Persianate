    Estate privileges¶

    Every grant is a line in the register: something the state gains, something it agrees not to see.

    This page is the complete list of the privileges the mod adds or changes, grouped by estate, with their conditions and effects as they stand in 1.4.

    What they represent¶

    A Persianate ruler governed less by statute than by grant: the iqṭāʿ assigned to a commander, the soyurghāl that made it hereditary and immune, the patent of tarkhan exemption, the charter confirming an endowment, the diploma investing a chief accountant or a chief of the merchants. Each was a bargain between the throne and a body of its servants, and each named an office or an institution with a long record: the Ṣāḥib-Dīwān, the Mustawfī al-Mamālik, the Amīr al-Umarāʾ, the Naqīb al-Ashrāf. The privilege panel is where those bargains are struck.

    How to read the tables¶

    • Sat is the estate's target satisfaction; power is that estate's power. Both are percentages: "Sat +5%, power +50%".
    • Max tax is the ceiling on what an estate can be assessed. Many privileges lower the granting estate's own ceiling and raise someone else's; this is the quiet half of almost every bargain.
    • Urban / rural are maximum control in towns and cities and in the countryside.
    • Turk / Tazik, centralization and the like are monthly movement on the societal values; see Turk vs Tazik.
    • PI gain is the monthly gain of political influence (see the end of this page).
    • "PS only" means the privilege is offered only to a country with the Persianate Sultanate government.

    Three patterns recur:

    • Rival pairs. Two privileges, one from each elite, that cannot be held together. You must lose the one to grant the other.
    • Crossing the line. Two privileges break the division of pen and sword set by the Persianate Bureaucracy reform: one lets the Dīwān command armies, the other seats amirs in the cabinet.
    • The Matter of Tork lock. Four privileges (marked ‡) cannot be granted while the Matter of Tork is open and unanswered unless the Amirs, the Dīwān and the Tribes are all at 50% satisfaction or better.
    Rival pair Question
    Mavājib Cash Pay (Dīwān) / Hereditary Tuyul (Amirs) How the cavalry is paid
    Dīwān al-Barīd (Dīwān) / Exemption from Audit (Amirs) Whether the court can see the provinces
    Muṣādara Right (Dīwān) / Soyurghal Grants (Amirs) Whether wealth can be recalled
    Qānūn Codification (Dīwān) / Tribal ʿUrf Jurisdiction (Amirs) Who judges
    Dīwān al-ʿArḍ (Dīwān) / Amir-Held Commands (Amirs) Who administers the ghulām corps

    The other estates' privileges¶

    The Sufyānī notables and Shaykhī dervishes of the Sarbadār state have privilege lists of their own; see The Commonwealth of Martyrs.

    Political influence¶

    1.4 introduced political influence (PI) as a currency gained each month from the ruler and a full cabinet. The mod's rule of thumb is that political influence pays for acts inside the existing order and legitimacy pays for changing it.

    Within the estates, that means:

    Source Effect on monthly PI gain
    Mustawfī al-Mamālik +10%
    Mongol-Persian Documentary Synthesis +10%
    Qānūn Codification +10%
    Dīwān of the Realms (building) +10%
    Amīr al-Umarāʾ −10%
    Umarāʾ at the Dīwān −10%
    Ṣāḥib-Dīwān Plenipotentiary −15% (and −0.15 monthly legitimacy: the vizier is seen to rule)

    A working chancery makes the court's orders move by register; a camp that has taken over the offices, or a minister who has taken over the throne's business, drains them.

    The estate action that spends it is Summon the Amirs' Tümens: 15 political influence, or 5 when the Dīwān outweighs the Amirs (see the Amirs estate). The other places the mod prices in political influence (demanding fealty and integrating governorates, posting a darugha, enthroning a khan) belong to Custody of the Ulus, the Ilkhanate governorate, Hordes and Ulus Chaghatay.

    Practical notes¶

    • Read the max-tax entries before the headline effects. Five chancery privileges later, the Dīwān may be nearly untaxable and the burghers and peasants carrying the difference; the amir list does the same for the Amirs, ending in Universal Tarkhan, which removes them from the tax base altogether.
    • Power is cumulative and large. A single +100% privilege on top of the demographic bases can tip an estate into its "powerful" effects, good and bad.
    • The two line-crossing privileges are the strongest single moves on the Turk vs Tazik value available from the panel, and each costs the other elite 10% satisfaction and 15% power.
    • The three waqf charters buy satisfaction and legitimacy cheaply, and each one surrenders the court's power to dissolve endowments.
