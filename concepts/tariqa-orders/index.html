    Ṭarīqa Orders and the Shia Rework¶

    The lodges are waiting to hear whose name to recite.

    New in 1.4 — not yet verified in play

    The orders load and the Two Pirs has been exercised with a debug kit, but the endgames (the khurūj and its coronation, Rise Against the Patron, Capture the Court) and the rebel-banner events have not been played through to the end in a 1.4 campaign. One in-game tooltip on the Manifestation event still describes an older version of the rising (provinces coming over, half a war chest); this page describes what the event actually does.

    What it represents¶

    By 1337 the Sufi orders were among the most durable institutions between the Nile and the Jaxartes. A ṭarīqa was a chain of teaching from master to disciple, a shrine, an endowment, and a web of lodges whose deputies (khalīfas) answered to the shaykh at the shrine rather than to any governor. Princes endowed them because a shaykh's blessing was worth having when thrones had no settled owner. This world was not divided into "Sunni" and "Shia" as later centuries would understand the words. Reverence for ʿAlī and the Prophet's house ran through Sunni orders as a matter of course; the Kubrawiyya, the Ṣafaviyya at Ardabīl and others stood in a confessional middle ground that modern historians call ʿAlid loyalism. Explicit Shiism, messianic or juristic, grew out of that ground in particular places over generations: the Ṣafavī order was a respectable Sunni house under Ṣadr al-Dīn in 1337 and a militant Shia movement under Junayd and Ḥaydar a century later. The mod therefore starts every order Sunni and makes the change of confession a long, costly and optional passage, not a starting trait.

    The Shia rework¶

    The base game gives Shia Islam a list of schools and nothing else. The mod separates three things the base game ran together.

    Layer What it answers Where it lives
    Imamate and doctrine Whose Imamate, and what follows from it Religious aspects (four slots)
    Establishment Which network runs your religious institutions The religious school slot
    Law Which madhhab the courts apply The sharia law policy, as in the base game

    Aspects¶

    Shia countries have four aspect slots. Adopting or changing an aspect costs 30 stability. A Shia realm may hold no Imamate aspect at all, and most do at the start: that is the confessional ambiguity of the post-Mongol decades, and committing is meant to be a decision.

    Aspect Requires Effects
    The Twelve Imams No other Imamate +1 tolerance of the true faith, a small clergy satisfaction bonus
    The Ismaili Imamate No other Imamate +5 maximum literacy, drift towards mysticism
    The Zaydi Imamate No other Imamate +5% land morale, drift towards jurisprudence
    Quietist Awaiting The Twelve Imams +10% stability cost efficiency, +5% improve relations
    The General Vicariate The Twelve Imams +10% clergy power, a small clergy satisfaction bonus, drift towards jurisprudence
    The Manifestation The Twelve Imams +10% land morale, +20% conversion speed, −10% stability cost efficiency, drift towards spiritualist
    Qiyām bi'l-Sayf The Zaydi Imamate; not Taqiyya +10% manpower, +5% land morale, drift towards mysticism
    Usuli Rationalism The Twelve Imams +5% research speed, +10% clergy power, drift towards jurisprudence
    Akhbari Traditionism The Twelve Imams +10% stability cost efficiency, +1 tolerance of the true faith
    Taqiyya Not Tabarra, not Qiyām bi'l-Sayf +10% improve relations, +1 tolerance of heretics
    Tabarra Not Taqiyya +25% conversion speed, +1 tolerance of the true faith, −15% improve relations

    Quietist Awaiting, The General Vicariate and The Manifestation exclude one another, as do Usuli Rationalism and Akhbari Traditionism. Countries that share an aspect like each other a little more; rival positions cost opinion, and two realms that both claim The Manifestation dislike each other most of all (−20).

    A landed state may claim The Manifestation or Qiyām bi'l-Sayf freely. An order may not until it has circulated a sayyid genealogy (see the Two Pirs below).

    Establishments¶

    The generic labels Ja'farī, Ismā'īlī and Imāmīya are no longer offered to Shia countries as schools; the branch is now the Imamate aspect.

    Establishment Open to Leaning required Effects
    The Hilla Hawza Shia None (always available) +10 maximum literacy, +5% research speed, drift towards jurisprudence
    The Shaykhiyya Shia Not on the jurisprudence side +5% land morale, +5% manpower, drift towards mysticism
    The Kubrawiyya Sunni or Shia Mysticism beyond 50 +5 maximum literacy, +1 tolerance of heretics, drift towards mysticism
    The Nurbakhshiyya Shia Mysticism beyond 50 +10% conversion speed, +5% land morale, drift towards mysticism
    The Nimatullahiyya Sunni or Shia Mysticism beyond 50 +5% trade income, +5% improve relations, drift towards mysticism

    The Zaydiyya, The Nizari Daʿwa and The Tayyibi Daʿwa are kept from the base game and re-described as networks. The Hilla Hawza counts as a school of jurisprudence wherever the game asks for one.

    Two Sunni schools were also rebalanced: Ḥanafī now gives +5% cabinet efficiency and +5% tax efficiency, Shāfi'ī +10% export efficiency and +10% clergy power, and both drift towards jurisprudence.

    Other religion-level changes¶

    • Sunni and Shia realms now accumulate religious influence (+0.05 a month, to a cap of 400). The Zaydi imamate page covers where it is spent.
    • The blanket bans on importing wine, beer and liquor have been removed from the Sunni, Shia and Ibadi religions. Prohibition was a matter of market inspection and policy, not an automatic consequence of the faith.

    Who starts with what¶

    Country Imamate aspect Establishment
    Sarbadars (SRB) The Twelve Imams, The General Vicariate The Shaykhiyya
    Baduspanids (BDS) The Twelve Imams unchanged
    Tabaristan (MZN), Tulam (TLM), Khorshidi (LCK), Hillah (HLL), Ispahbad (IHB) The Zaydi Imamate unchanged
    Gilgit (GLI), Hunza (HNZ), Sindh (SND) The Ismaili Imamate The Nizari Daʿwa
    Chobanids (CHB) none The Hilla Hawza

    Each entry applies only if the country is Shia when the game starts. The Chobanids are the deliberate case of a Twelver-leaning court that has not committed.

    The orders in 1337¶

    A Tariqa Order is a country without land. Its capital, the shrine-seat, is a city that belongs to someone else, and its presence on the map is the lodges it keeps in other rulers' locations. Seven exist at the start, all Sunni, all with a Persianate Sultanate's court and an elective succession among the shaykh's house.

    Order Tag Shrine-seat Base Establishment Starting lodges
    Ardabil (the Ṣafaviyya) ABL Ardabil none chosen yet Ṣafavī Tabriz, Ahar
    Kubrawiyya KBW Semnan Popular The Kubrawiyya Damghan, Nishapur
    Kazaruniyya KZR Kazerun Urban Shāfi'ī Shiraz, Siraf
    Qadiriyya QAD Baghdad Urban Ḥanbalī Mosul, Samarra
    Khwajagan KWJ Bukhara Urban Ḥanafī Samarkand, Karmana
    Yasaviyya YSV Turkestan Tribal Ḥanafī Otrar, Sawran
    Rifaiyya RIF Wasit Popular Shāfi'ī Basra, Hillah

    Each also starts with a Grand Khānaqāh at its shrine-seat. Six lean strongly towards mysticism; the Khwajagan, the sober masters of Bukhara, lean towards jurisprudence. Ardabil has not yet chosen a base, so that choice is the first decision of a Ṣafavī game.

    At the opening of the Succession of Erānzamīn an order has its own answer to the empty throne: it claims nothing and gains +5 legitimacy. Orders are exempt from the unsettled-legitimacy penalty that landed sultanates suffer.

    The order's reforms¶

    Tariqa Order itself allows lodges to be planted and gives +10% improve relations, +1 diplomatic reputation, +50% levy size and a drift towards mysticism. On top of it an order takes exactly one base, which decides whom it recruits and how it can end.

    Base Unlocks Bonuses Endgame
    The Tribal Base Tribal Devotion Circle +10% land morale, +50% tribal levies Proclaim the Khuruj
    The Popular Base Rural Zāwiya +10% manpower Rise Against the Patron
    The Urban Base Endowed Waqf +1 diplomatic reputation, +5 maximum literacy Capture the Court

    Lodges and chapters¶

    All of an order's buildings stand in other rulers' locations and need good relations with the host to be built. Each sends a monthly donation (futūḥ) back to the shrine-seat; the "income" column gives the relative size.

    Building Where For the host Converts to Income
    Tariqa Lodge (3 levels) Any settlement Lower maximum control, −0.1 unrest, a little prosperity Sunni, slowly 1
    Grand Khānaqāh (1 level) A capital, town or larger, with Muslim pops +0.1 monthly legitimacy for the country whose capital it is; prosperity none 3
    Tribal Devotion Circle (2 levels) Rural settlements and towns none Sunni, slowly 0.5
    Rural Zāwiya (2 levels) Rural settlements and towns −0.1 unrest, a little prosperity Sunni, slowly 0.5
    Endowed Waqf (3 levels) Towns and cities Prosperity Sunni, slowly 2

    Devotion Circles, Zāwiyas and Waqfs are chapters: the count of chapters of your own base is what the Two Pirs measures. After the recitation changes, the buildings change with it:

    Building Replaces For the host Converts to
    Ghulāt Khānaqāh Tariqa Lodge Lower maximum control, +0.1 unrest Shia, at twice the lodge's rate
    Qizilbash Lodge Tribal Devotion Circle +0.2 unrest Shia
    Dervish Takiya Rural Zāwiya +0.1 unrest Shia
    Futuwwa Brotherhood Endowed Waqf +0.1 unrest Shia

    The three militant buildings employ ten times the people of the chapters they replace. Once an order has declared, it can no longer build ordinary Tariqa Lodges, only Ghulāt ones.

    A host who destroys a Rural Zāwiya or a Dervish Takiya is remembered for ten years; that memory is what licenses Rise Against the Patron.

    Patronize An Order¶

    Any Muslim ruler who is not an order, and who has an order's Tariqa Lodge somewhere in the realm, can endow it. The action sits in the panel of the Sarbadar Phenomenon and so is only offered while that situation is active (see the Commonwealth of Martyrs).
    Price Six months of the patron's income
    Cooldown 5 years
    If the order accepts The order receives the endowment. The patron gains +10 legitimacy and +5 stability, and a Grand Khānaqāh is founded in the patron's capital (+0.1 monthly legitimacy while it stands).
    If the order refuses The gold is returned, the patron loses 15 legitimacy, and the order gains 5.

    An AI order accepts almost always. Refusal becomes likely only when the order's opinion of the patron is below −50, or when a radical order is courted by a non-Shia throne. For an order, patronage is the main source of large sums; for a ruler, it is some of the cheapest legitimacy available in the years after 1335.

    The Two Pirs¶

    A disaster for orders: the succession at the shrine is contested between the quietist establishment and a militant claimant.

    It can begin for a Sunni order that has at least five chapters of its base, has not been through it before, and has not upheld the quietist within the last 20 years. The chance is 2.5% a month. While the disaster is active the order suffers −1.0 monthly legitimacy and −1 diplomatic reputation.

    It opens with a three-way choice.

    Choice Result
    Let the recitation change. The order, its shaykh, heir and house become Shia with no Imamate claimed yet. Permanent Militant Zeal (+10% land morale, +15% conversion speed, −25% improve relations). Every Tariqa Lodge in the shrine-seat's area becomes a Ghulāt Khānaqāh; the rest stay veiled. The passage begins and the legitimacy drain continues until it is completed.
    The claimant can burn somewhere else. The disaster ends. +10 legitimacy; The Exile's Wound for 20 years (−10% manpower, −0.1 monthly legitimacy). The question cannot return for 20 years.
    Petition for land. The disaster ends for good. The order takes ownership of its shrine-seat, becomes an ordinary landed country, loses the Tariqa Order reform and gains +20 legitimacy.

    The passage¶

    The disaster panel shows three bars and the actions that fill them.

    Bar Midpoint Completion
    The Chapter Census (chapters of your base, anywhere) 10 20
    The Shrine-Seat Province Recites (Shia share of the province) 50%
    The Wider Area Recites (Shia share of the surrounding area) 15%
    Action Requires Price Cooldown Effect
    Send Forth the Khalifas A location you do not own, with Muslim pops and no chapter 3 months' income 12 months A new chapter of your base, at once
    Plant the Seed A location with non-Shia Muslims 1.5 months' income 6 months 2% of every non-Shia Muslim pop there becomes Shia
    Proclaim the Mystery Legitimacy 20 or more; a veiled Tariqa Lodge 15 legitimacy 24 months That lodge becomes a Ghulāt Khānaqāh
    Spread the Sayyid Genealogy Legitimacy 60 or more; five lodges (veiled or unveiled) 6 months' income once Permanent House of the Prophet (+0.1 monthly legitimacy); the order may now claim The Manifestation or Qiyām bi'l-Sayf
    Claim the Age 10 chapters; half the shrine-seat province Shia free once The Proofs Multiply until the passage ends (+0.5 monthly legitimacy, +25% conversion speed); Shia risings in the region now offer the order their banners

    Completion¶

    The passage completes when all of these hold:

    • the Age has been claimed;
    • 20 chapters stand;
    • 15% of the shrine-seat's area is Shia;
    • legitimacy is 50 or more;
    • the order holds The Twelve Imams with The Manifestation, or The Zaydi Imamate with Qiyām bi'l-Sayf (two aspects, 30 stability each).

    The Manifestation then fires. Every chapter becomes its militant twin, the last veiled lodges unveil, The Proofs Multiply ends, and the order gains permanent The Manifest Order (+25% levy size, +5% land morale). The order chooses between consolidating (+10 legitimacy, with the endgames open on its own schedule) and rising at once.

    The three endgames¶

    The khurūj (tribal orders)¶

    Proclaim the Khuruj needs a completed passage and at least three Qizilbash Lodges. When proclaimed:

    • the shrine-seat location becomes the order's own;
    • the order becomes an army-country under a Persianate Sultanate with The Khurūj reform (+20% land morale, +10% discipline, +50% supply limit);
    • two Turkoman Light Cavalry regiments muster at the shrine-seat for every five lodges (rounded up), with 500 manpower in reserve for each pair;
    • the treasury receives 24 months of income;
    • war is declared, with the Succession of Erānzamīn casus belli, on every country whose capital lies in the shrine-seat's area.

    Rising directly from the Manifestation event does the same for an order of any base, counting all its militant chapters: tribal orders muster Turkoman Light Cavalry, the others muster Dervish Corps. On that road the shrine-seat is not handed over; the order takes the field landless.

    The Khurūj is not an answer to the khuṭba question, so the risen order carries the same unsettled-legitimacy penalty as any undecided sultanate (−0.6 monthly legitimacy and lower estate satisfaction). That drain is the clock.

    The coronation¶

    A risen order that takes Tabriz, Isfahan, Baghdad, Nishapur, Shiraz or Herat receives The Khuṭba of the Twelve:

    • the conquered city becomes the capital and the order becomes a settled country of kingdom rank;
    • The Khurūj is replaced by Persianate Bureaucracy and The Crowned Murshid (+0.1 monthly legitimacy, +10% land morale, +5% discipline);
    • +100 legitimacy.

    The Crowned Murshid ends the legitimacy drain, but it stands only while the realm leans towards mysticism beyond 50. If the jurists pull the realm back, the reform is lost and the crown keeps only the throne.

    Rise Against the Patron (popular orders)¶

    Available when a host has destroyed one of the order's Zāwiyas or Takiyas within the last ten years, or when the order is at war. Choose a realm that hosts at least two of them. Every location in that realm with a Zāwiya or Takiya passes to the order, which becomes a landed Commonwealth of Martyrs, musters two Dervish Corps regiments per five lodges, and receives 12 months of income. As written, this does not require the Two Pirs to have run.

    Capture the Court (urban orders)¶

    Needs a completed passage. Choose a Muslim realm that hosts at least two of the order's Endowed Waqfs or Futuwwa Brotherhoods.
    Price Twelve months of the order's income
    Cooldown 20 years
    Effect The target's ruler, heir and dynasty become Shia; the realm takes the order's Imamate (Zaydi if the order is Zaydi, otherwise The Twelve Imams) and the Ṣafavī establishment. The order gains +20 legitimacy and a Grand Khānaqāh in the captured capital.

    The order remains an order and can repeat the act elsewhere after the cooldown.

    Shia risings and their banners¶

    When Shia religious rebels open a civil war, the mod asks who leads them.

    If an order is in reach (it has claimed the Age or completed the passage, has not settled, and keeps a building in the region of the rebels' capital), it receives The Banners Seek a Pir. Accepting is a full khurūj on the spot: the order annexes the rebels and their land, musters its lodges, receives 24 months of income, and goes to war with the country the rebels rose against and with the neighbours of the shrine-seat. Declining costs nothing in numbers.

    If no order is in reach, the rebels decide for themselves in In Whose Name Is the Coin Minted:

    Answer Result
    No name but the Imām's. A new Commonwealth of Martyrs with a full 25 years of Revolutionary Zeal, The Twelve Imams and The General Vicariate, the Shaykhiyya establishment, and membership of the Sarbadār Movement. Favoured where Sarbadar Preachers have prepared the ground and where the rising is in a city.
    The waiting is over. A Persianate Sultanate with The Twelve Imams and The Manifestation and the Nurbakhshiyya establishment. Favoured in the countryside.

    Practical advice¶

    Playing an order

    • Income is lodges. Build Tariqa Lodges widely before anything else, and take every patron's endowment.
    • The Two Pirs needs five chapters to begin at all. An order that never builds chapters stays a quiet, wealthy Sunni house indefinitely, which is a legitimate way to play the Khwajagan or the Qadiriyya.
    • If you mean to walk the passage, enter it with legitimacy high and a full treasury. Spread the Sayyid Genealogy needs 60 legitimacy and the drain starts at once, so commission it in the first months.
    • Unveil lodges sparingly. Each costs 15 legitimacy; let Plant the Seed do the work in the home province and save unveiling for the places where the 50% line is close.
    • Claim the Age as soon as it is available. It halves the drain.
    • Completion needs 60 stability for the two aspects, on top of legitimacy 50. Arrive with both.
    • Before proclaiming the khurūj, look at who has a capital in your shrine-seat's area: you will be at war with all of them at once, and you need one of six great cities to stop the bleeding.

    Living next to an order

    • A Tariqa Lodge is good for the location and one Patronize away from +10 legitimacy. Host them.
    • Watch for lodges turning into Ghulāt Khānaqāhs. From then on the building raises unrest and converts your subjects, and removing a village order's Zāwiya gives it the pretext to rise against you.
