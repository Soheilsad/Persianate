    The Maẓālim court¶

    "Here, the Shadow of God sits in judgment over his servants."

    A Persianate Sultanate does not have a parliament. In the parliament slot it has the Majlis-e Mazalim: the court of grievances, where the ruler hears complaints against his own officials, commanders, judges and merchants.

    What it represents¶

    The maẓālim (literally "injustices") was the sovereign's own jurisdiction, sitting above and beside the qāḍī's court to hear what ordinary procedure could not reach: the abuses of the powerful. It is an Islamic institution of long standing, described by al-Māwardī as one of the offices of government, and the Siyāsatnāma makes holding it in person a duty no king may neglect. It is not a consultative assembly. Nobody votes the ruler his taxes there; he earns his standing by being the one authority that can call every other to account, and the "Circle of Justice" that ties the army to the treasury, the treasury to the cultivator and the cultivator to justice closes in this room.

    The court as a parliament¶

    The court uses the base game's parliament machinery, with its own rules.
    Who holds it Countries with the Persianate Sultanate government. Both starting forms begin with it.
    Who is seated Clergy, Dīwān, Amirs, peasants and Tribes. The court's rules do not seat the burghers (see the note under their cases).
    Who is barred The base Nobility cannot participate.
    Weight of the elites Agendas of the Amirs and the clergy carry +25% impact; those of the Dīwān +10%.

    The base game's Western parliament events never fire for the Majlis-e Mazalim.

    How a session runs¶

    A session is a debate on one matter, a decree of the crown or a petition of an estate. As far as the files show, the matter is drawn from those currently available, by weight; the crown's decrees and the chancery's petitions carry weights of 25–40, the petitions of the other estates 10, so most sessions turn on the crown's or the Dīwān's business.

    Three things then matter.

    While the debate is open, the matter's listed modifier applies to the whole country (for example +0.5 monthly legitimacy during a Royal Progress). It ends when the debate ends, whatever the result.

    The cases. In a base parliament each estate brings an agenda, a demand you may meet to win its support. Here each estate comes before the court with a case, an accusation of which it is itself the subject: embezzlement in the chancery, forced billeting by the amirs, bribery on the qāḍī's bench. Taking up a case settles that estate's agenda and counts its weight toward the debate, as in any parliament, but what you are doing is passing judgment:

    • the accused estate loses satisfaction (10–25%);
    • every other estate gains satisfaction equal to one fifth of its own share of estate power, sometimes with a little more for the injured party;
    • in many cases a share of the accused estate's treasury is confiscated to yours;
    • a lasting benefit (or a short disruption) follows, as listed in the tables below.

    Cases can be bribed through in the usual way.

    The outcome. If the matter passes, its listed effects apply. If it fails, you take the base game's standard penalty for a failed debate: −7 stability and −10 legitimacy.

    Two further things happen only in session: the Muṣādara action, and the support a great act of justice can lend (see The Silent Realm below).

    The cases¶

    In game each name is prefixed "Case:". "Seized" is the share of the accused estate's treasury paid into yours. For the Dīwān that share is reduced by the waqf shelter (see the Dīwān estate). Cases marked ● are the grave ones, which weigh most in the debate.

    Recurring results: Reformed Tax Code is +3% tax efficiency; Fair Assessment is +0.1 pop rebel threshold; Administrative Purge is −10% tax efficiency and −10% integration speed; Justice Capital is +5% (Small) or +10% (Moderate) base support in the court.

    Muṣādara in the court¶

    With the Muṣādara Right, a dīwān member in the cabinet and the court in session, you may pronounce Muṣādara: half the Dīwān's treasury (less its waqf shelter) for half a request's worth of debate support, 40% of the estate's satisfaction and a dīwān minister's life, once every five years. The full account, with the endowments it provokes, is on the Dīwān estate page.

    The Silent Realm¶

    One story is told in this court above all others, and the mod tells it: Bahrām Gūr, the minister Rāst-Ravishn and the shepherd who hanged his dog, from the Siyāsatnāma.

    It can begin when you hold the Majlis-e Mazalim, your head of cabinet is someone other than the ruler, the Dīwān holds more than 40% of estate power, and the realm is failing: manpower under a quarter, or income negative, or a treasury below three months of income. It can happen once in twenty years.

    Using the court¶

    • Call it when you want the debate modifier as much as the result. A Royal Progress or a Nowrūz is worth +0.5 legitimacy a month for as long as it is before the court.
    • Cases are how you move satisfaction between estates. One judgment against a single estate lifts all the others in proportion to their power, so prosecute the estate you can afford to anger and the strong ones are the most pleased.
    • The treasury cases are real money: a grave judgment against a rich chancery or camp brings 30–40% of its hoard. Against the Dīwān, check the waqf shelter first.
    • A failed debate costs 7 stability and 10 legitimacy. Do not open a matter you cannot carry.
    • Keep the waqf charters and Reclaim the Illegal Waqf in mind as a pair: granting any charter gives up the decree.
