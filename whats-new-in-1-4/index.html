    What changed in 1.4¶

    For players coming back from the summer builds. The mod now targets Europa Universalis V 1.4.7, and it has grown well beyond the Iranian plateau: the last version of this wiki (June) described a government type, two estates and the Erānzamīn succession. This page lists what is new or different since then, with a link to the page that explains each item.

    Much of this is recent and has not yet been played through. Known issues and systems in testing says which parts.

    What the 1.4 engine changed for the mod¶

    • Estate power now comes from pops. Literate urban Iranian and Arab noble pops form the Dīwān; other Muslim noble pops form the Amirs; the Amirs' weight also scales with the share of tribesmen in the realm. Building up cities and literacy strengthens the pen; a large tribal population strengthens the sword. See the Dīwān estate and the Amirs estate.
    • Political influence is a currency the mod now uses. The Chinggisid Regency costs −20% political influence gain, sworn governorates pay their regent in it, the Dīwān of the Realms adds +10% to its gain, and several privileges and actions are priced in it. See the closing section of estate privileges.
    • Cavalry is split into light and heavy. Amirs ride heavy; the tribes ride light until the Qizilbash. Amir Cavalry levies 30% of amir nobles and the tribes 10% of tribesmen, while peasant and Dīwān levies are cut by 90%. See the military system.

    The Persianate state¶

    • Five chancery reforms instead of one: Persianate Bureaucracy, Mamluk Government, The Delhi Dispensation, Rasulid Chancery and Timurid Empire. Egypt and Delhi now start as Persianate Sultanates. See government reforms and periphery sultanates.
    • New reforms: Ilkhanate Governorate, Amīr Sultanate, The Gurkani Regime, The Īlāt Compact, Sword of The Sharia. A sultanate with no answer to the legitimacy question carries Unsettled Legitimacy (−0.6 legitimacy a month).
    • The Amirs keep private regiments through the Right to Retinue, and the crown can Summon the Amirs' Tümens. See the Amirs estate.
    • The privilege catalogue has more than doubled, and the Maẓālim court now has 18 crown decrees, 17 estate petitions and 52 cases. See estate privileges and the Maẓālim court.
    • Buildings: the iqṭāʿ now decays into Unaudited Iqṭāʿ and Tuyul unless audited; six estate-built waqf endowments; Qanāt Networks, forty of them damaged at the start in Khurāsān and Balkh. There are three Dīwāns of the Realms in 1337 (Tabrīz, Delhi, Cairo), not one. See buildings, goods and materials.
    • Goods: tar is gathered at Baku, horses are no longer food and are cheaper to move, glass is no longer needed to build a town, trade range is +300 worldwide, and Herāt has a market.
    • Persia as a formable now requires the legitimacy wager and the Matter of Tork settled; Iran o Toran is a new formable.

    The succession of Erānzamīn¶

    • The Erānzamīn organisation has its own panel: the Five Seats, the Khans in Custody, a Notables tab, and four roles for its 73 members. See the Erānzamīn organisation.
    • Contenders are Muslim realms seated in the region. Christian realms are members but not contenders; the ṭarīqa orders and the amirs of Ulus Chaghatay stand outside the contest.
    • Conquest is immediate. A contender who takes a place by siege or occupation annexes it on the spot, and a province capital carries its province. Victory now requires Shiraz as well as the other four seats. See the succession of Erānzamīn.
    • The victory is a single outcome followed by a branch on the Türk–Tāzīk axis: Integrate into the Dīwān or Invite Turkic Settlers.
    • Custody of the Ulus: sworn governorates pay the regent +0.05 legitimacy and +0.10 political influence each, and defending them is a choice. See the Borrowed Mandate and the Ilkhanate Governorate.
    • The Matter of Tork was rebuilt. Its paths now stack and the disaster closes with a proclamation; the march became the Great Raid; it fires at 750,000 Turco-Mongol tribesmen and more than 240 locations. The Farr and Ghāzī audits were renumbered throughout. See the Matter of Tork, the Farr Under Examination and the Burden of the Sword.

    The Shia world¶

    • The Commonwealth of Martyrs is the Sarbadars' own government, with the Sufyānī and Shaykhī estates, and the Sarbadar Phenomenon runs from the first day. See the Commonwealth of Martyrs.
    • Shia Islam was rebuilt: four aspect slots and eleven aspects, and new establishments in the school slot. Seven landless ṭarīqa orders exist in 1337, among them Ardabil, and an order can rise, capture a court and be crowned. See ṭarīqa orders and the Shia rework.
    • Yemen is divided between the Rasulids at Taʿizz and a new Zaydi Imamate at Ṣaʿda, an organisation whose members swear bayʿa, pay zakāt and answer the Imam's wars. See the Zaydi Imamate and Yemen.

    The steppe¶

    • Borjigin hordes get the yam (+33% proximity efficiency and eight more governors), Darugha's Seats, and ulus grants to princes. The flat loss of horde unity at a khan's death is replaced by a poll with four outcomes, and Jochi and Chagatai choose their khans by the scored Törü. See hordes.
    • Ulus Chaghatay is an organisation: the Chagatai khan and fifteen Amirs of the Ulus, who owe him a 5% qubchur. Four new countries are carved out of the khanate. Base's scheduled Timur is switched off. See Ulus Chaghatay, and the next section for what has been built on it since.

    The steppe, November 2026¶

    Everything in this section was built after the wiki's last revision and is largely untested in play; see Known issues. The numbers are collected on Ulus and regency numbers.

    • Weight decides the ulus. A realm's weight is now 40% military strength, 30% tax base, 20% its ruler's abilities and 10% prestige. The amirs can raise a khan when the three greatest of them outweigh the khan four times over, or when the begs hold half the power in his realm. The panel shows what each house's weight and kingmaker score are made of. See Ulus Chaghatay.
    • Pledges. A house can pledge its whole tümen to another, or be asked to; the khan can ask the lesser lords to stand with the seat. A pledged house drops out of the reckoning for the khan and rides in its patron's wars. Allies and patrons outside the ulus count as well, to a limit, and an outsider can Back an Amir of the Ulus.
    • Rise Against the Khan. An amir among the three likeliest kingmakers need not wait for a funeral once a bar is met. If he wins he takes the khan; if he loses he pays in silver and land, and perhaps with his head.
    • Lead a Raid. Amirs and regents raid their neighbours for plunder and captives. Raiding unbelievers is a ghazā; raiding one's own faith without a cause costs prestige.
    • Client kings. Badakhshan and Karategin are no longer vassals of Chagatai. They sit in the ulus as Persianate courts with their own status and reform. The ground of a tümen that has lost its whole army is disposed of by whoever broke it: taken, raised again as a tümen, or given to a client king.
    • The Amirs' Regency is complete: four stages, a legitimacy gap that drains each month and is filled by victories and largesse, the regent's three actions on the disaster's own panel, the houses' Claim the Khan and Back the Khan's Purge, the cadet who takes a tümen when its chief flees, and three endings: the Khan Triumphant, the Ṣāḥib-qirān and the Bulqaq, with the Breaking of the Chaghatay after it. See The Amirs' Regency.
    • The Ṣāḥib-qirān is the mod's Timur: whoever ends the regency with a khan in keeping, a wife of the line and five years of standing. Loyal houses beside his own seat are annexed, the rest of the loyal become Sworn Amirs, and his realm rides out under his house's name. What holds it together is his fortune, which drains at peace and is filled by victory and by dividing the plunder. The Timurids can no longer be formed by decision. See The Ṣāḥib-qirān.
    • Chaghatay culture. The khan's seat, the western tümens and the carved domains are Chaghatay, not Mongolian: Turkic-speaking, Muslim in the west, still of the steppe. Temür's house is the Barlas dynasty, no longer filed under the Borjigin. See Demography and cultures.
    • Steppe privileges. Hordes that begin from the base horde setup start with Hereditary Tümens and Bound to the Tümen in place of two manorial privileges. A Kurultai may stand wherever the ruling people is of the steppe, and the tribes sit in a horde's parliament. See Hordes.
    • The Beglerbeg's Regency. When the Golden Horde's Törü elects a beg over the princes, he no longer becomes a khan of an invented dynasty. He rules through a kept khan under a disaster of his own, in which princes with shares declare themselves khan, and which ends with a khan of the line, the office made hereditary, or the khanate partitioned. See The Beglerbeg's Regency.

    The periphery¶

    • Rise of the Ottomans is now Shattered Rum. The beyliks become Persianate Sultanates and choose between the ghazā, the Seljuk claim, or remaining a march; the Seljuk Inheritance leads to a Sultanate of Rum. See Shattered Rum.
    • The Kurdish emirates have their own government, the Iranic Tribal Polity, with Zagros infantry and +50% levies in defence. See Kurdish emirates.

    The map and its people¶

    • Population in Iran, Iraq, the Caucasus and Afghanistan is calibrated to Mustawfī's register of 1340: the Persia region rises from about 4.0 to 13.3 million, the Caucasus from 1.9 to 4.7 million, the Khorasan region from 3.8 to 5.7 million, and Tabrīz is a city of a quarter of a million. Eighty-five more places between the Euphrates and the Pamirs are ranked as towns or cities.
    • There are no Hazara in 1337, Sistan and the lower Helmand are Khorasani, the Neguderi are Mongol, and Türkmen pastoralists are seeded across the Jazira. The Mongol elites who had become Persian in everything but lineage have a culture of their own, Persianized Mongol.
    • Accepted and tolerated cultures are set for 83 realms on the first day, by population weight. The Jews of Iran and Yemen are no longer typed as slaves.
    • No war is under way inside Erānzamīn at the start, and the base game's swarms of Jalayirid and Gurgan vassals are gone. The Dīwān and Amirs estates exist from day one.

    See demography and cultures, and countries and tags for the changed map.
